## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

- Understand why on-call ownership is a first-class engineering skill at FAANG-tier companies
- Design and configure alerting that pages on user impact, not system internals
- Build SLO-based alerting with multi-burn-rate alerts using Prometheus and Go
- Write production-quality runbooks for common Go service failure modes
- Lead and contribute to incident response with clear communication and structured decision-making
- Perform blameless postmortems that produce action items which actually ship
- Instrument Go services for maximum debuggability during outages
- Identify, measure, and eliminate toil from your on-call rotation
- Answer staff-engineer-level interview questions about reliability engineering

### Junior → FAANG: Mapping Learning Objectives to Interview Topics

Each learning objective above maps to at least one interview question category. "Design alerting that pages on user impact" maps to the question "walk me through how you would set up monitoring for a new service." "Instrument Go services for maximum debuggability" maps to "what tools and techniques would you use to diagnose a Go service in production?" Know which questions correspond to which objectives and prepare a concrete example for each.

### Senior @ FAANG: Learning Objectives as Staff-Level Competency Signals

The last objective ("answer staff-engineer-level interview questions") is the goal for L6 interview preparation. Staff-level reliability questions probe organizational scope: "How would you improve on-call health across 20 teams?" "How do you decide which reliability investments to prioritize?" "How do you build a culture of blameless postmortems in an organization that has blamed individuals in the past?" These require organizational thinking, not just technical knowledge.
