### Junior → FAANG: Using the Table of Contents as a Study Guide

The 18 sections in this project cover the full spectrum of backend engineering for a social platform. For interview preparation, prioritize sections 5-9 (social graph, posts, news feed, notifications): these map directly to the most common social network system design questions.

Sections 13-16 (performance, security, monitoring, testing) are the sections that distinguish candidates who have operated systems from those who have only built them. A candidate who can discuss privacy controls in a social graph context (section 14) or metrics that matter for a news feed system (section 15) demonstrates production awareness that most entry-level candidates lack.

### Senior @ FAANG: Architecture Coverage as a Staff-Level Evaluation Criterion

At senior level, the completeness of this project's scope (18 sections, including deployment, scaling, testing, and observability) is the right model for any significant new system. A design document that covers only the happy path (no testing strategy, no observability plan, no scaling discussion) is incomplete.

The table of contents structure also models good technical documentation: each section is independently navigable, has a clear responsibility, and builds on previous sections without assuming you have read all previous sections. This organization makes the document useful as both a learning guide and an operational reference.
