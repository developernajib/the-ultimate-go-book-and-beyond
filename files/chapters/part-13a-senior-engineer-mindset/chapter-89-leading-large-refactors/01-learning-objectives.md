## Learning Objectives

This chapter covers the full lifecycle of leading a large refactor, from deciding whether to do it in the first place, through planning and execution, to knowing when to stop. These are the skills that distinguish senior engineers who talk about refactoring from those who actually deliver successful migrations in production.

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Plan and execute large-scale code migrations
- Manage risk during major refactoring efforts
- Coordinate refactors across multiple teams
- Use patterns like strangler fig for gradual migration
- Maintain velocity during major changes
- Know when to refactor and when not to

### Junior → FAANG: Mapping Objectives to Interview Evidence

Each objective maps to a behavioral question you will face. For "manage risk during major refactoring efforts," your example should name the specific risk, the mitigation you chose, and the outcome. For "coordinate refactors across multiple teams," your example should describe at least two teams with different priorities and the artifact (RFC, shared dashboard, codemod) that coordinated them. If you cannot find a real example for an objective, take on the work before applying.

### Senior @ FAANG: Objectives as a Staff Engineer Evaluation Rubric

At L6, these objectives are evaluated continuously in performance review, not just in interviews. "Maintain velocity during major changes" is assessed by looking at whether feature development continued in parallel with the migration and whether your team's incident rate stayed flat during the transition. Collect these metrics before your review cycle, not after.
