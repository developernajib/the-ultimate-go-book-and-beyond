## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Lead technical initiatives without formal authority
- Influence technical decisions across teams
- Build consensus on complex technical problems
- Mentor engineers effectively at all levels
- Drive architectural decisions with lasting impact
- Navigate the transition from individual contributor to technical leader

### Junior → FAANG: Mapping Learning Objectives to Interview Questions

Each learning objective in this chapter maps directly to a behavioral interview question you will face in a FAANG loop. "Lead technical initiatives without formal authority" maps to: "Tell me about a time you drove a technical direction without being the manager." "Build consensus on complex technical problems" maps to: "Describe a situation where you had to align people with opposing technical views."

Before your interview, write one concrete example for each objective. The example should be specific: name the system, the constraint, the competing perspectives, and the outcome. Vague stories lose bar-raisers. Specific stories with measurable impact win them.

If you cannot find an example from your experience for an objective, that is the gap to close before applying to FAANG. Take on the next design discussion, volunteer to write the next RFC, offer to run the next architectural review. Create the evidence deliberately.

### Senior @ FAANG: Learning Objectives as a Staff Engineer's Operating Checklist

For L6 and above, these objectives are not things you learn once. They are skills you exercise daily and are evaluated on continuously in performance review cycles. "Drive architectural decisions with lasting impact" is not a past achievement. It is an ongoing expectation. Promotion committees want to see that your decisions are still being cited, built upon, and maintained by others 12 to 24 months after you made them.

The most undervalued objective in this list for senior engineers is mentoring effectively without becoming a bottleneck. At L5, engineers mentor by answering questions. At L6, they mentor by creating systems that answer questions at scale: runbooks, design principles, review processes, and documented decisions. If every mentoring relationship depends on your direct availability, you are not operating at staff level regardless of your title.

When preparing for a staff or principal interview, map each objective to an org-wide artifact you have created. Not "I mentored three engineers" but "I designed the onboarding curriculum that reduced time-to-first-PR from six weeks to two across the entire platform team." The difference in scope is the difference in level.
