## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Conduct effective, constructive code reviews
- Build a healthy code review culture in your team
- Scale code reviews across large organizations
- Balance thoroughness with velocity
- Mentor through code reviews
- Handle disagreements constructively

### Junior → FAANG: Mapping Objectives to Interview Evidence

Each objective in this chapter corresponds to a behavioral question in a FAANG loop. "Give constructive feedback" maps to: "Tell me about a time you gave difficult feedback in a code review." "Handle disagreements constructively" maps to: "Describe a situation where you and a reviewer had a fundamental disagreement about approach."

For each objective, write one concrete example from your current experience before submitting a FAANG application. If you cannot produce an example for "balance thoroughness with velocity," you have not yet developed the judgment to manage review tradeoffs under real pressure. Create that experience first.

The "mentor through code reviews" objective is the most differentiating at L5. Candidates who can describe changing an engineer's mental model through a review comment, not just catching a bug, are demonstrating the kind of leverage that FAANG expects at senior level.

### Senior @ FAANG: These Objectives as a Review Culture Maturity Model

At L6 and above, these objectives describe the behaviors your entire team should exhibit, not just the goals you have achieved personally.

A mature review culture is one where every engineer on the team can "conduct effective, constructive code reviews" and "handle disagreements constructively" without your direct involvement. Staff engineers who measure their success by whether they are personally writing great reviews are operating at L5 scope. Staff engineers who measure their success by whether the team's review culture has improved are operating at L6 scope.

"Scale code reviews across large organizations" is explicitly a platform-level objective. It requires investing in tooling, processes, and norms that work without your presence in the review thread. CODEOWNERS, automated pre-review checks, and review SLO dashboards are engineering investments, not soft-skills work, and they belong in quarterly planning with staffing and time allocation.
