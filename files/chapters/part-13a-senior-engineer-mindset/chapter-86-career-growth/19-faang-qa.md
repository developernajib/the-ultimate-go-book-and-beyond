## FAANG Interview Q&A: Career Growth

These questions come up in internal promotion discussions and in external loops where the interviewer is calibrating whether you are operating at the level you claim.

### 1. Describe the most significant work you have done that was outside your assigned scope.

Staff-level promotion committees at Google, Meta, and Stripe weigh this heavily. Identify the problem no one owned, how you made the case that it mattered, the cross-team coalition you built, and the measurable impact. Avoid framing it as "I saw a bug and fixed it." Frame it as "I saw a systemic gap, proposed a direction, and shipped the solution with N other teams."

### 2. Tell me about a time you made another engineer significantly better at their job.

This is the clearest Staff-versus-Senior signal. Senior engineers describe mentorship conversations. Staff engineers describe mentorship outcomes: the engineer who got promoted, the pattern you taught that now appears across the codebase, the on-call rotation that improved because you wrote the runbook. Include the measurable delta.

### 3. You are being considered for promotion to Staff. What are the three strongest artifacts in your packet?

Interviewers want to see that you understand what the level actually rewards. Strong artifacts are usually: an RFC that drove a multi-team technical decision, a migration or platform project where you owned the architecture end-to-end, and a piece of shared infrastructure or developer tooling used by engineers beyond your immediate team. Avoid listing features you shipped.

### 4. How would you evaluate whether to take an IC Staff role or a line management role?

A thoughtful answer weighs your energy sources (technical problems versus people problems), your current strengths and gaps, and the organization's actual needs. Be honest about compensation parity (usually equal at Staff IC and Engineering Manager at FAANG) and about the reversibility of the decision. Most companies allow one track switch per career without penalty, but the second is much harder.

### 5. How do you measure your own impact at the Staff level?

Staff impact is not measured in PRs merged. It is measured in engineer-years saved, incidents prevented, systems that scale without rewrites, and decisions whose positive consequences compound over 12+ months. Name three metrics you track for yourself, at least one of which is organizational rather than technical, and describe how you would know if you were drifting off the Staff path.
