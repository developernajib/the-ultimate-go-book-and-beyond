## Learning Objectives

This section outlines the core skills and knowledge you will gain from studying this chapter. Each objective maps to a specific section below, so you can use this list as a roadmap and come back to check your progress as you work through the material.

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Understand the different engineering career tracks
- Identify what's required at each level
- Create a personal development plan
- Build the skills that drive promotion
- Navigate career conversations effectively
- Choose between IC and management paths

### Junior → FAANG: Using Learning Objectives as a Promotion Checklist

The learning objectives in this chapter are almost identical to the competency expectations in FAANG promotion rubrics. Read each one and ask: can I give a concrete example from my recent work that demonstrates this? The objectives you cannot answer with a concrete example are exactly the experiences you need to create before your next review cycle.

The most common gap: "understand your current level" sounds obvious but most engineers overestimate their current level by one step. The engineers who are consistently promoted on time are the ones who have an accurate, honest picture of where they are relative to the next level and actively close those gaps rather than waiting to be told they are ready.

### Senior @ FAANG: Objectives as Team Capability Assessment

At staff level, these objectives are also a team health assessment tool. Does your team have multiple engineers who can build staff-level skills proactively? Do you have a shared understanding of how to navigate the IC vs management decision? Do you have a development planning practice that is more than an annual performance review?

If the answer to any of these is no, that is an organizational gap that falls within your scope to address. A staff engineer who builds the team's career development infrastructure, even informally, is producing the kind of organizational multiplier impact that justifies the title.
