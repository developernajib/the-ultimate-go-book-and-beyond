## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Distinguish meaningful engineering metrics from vanity metrics that mislead without informing
- Calculate and interpret all four DORA metrics and benchmark your team against elite, high, medium, and low performer profiles
- Explain flow metrics - flow time, flow efficiency, flow velocity, and flow load - and connect them to Little's Law
- Identify developer experience metrics that correlate with team productivity and retention
- Define quality metrics that track real improvement rather than compliance theater
- Set up an engineering metrics dashboard using both commercial tools and a DIY GitHub API + Grafana stack
- Apply Goodhart's Law to protect your metrics from gaming
- Answer common metrics-related interview questions at the senior and staff level

### Junior → FAANG: Mapping Objectives to Interview Questions

Every objective in this list maps to at least one common FAANG interview question at the senior and staff levels. "Distinguish meaningful metrics from vanity metrics" maps to "how would you measure your team's engineering health?" "Apply Goodhart's Law" maps to "tell me about a time a metric led your team in the wrong direction."

Before your next FAANG interview, write a one-paragraph answer to each of those mapped questions. If your answer is thin, the corresponding objective needs more study and practice. The objectives are not abstract goals. They are the specific competencies the interviewer is assessing.

### Senior @ FAANG: Using Objectives to Design a Metrics Program

These objectives can be used directly to design a structured metrics education program for a team that is new to the practice. A four-session workshop series covering DORA metrics, flow metrics, developer experience, and Goodhart's Law gives a team the conceptual foundation they need to use metrics intelligently rather than mechanically. Staff engineers who run this workshop are investing in the team's long-term ability to self-diagnose and self-improve, which is the most valuable form of metrics work.

The "set up an engineering metrics dashboard" objective is the one most likely to be skipped because it requires tooling investment. Resist the temptation to skip it. A team that understands DORA metrics but cannot measure their own DORA metrics is at Level 1 maturity (instrumentation awareness) rather than Level 2 (visibility). The gap between understanding and implementation is where most teams stall.
