## 91.8 Foundation Profile Checklist & Mid-Chapter Synthesis

### Key Takeaways

The following points capture the most important lessons from this chapter. Keep them in mind as you build and refine your professional materials, and revisit them each time you update your resume, LinkedIn, or GitHub profile.

1. **Your resume is a marketing document** - Focus on impact, not activities
2. **Quantify everything** - Numbers make achievements concrete
3. **LinkedIn headline matters most** - Optimize for recruiter searches
4. **GitHub shows real work** - Quality over quantity
5. **Consistency builds brand** - Same story across platforms

### Profile Checklist

Use this checklist as a final review before you start applying to jobs. Go through each item carefully and check it off only when you are confident that section of your profile meets the standards described in this chapter. An incomplete checklist means there are easy improvements still available to you.

**Resume:**
- [ ] One page (or two if 10+ years)
- [ ] Impact bullets with metrics
- [ ] Keywords matching target roles
- [ ] Clean, ATS-friendly format
- [ ] No typos or errors

**LinkedIn:**
- [ ] Professional photo
- [ ] Optimized headline with keywords
- [ ] Compelling About section
- [ ] Complete experience section
- [ ] 3+ recommendations

**GitHub:**
- [ ] Profile README
- [ ] 6 pinned quality repos
- [ ] Each repo has README
- [ ] Consistent activity
- [ ] Contributions visible

### Junior → FAANG: What to Prioritize First

The checklist in this summary is comprehensive, but you do not need to complete all of it before starting your job search. The highest-leverage items for early-career candidates, in priority order: impact-framed resume bullets (every bullet answers "what changed and by how much"), correct LinkedIn headline with technology keywords and open-to-work enabled, and two or three finished GitHub repositories with quality READMEs. Everything else in the checklist improves your results incrementally. Those three items determine whether you get any results at all.

Set a concrete deadline of two weeks to complete those three items before sending any applications. An unoptimized application sent immediately is almost always less effective than a well-prepared application sent two weeks later.

### Senior @ FAANG: Summary as a Governance Audit Framework

At senior and staff level, use this checklist as a quarterly audit, not a one-time setup task. Your materials should evolve as your scope evolves. If you drove a significant architectural decision three months ago and it is not yet reflected in your resume or LinkedIn, you are leaving evidence off the record that a future hiring committee needs to see.

The most important summary principle for senior candidates: the story your materials tell should be the story a hiring committee would construct if they interviewed your past managers, skip-levels, and cross-functional peers. If there is a gap between those two stories, either your materials need updating or you need to be more intentional about making your scope visible to the people who would describe you to a future employer.
