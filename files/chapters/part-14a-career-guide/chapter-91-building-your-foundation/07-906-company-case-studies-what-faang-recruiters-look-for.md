## 91.6 Company Case Studies: What FAANG Recruiters Look For

### Google's Hiring Signals

Google's hiring process is famously rigorous, and recruiters are trained to look for specific patterns when screening resumes. Understanding these signals helps you present your experience in the way that Google's evaluation system is designed to receive it. The criteria below come from publicly shared information by Google recruiters and hiring managers about what actually moves a resume from the "maybe" pile to the "definitely interview" pile.

```
GOOGLE RESUME SCREENING CRITERIA:

Google recruiters are trained to look for specific signals:

1. IMPACT OVER ACTIVITIES
   Bad: "Worked on Google Search"
   Good: "Improved search latency by 30%, affecting 1B+ queries/day"

2. TECHNICAL DEPTH SIGNALS
   - Open source contributions (especially Go ecosystem)
   - Conference talks or publications
   - Patents or technical blog posts
   - Evidence of system design thinking

3. GOOGLE-SPECIFIC PREFERENCES:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  STRONG SIGNALS                                            │
   │  ✓ Computer Science degree (but not required with exp)     │
   │  ✓ Experience at other top-tier companies                  │
   │  ✓ Open source project maintainer                          │
   │  ✓ Quantified impact metrics                               │
   │  ✓ Evidence of "Googleyness" (collaboration, growth)       │
   │                                                            │
   │  NEUTRAL                                                   │
   │  ○ Years of experience (beyond 2-3)                        │
   │  ○ Title at current company                                │
   │  ○ Certifications                                          │
   │                                                            │
   │  WEAK/NEGATIVE                                             │
   │  ✗ Job hopping (<1 year stints)                            │
   │  ✗ Vague bullet points                                     │
   │  ✗ Technology laundry lists                                │
   │  ✗ Long periods of unemployment (without explanation)      │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

4. GOOGLE RESUME EXAMPLE (what works):
   "Led migration of YouTube's video processing pipeline to Go,
   reducing processing time by 40% and infrastructure costs by
   $2M annually. The system processes 500K videos/day with 99.99%
   reliability."
```

### Meta's Profile Evaluation

Meta (formerly Facebook) has a distinct hiring culture that prioritizes speed, impact, and boldness. Their recruiters evaluate candidates through the lens of Meta's core values, so your resume and LinkedIn profile should reflect those values in the language you use and the achievements you highlight. The guide below shows how to frame your experience in ways that align with what Meta's screening process specifically rewards.

```
META RECRUITING PRIORITIES:

1. IMPACT FOCUS
   Meta looks for candidates who can articulate business impact:
   - "Reduced revenue loss" not "fixed bugs"
   - "Improved user engagement" not "built features"
   - "Scaled system to handle growth" not "worked on infrastructure"

2. LINKEDIN OPTIMIZATION FOR META:
   - Use "impact" language in headlines
   - Include specific metrics
   - Show growth trajectory
   - Highlight collaborative projects

3. META'S UNIQUE SIGNALS:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  What Meta recruiters specifically look for:               │
   │                                                            │
   │  MOVE FAST                                                 │
   │  - Multiple shipped projects                               │
   │  - Evidence of rapid iteration                             │
   │  - Startup or high-growth experience                       │
   │                                                            │
   │  FOCUS ON IMPACT                                           │
   │  - Clear metrics in every role                             │
   │  - Business outcomes, not activities                       │
   │  - Scope of influence                                      │
   │                                                            │
   │  BE BOLD                                                   │
   │  - Risk-taking evidence                                    │
   │  - Innovative solutions                                    │
   │  - Challenging status quo                                  │
   │                                                            │
   │  BUILD SOCIAL VALUE                                        │
   │  - Connection to products that help people                 │
   │  - User-focused thinking                                   │

4. META-STYLE BULLET POINTS:

```

### Amazon's Leadership Principle-Aligned Resume

Amazon's entire hiring process revolves around their Leadership Principles, and this starts at the resume screening stage. Recruiters are trained to identify which Leadership Principles each bullet point on your resume maps to, so writing your resume with these principles in mind gives you a significant advantage. The examples below show how to reframe common engineering achievements using language that directly signals the Leadership Principles Amazon values most.

```
AMAZON RESUME OPTIMIZATION:

Amazon recruiters screen for Leadership Principles:

1. LP-ALIGNED RESUME WRITING:
   Each bullet should map to an LP:

   CUSTOMER OBSESSION:
   "Redesigned checkout flow based on customer feedback,
   reducing cart abandonment by 23% and increasing
   conversion by $12M annually"

   OWNERSHIP:
   "Owned end-to-end delivery of payment processing system
   from design through launch, leading 4-person team"

   INVENT AND SIMPLIFY:
   "Created novel caching strategy reducing database load
   by 70%, subsequently adopted as company-wide standard"

   DIVE DEEP:
   "Identified root cause of production outages through
   deep packet analysis, preventing $500K in potential losses"

2. AMAZON SCREENING CHECKLIST:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  MUST HAVE:                                                │
   │  □ Quantified achievements with metrics                    │
   │  □ Evidence of ownership (not just "worked on")            │
   │  □ Customer impact stories                                 │
   │  □ Technical depth signals                                 │
   │                                                            │
   │  BONUS:                                                    │
   │  □ Experience with AWS services                            │
   │  □ Evidence of operational excellence                      │

   │  □ Hiring or mentoring experience                          │

### Stripe's Craft-Focused Screening

Stripe is known for placing an unusually high value on engineering "craft," which means they care deeply about code quality, attention to detail, and thoughtful design rather than just raw speed or scale. This emphasis extends to how they evaluate candidates before the interview even begins. Your resume language, GitHub code quality, and even the formatting of your application materials all send signals about the level of craft you bring to your work.

```
STRIPE RESUME EVALUATION:

Stripe emphasizes "craft" in engineering:

1. WHAT STRIPE LOOKS FOR:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  CRAFT SIGNALS:                                            │
   │  - Evidence of code quality focus                          │
   │  - Testing and reliability mentions                        │
   │  - Open source code samples                                │
   │  - Attention to detail in resume itself                    │
   │                                                            │
   │  TECHNICAL DEPTH:                                          │
   │  - Systems programming experience                          │
   │  - Financial/payments domain (helpful)                     │
   │  - API design experience                                   │
   │  - Distributed systems                                     │
   │                                                            │
   │  COMMUNICATION:                                            │
   │  - Clear, precise language                                 │
   │  - Well-organized presentation                             │
   │  - Technical writing samples (blog, docs)                  │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

2. STRIPE GITHUB EXPECTATIONS:
   - Clean, well-documented code
   - Tests included in projects
   - Evidence of attention to edge cases
   - Clear commit messages

3. STRIPE-OPTIMIZED BULLET:

### Netflix's Culture Fit Screening

Netflix operates under a unique "Freedom and Responsibility" culture that fundamentally shapes how they evaluate candidates. They look for engineers who can operate with high autonomy, make bold decisions without waiting for permission, and take full ownership of outcomes. Your materials need to demonstrate these qualities explicitly, because Netflix actively screens out candidates who seem like they need heavy process or management oversight to be effective.

```
NETFLIX RESUME AND PROFILE SIGNALS:

Netflix's unique culture requires specific signals:

1. FREEDOM & RESPONSIBILITY:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  POSITIVE SIGNALS:                                         │
   │  - Evidence of autonomous decision-making                  │
   │  - High impact with minimal supervision                    │
   │  - Judgment calls documented                               │
   │  - Handling ambiguity examples                             │
   │                                                            │
   │  KEEPER TEST EVIDENCE:                                     │
   │  - Would your current manager fight to keep you?           │
   │  - Are you the best person available for this role?        │
   │  - Show irreplaceable contributions                        │
   │                                                            │
   │  WHAT NETFLIX AVOIDS:                                      │
   │  - Process-heavy language                                  │
   │  - Committee decision-making                               │
   │  - Titles over substance                                   │
   │  - Incremental improvements only                           │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

2. NETFLIX-STYLE IMPACT STATEMENT:
   "Unilaterally decided to rewrite streaming buffer algorithm
   after identifying 30% efficiency opportunity. Implemented
   and deployed in 6 weeks, reducing buffering events by 40%
   for 200M+ subscribers."

3. LINKEDIN FOR NETFLIX:
   - Emphasize high-agency behavior
   - Show bold decision-making
   - Highlight operating without direction
   - Demonstrate learning from failures
```

### Junior → FAANG: Reading Company Signals and Tailoring Accordingly

Each company in these case studies is telling you exactly what to optimize for. Google's emphasis on intellectual curiosity and problem-solving process means your resume should highlight ambiguous problems you navigated and the reasoning you applied, not just the solutions you shipped. Amazon's leadership principle evaluation means every behavioral question in the recruiter screen maps to a specific principle, and your materials should use language that reflects ownership, bias for action, and customer obsession without sounding like you memorized a vocabulary list.

The practical application: before applying to any company in this list, spend 30 minutes reading their engineering blog. Note the specific technical challenges they describe, the language they use to frame problems, and the outcomes they highlight. Then review your resume and ensure at least two of your bullets speak directly to those problem types. This is not manipulation. It is demonstrating that you have read the room and that your experience is genuinely relevant to what they are building.

For Go-specific roles, the case studies reveal which companies treat Go as a first-class language versus an opportunistic choice. Google, Cloudflare, Stripe, and Uber treat Go as a core infrastructure language. At these companies, deep Go knowledge is a direct signal. At companies where Go is used for specific services alongside other primary languages, Go fluency is a differentiator but not a gating requirement.

### Senior @ FAANG: Company Evaluation as a Two-Way Fit Assessment

At senior level, the case studies in this section should be read as evaluation criteria for the company, not just criteria the company applies to you. A staff engineer interviewing at Amazon should be assessing whether Amazon's culture of written communication and strong opinions held loosely aligns with how they actually work, not just preparing to demonstrate that they can answer behavioral questions in leadership principle terms.

The governance implication: each of these company cultures has a distinct model for how technical authority is established and exercised. At Google, technical authority flows through the TL (Tech Lead) role and through design doc review culture. At Amazon, it flows through the PR/FAQ process and the single-threaded ownership model. At Meta, it flows through speed of execution and measurable product impact. Joining a company whose authority model conflicts with how you naturally work is a recipe for an unsuccessful placement regardless of your technical qualifications.

For L6 and above candidates, the company case studies should inform a concrete set of questions to answer during the interview process before accepting any offer. How are architectural decisions made? What is the governance process for cross-team technical standards? How do staff engineers spend their time across individual contribution, influence, and strategy? How does the organization evaluate staff-level performance differently from senior-level performance? The answers to these questions predict whether your first year will be a success or a frustrating mismatch more accurately than any technical signal.

ADR thinking applied to the company selection decision itself: write down the criteria that matter to you, the options you are considering, the tradeoffs you see, and the reasoning behind your final choice. This exercise is useful whether or not you share it publicly, because it forces the kind of rigorous thinking about fit that most engineers skip in their excitement about an offer.
