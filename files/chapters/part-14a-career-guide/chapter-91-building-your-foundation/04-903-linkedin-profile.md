## 91.3 LinkedIn Profile

### Profile Optimization

LinkedIn is where most tech recruiters do their initial candidate sourcing, so your profile needs to work as both a search result and a landing page. The headline is the single most important field because it appears in search results, connection requests, and comments you leave on other posts. Beyond the headline, each section of your profile serves a different purpose in the recruiter's evaluation process. The optimization guide below covers every element from photo to recommendations.


### Sample LinkedIn About Section

Your About section is where you tell your professional story in your own voice. The first two lines are critical because they appear before the "see more" fold, so they need to hook the reader immediately. The example below shows how to combine a compelling opening, quantified achievements, technical interests, and a clear call to action into a section that both recruiters and hiring managers find engaging.

```
Senior Software Engineer passionate about building reliable,
high-scale systems that handle real money.

Currently at Stripe, I lead the payment routing team. Highlights:
• Redesigned routing engine handling $50B annually (40% faster)
• Built fraud detection processing 500K events/second
• Mentored 4 engineers to promotion

Previously at Uber, I worked on rider matching and observability:
• Core matching serving 20M rides/day
• Distributed tracing adopted by 50+ services
• Migrated Python → Go, 5x throughput improvement

Tech I love: Go, Kubernetes, PostgreSQL, Kafka, distributed systems

I write about distributed systems and Go on my blog: [link]

Open to: Senior/Staff Engineer roles focused on payments,
financial infrastructure, or distributed systems.

Let's connect! Always happy to chat about Go, system design,
or career growth. DM me or email: alex@email.com
```

### LinkedIn Settings for Job Search

LinkedIn has several hidden settings that control whether recruiters can find you and how your profile appears in their searches. Many engineers miss these entirely, which means their optimized profile never surfaces in the right search results. Taking five minutes to configure these settings correctly can be the difference between getting inbound recruiter messages and hearing nothing at all.

```
OPEN TO WORK (Visible to recruiters only):
Settings → Preferences → Job seeking preferences

Set:
✓ "Let recruiters know you're open"
✓ Job titles you're interested in
✓ Locations (or Remote)
✓ Start date
✓ Job types (Full-time, Contract, etc.)

PROFILE VISIBILITY:
Settings → Visibility → Profile viewing options
→ Show name and headline (not anonymous)

INMAIL:
Settings → Communications → Who can reach you
→ Open to receiving InMail
```

### Junior → FAANG: LinkedIn as Your Inbound Search Engine

Most junior engineers treat LinkedIn as an online resume. Recruiters treat it as a search engine. The difference in framing changes what you optimize for. Recruiters at FAANG companies run Boolean searches with role titles, technologies, and location. If your headline is "Software Engineer" and your experience section uses only job titles without technology keywords, you will not appear in those searches.

The fastest fixes: update your headline to include your primary technology and domain ("Go Backend Engineer | Distributed Systems | Infrastructure"), add specific technology keywords to each experience entry and to the skills section, and turn on the "Open to Work" signal visible to recruiters only. This last step costs nothing and immediately increases the volume of recruiter outreach.

Activity matters more than most junior engineers expect. Recruiters are more likely to reach out to profiles they have seen active recently. Commenting thoughtfully on technical posts by engineers at your target companies, sharing short observations about Go-related topics, or writing a brief post about a problem you solved all increase your profile's visibility and signal that you are engaged with the community.

### Senior @ FAANG: LinkedIn as Platform and Signal Management

At senior level, LinkedIn serves a different function. The recruiter search optimization still matters, but the more important signal is the quality and recency of the content you have produced and the network endorsements you have accumulated from people whose names carry weight in the industry.

A staff engineer who has published three well-reasoned technical posts on LinkedIn in the past year, has endorsements from recognized technical leaders, and whose activity feed shows thoughtful engagement with complex system design discussions is positioned categorically differently from a staff engineer with an identical resume who has been inactive for two years. The LinkedIn algorithm surfaces active contributors, and FAANG technical recruiters are more likely to reach out to candidates who are visibly engaged in technical discourse.

Governance of your LinkedIn presence at senior level means treating it as a managed communication channel, not a static profile. This includes: curating recommendations to ensure they speak to leadership and scope (not just "great to work with"), using the featured section to highlight technical writing, talks, or projects that demonstrate staff-level impact, and managing your connections actively so that your network includes people at the companies you want to target.

The summary section is where seniority signals most clearly. A strong L6-L7 summary names the types of problems you are drawn to (large-scale distributed systems, developer platform design, data-intensive architectures), the organizational contexts in which you have operated (cross-org technical leadership, 0-to-1 platform builds), and the values that drive your decision-making. This is not a job description. It is a positioning statement that tells a technical hiring manager in three sentences what kind of problems you should be working on next.
