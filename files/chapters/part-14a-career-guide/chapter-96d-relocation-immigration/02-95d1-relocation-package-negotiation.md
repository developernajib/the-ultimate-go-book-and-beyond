```
TWO COMMON APPROACHES:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LUMP SUM RELOCATION                                             │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                  │
│  HOW IT WORKS:                                                   │
│  Company gives you fixed amount (e.g., $15,000-$50,000)          │
│  You handle all logistics yourself                               │
│                                                                  │
│  PROS:                                                           │
│  ✓ Flexibility - spend how you want                              │

│  ✓ Less paperwork and receipts                                   │
│  ✓ Can optimize for your situation                               │
│                                                                  │
│  CONS:                                                           │
│  ✗ Taxed as income (you receive ~65% after taxes)                │
│  ✗ Risk of underestimating costs                                 │
│  ✗ You handle all the logistics                                  │
│  ✗ May not cover expensive moves                                 │
│                                                                  │
│  EXAMPLE:                                                        │
│  $30,000 lump sum                                                │
│  After ~35% tax: $19,500 actual                                  │
│  Cross-country move cost: $15,000                                │
│  Remaining for other expenses: $4,500                            │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  MANAGED RELOCATION                                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                  │
│  HOW IT WORKS:                                                   │
│  Company partners with relocation firm                           │
│  They coordinate and pay for everything directly                 │
│                                                                  │
│  PROS:                                                           │
│  ✓ Less taxable to you (company pays vendors directly)           │
│  ✓ Professional coordination                                     │
│  ✓ Usually broader coverage                                      │
│  ✓ Less personal risk                                            │
│                                                                  │
│  CONS:                                                           │
│  ✗ Less flexibility                                              │
│  ✗ Must use approved vendors                                     │
│  ✗ Company controls the process                                  │
│  ✗ May have caps on services                                     │
│                                                                  │
│  TYPICAL INCLUSIONS:                                             │
│  - Full-service moving company                                   │
```

### Tax Gross-Up: Critical Understanding

Relocation benefits are taxable income. Without a tax gross-up, a $20,000 relocation package might net you only $13,000 after taxes. A gross-up means the company pays the additional taxes so you receive the full benefit amount. This is one of the most commonly overlooked details in relocation negotiations.

```
WHY TAX GROSS-UP MATTERS:

Without gross-up, relocation benefits are taxable income.

EXAMPLE WITHOUT GROSS-UP:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Relocation package value: $50,000                               │
│  Your tax bracket: 35%                                           │
│  Tax owed on relocation: $17,500                                 │
│                                                                  │
│  You receive: $50,000 in moving services                         │
│  You owe: $17,500 additional taxes                               │
│  Net benefit: $32,500                                            │
│                                                                  │
│  This $17,500 comes out of YOUR pocket.                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

WITH TAX GROSS-UP:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Relocation package value: $50,000                               │
│  Company also provides: ~$27,000 gross-up                        │
│  (Extra payment to cover taxes on the taxable portion)           │
│                                                                  │
│  Result: You receive full $50,000 benefit                        │
│  Company pays the taxes for you                                  │
│  Net cost to you: $0                                             │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

NEGOTIATION TIP:
Always ask: "Is the relocation package tax gross-up included?"
If not, negotiate for it or factor taxes into lump sum amount.
```

### Relocation Negotiation Strategies

Compensation negotiation is a professional skill that engineers often underprepare for, leaving significant value on the table. The framework below provides structured language and decision criteria for evaluating and responding to offers effectively.

```
WHAT'S TYPICALLY NEGOTIABLE:

HIGH FLEXIBILITY:
├── Lump sum amount
├── Temporary housing duration
├── Number of house-hunting trips
├── Start date (to align with move)
└── Sign-on bonus (can offset relo costs)

MEDIUM FLEXIBILITY:
├── Tax gross-up
├── Spouse career assistance
├── Home sale assistance terms
└── Storage duration

LOWER FLEXIBILITY:
├── Moving company selection (in managed relo)
├── Per diem rates
└── Policy exceptions

NEGOTIATION SCRIPT:
"The role is very exciting, and I'm ready to relocate.
Looking at the relocation package, I'd like to discuss
a few adjustments:

1. Could we extend temporary housing to 60 days?
   Finding housing in [city] takes time, especially
   with a family.

2. Is tax gross-up included? If not, could we add
   that or increase the lump sum to offset?

3. My spouse is also a professional. Is there
   spouse career assistance available?"
```

### Junior → FAANG: Relocation Package Negotiation Basics

Most early-career candidates accept the standard relocation package without negotiation because they do not know what is possible or feel awkward asking. The standard package at most FAANG companies includes moving expense reimbursement and sometimes one to two months of temporary housing. The negotiated package can additionally include a cost of living adjustment for the transition period, spouse career assistance, house-hunting trip expenses, and a signing bonus that specifically addresses the financial gap of relocating before your first paycheck arrives.

The specific items to request when the standard package is offered: itemized reimbursement up to a higher cap (most companies have unpublicized caps that exceed the standard offer), temporary furnished housing for 60 to 90 days rather than the standard 30, and a cash relocation bonus in addition to reimbursements (useful for deposits and incidentals not covered by reimbursement).

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Relocation Package Governance and the Cost Architecture Analysis

At senior level, relocation packages are fully negotiable and the key discipline is significant. A senior engineer who is genuinely relocating from one coast to another is incurring costs in the $15,000 to $50,000 range depending on family size, housing market conditions, and the need to break a lease or bridge a home sale. Negotiating a package that covers the actual cost is reasonable and expected.

The governance analysis: model your actual relocation costs before the negotiation. Include: moving company costs, temporary housing, lease break penalties or real estate transaction costs if selling a home, travel for family during the transition, and the income gap if your spouse or partner needs time to find employment in the new market. Present this analysis in your negotiation rather than accepting whatever is offered. A well-documented cost analysis is much more persuasive than a general request for a "better" relocation package.

Dual-income households with one partner in a career that is not easily transferable require explicit negotiation of spouse career assistance programs. Most FAANG companies have these programs but do not publicize them. Asking specifically whether outplacement assistance, career coaching, or networking support is available for your spouse is worth the conversation.
