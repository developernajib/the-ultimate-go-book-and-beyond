### Exercise 1: Comprehensive Relocation Financial Analysis

**Scenario**: You have offers from two companies:
- **Company A**: $200,000 total comp in San Francisco
- **Company B**: $160,000 total comp in Austin, TX

Analyze which offer provides better purchasing power.

**Complete Solution**:

```markdown
# Relocation Financial Analysis Worksheet

## Step 1: Calculate Net Income After Taxes

### San Francisco (Company A): $200,000

**Federal Tax:**
- Taxable income (single, standard deduction): $186,400
- Federal tax estimate: ~$42,000

**California State Tax:**
- California tax on $200K: ~$16,000

**FICA (Social Security + Medicare):**
- 7.65% up to SS cap + Medicare: ~$13,500

**Net Income:**
$200,000 - $42,000 - $16,000 - $13,500 = **$128,500**

---

### Austin (Company B): $160,000

**Federal Tax:**
- Taxable income (single, standard deduction): $146,400
- Federal tax estimate: ~$30,000

**Texas State Tax:**
- $0 (no state income tax)

**FICA:**
- ~$11,500

**Net Income:**
$160,000 - $30,000 - $0 - $11,500 = **$118,500**

---

## Step 2: Cost of Living Comparison

| Category | San Francisco | Austin | Notes |
|----------|---------------|--------|-------|
| Rent (1BR) | $3,500/mo | $1,600/mo | -$22,800/yr |
| Utilities | $150/mo | $150/mo | Similar |
| Transportation | $200/mo | $300/mo | +$1,200/yr (car needed in Austin) |
| Food | $800/mo | $600/mo | -$2,400/yr |
| Healthcare | $300/mo | $300/mo | Similar (employer plan) |
| Entertainment | $400/mo | $300/mo | -$1,200/yr |

**Annual Cost Difference:**
San Francisco: $64,200/year
Austin: $39,000/year
**Difference: $25,200/year savings in Austin**

---

## Step 3: Calculate Real Purchasing Power

### San Francisco:
- Net income: $128,500
- COL expenses: -$64,200
- **Remaining for savings/discretionary: $64,300**

### Austin:
- Net income: $118,500
- COL expenses: -$39,000
- **Remaining for savings/discretionary: $79,500**

---

## Step 4: Summary

| Metric | San Francisco | Austin | Winner |
|--------|---------------|--------|--------|
| Gross Income | $200,000 | $160,000 | SF (+$40K) |
| Net Income | $128,500 | $118,500 | SF (+$10K) |
| Cost of Living | $64,200 | $39,000 | Austin (-$25K) |
| Purchasing Power | $64,300 | $79,500 | **Austin (+$15K)** |

## Conclusion:

Despite $40,000 lower gross income, Austin provides **$15,200 more
purchasing power** per year due to:
- No state income tax (-$16K tax savings)
- Much lower housing costs (-$22.8K)

**Over 5 years**: Austin = $76,000 more savings

---

## Additional Considerations:

**Factors favoring San Francisco:**
- More job opportunities if this role doesn't work out
- Higher comp ceiling for future roles
- Network density (more senior engineers)

**Factors favoring Austin:**
- Growing tech hub (increasing opportunities)
- Can buy a home much sooner
- Better quality of life for the salary

**Recommendation:** Unless SF role has significantly better career
trajectory (promotion path, company brand, specific learning),
Austin provides meaningfully better financial outcome.
```

### Exercise 2: Immigration Timeline Planning

**Scenario**: You're a software engineer from India on OPT (Optional Practical Training), considering job offers. Create a detailed immigration timeline plan.

**Complete Solution**:

```markdown
# Immigration Planning for Indian National on OPT

## Current Status Analysis

**OPT Timeline:**
- OPT expires: [Current date + remaining OPT time]
- STEM extension eligible: Yes (3 additional years possible)
- Total OPT time available: Up to 3 years

---

## Path Comparison

### Path A: H-1B → EB-2 Green Card

**Year 1: H-1B Application**
```
October: Start job on OPT
March: Employer registers for H-1B lottery
April: Lottery results
 - If selected: H-1B petition filed
 - If not selected: Remain on OPT/STEM extension
October: H-1B status begins (if approved)
```

**Year 2-3: Continue H-1B, Start Green Card**
```
Year 2: Employer begins PERM process
 - Prevailing wage determination (3-6 months)
 - Recruitment period (2-3 months)
 - PERM filing and approval (6-18 months)

Year 3: I-140 Petition
 - File I-140 after PERM approval
 - Premium processing: 15 days
 - I-140 approval locks priority date
```

**Year 4-15+: Wait for Priority Date**
```
Current EB-2 backlog for India: ~10-12 years
Priority date: Date PERM was filed
Wait for visa bulletin to become current
File I-485 when date is current
Green card approval
```

**Total time (India, EB-2): 12-15+ years**

---

### Path B: O-1 Visa (If Qualifiable)

**Requirements Check:**
□ Patents filed/granted
□ Judging others' work (conference reviews)
□ Original contributions (open source, papers)
□ Published articles/media coverage
□ High salary (top 10% in field)
□ Critical role at distinguished organization
□ Awards or prizes

**If 3+ criteria met:**
```
Month 1-3: Gather evidence, prepare petition
Month 4: File O-1 petition
Month 5: Approval (or 15 days with premium)
Immediate: Start working, no lottery!
```

**Advantages:**
- No lottery, no cap
- Faster processing
- Can start green card immediately

---

### Path C: L-1 via International Office

**If employer has international offices:**
```
Year 1: Work at US office on OPT
Year 2: Transfer to Canada/UK office for 1 year
Year 3: Return on L-1 visa (no lottery)
Year 4+: Start green card process
```

**Advantages:**
- Avoids H-1B lottery
- L-1 has direct EB-1C path for managers

---

### Path D: Canadian PR as Backup

**Parallel Process:**
```
Month 1: Submit Express Entry profile
 - CRS score calculation
 - Language test (IELTS)

Month 2-6: Wait for ITA (Invitation to Apply)
 - May need provincial nomination for extra points

Month 7-12: PR application processing
 - Background check, medical exam

Month 12-18: Canadian PR granted
```

**Why This Helps:**
- If H-1B lottery fails: Relocate to Canada
- Work for same company's Canadian office
- Apply for L-1 transfer to US after 1 year
- Or stay in Canada (shorter path to citizenship)

---

## Recommended Combined Strategy

**Timeline:**

| Month | H-1B Track | Backup Track |
|-------|------------|--------------|
| 1-3 | Start job on OPT | Begin Express Entry |
| 3 | H-1B lottery registration | Submit EE profile |
| 4 | Lottery results | |
| 6 | If not selected: | Pursue provincial nomination |
| 12 | Continue STEM OPT | Canadian PR (if needed) |
| 18 | Try H-1B lottery again | |
| 24 | If H-1B approved: begin PERM | |
| 36 | I-140 filed | |
| 48+ | Wait for priority date | |

---

## Key Dates and Deadlines

| Item | Deadline | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| STEM OPT application | 60 days before OPT expires | Don't miss! |
| H-1B registration | Early March annually | Employer must register |
| IELTS for Canada | Before Express Entry | Results valid 2 years |
| I-140 portability | 180 days after filing | Can change employers |

---

## Questions to Ask Employers

1. "What's your green card sponsorship policy?"
2. "When would you file PERM - immediately or after X months?"
3. "Do you support premium processing for H-1B and I-140?"
4. "Do you have international offices I could work at?"
5. "Have you sponsored EB-1 for senior engineers?"

---

## Financial Planning for Immigration

**Estimated Costs (often employer-covered):**
- H-1B filing: $5,000-$10,000
- Premium processing: $2,805
- PERM: $5,000-$10,000
- I-140: $715 + $2,805 premium
- I-485: $1,225 + biometrics

**Self-Funded (if needed):**
- O-1 petition: $10,000-$15,000 (lawyers)
- Canadian Express Entry: $2,000-$3,000
- Stock option exercise if leaving: Variable

---

## Decision Framework

If lottery selected → Stay on H-1B path
If lottery fails twice → Strongly consider Canada backup
If O-1 qualifiable → Pursue regardless of H-1B
If offered L-1 path → Often better than H-1B lottery risk
```

### Exercise 4: Relocation Package Negotiation Checklist

**Create a complete checklist for negotiating relocation packages.**

**Complete Solution**:

```markdown
# Relocation Package Negotiation Checklist

## Pre-Negotiation Research

**Understand Your Costs:**
□ Get moving quotes (3 companies)
□ Research temporary housing costs
□ Calculate first/last/deposit for new apartment
□ Estimate travel costs for house-hunting
□ Know your tax bracket (to calculate gross-up value)

**Understand Company Norms:**
□ Ask recruiter: "What's typical for relocating employees?"
□ Check Glassdoor/Blind for company relocation experiences
□ Ask connections at the company

---

## Negotiation Checklist

### Moving Costs
□ **Professional moving service**
   - Full packing and unpacking?
   - Insurance coverage limit?
   - What if items damaged?

□ **Vehicle shipping** (if applicable)
   - How many vehicles covered?
   - Door-to-door or terminal?

□ **Storage** (if needed)
   - Duration covered?
   - Monthly limit?

### Temporary Housing
□ **Duration**
   - Standard: 30 days
   - Ask for: 60-90 days
   - Extension process if needed?

□ **Type**
   - Corporate apartment?
   - Hotel?
   - Per diem for Airbnb?

□ **Family included?**
   - Spouse and children?
   - Pets allowed?

### House-Hunting
□ **Trips covered**
   - How many trips?
   - How many days per trip?
   - Flights + hotel included?

□ **Family included?**
   - Spouse travel covered?
   - Childcare during trips?

### Financial
□ **Lump sum amount**
   - Typical: $5,000-$50,000
   - Ask: "What's the range for this level?"

□ **Tax gross-up** (CRITICAL!)
   - "Is the package tax-grossed?"
   - If no: Negotiate higher lump sum

□ **Home sale assistance** (if homeowner)
   - Guaranteed buyout?
   - Loss-on-sale protection?
   - Closing cost coverage?

□ **Lease break fee** (if renting)
   - Will company cover early termination?
   - What documentation needed?

### Family Support
□ **Spouse career assistance**
   - Job search services?
   - Resume coaching?

□ **School search** (if kids)
   - Research assistance?
   - Application support?

### Timing
□ **Start date flexibility**
   - Buffer between arrival and start?
   - Can start date accommodate vesting/bonus?

---

## Negotiation Scripts

### Asking About Package Details:
"I'm excited about this opportunity. Before we finalize, can you walk
me through the complete relocation package? I want to make sure I
understand all the components."

### Requesting Tax Gross-Up:
"I noticed the package is [amount]. Is that before or after tax
gross-up? Relocation benefits are taxable, so without gross-up,
the actual value is significantly less. Can we include gross-up?"

### Requesting More Temporary Housing:
"30 days of temporary housing is tight for finding permanent housing
in [city], especially with my family. Could we extend to 60 or 90 days?
I want to find the right place without rushing, so I can focus fully
on the job."

### Requesting Spouse Support:
"My spouse is also a professional and will need to find work in [city].
Does the company offer any spouse career assistance programs? This
would significantly help with our transition."

### If Package Is Non-Negotiable:
"I understand the relocation policy is standard. Would it be possible
to increase the sign-on bonus to help offset the difference between
the package and my actual relocation costs?"

---

## Red Flags

□ "We'll figure it out after you start" - Get it in writing before
□ "Standard policy, no exceptions" - Sign-on can often compensate
□ No mention of tax gross-up - Ask explicitly
□ Very short temporary housing - 30 days is often insufficient
□ Lump sum with clawback - Know repayment terms if you leave early

---

## Final Checklist Before Accepting

□ Full package details in writing
□ Tax gross-up confirmed (or lump sum adjusted)
□ Start date accommodates your timeline
□ Temporary housing duration sufficient
□ Family needs addressed
□ Clawback terms understood
□ Total value calculated vs actual costs

---

## Package Value Calculator

| Component | Standard | Negotiated | Value |
|-----------|----------|------------|-------|
| Moving service | $8,000 | $10,000 | |
| Temporary housing (60 days) | $6,000 | $9,000 | |
| House-hunting trips (2) | $2,000 | $3,000 | |
| Lump sum | $10,000 | $15,000 | |
| Tax gross-up (35%) | $0 | $5,250 | |
| Lease break | $0 | $3,000 | |
| **Total** | **$26,000** | **$45,250** | |

The negotiated package is worth **$19,250 more** than standard.
This is money you'd otherwise pay out of pocket.
```

### Junior → FAANG: Exercises as Pre-Move Financial and Logistical Preparation

The financial analysis exercises in this section are most valuable when completed before you begin a relocation job search, not after you receive an offer. Understanding your actual cost-of-living requirements in multiple target markets lets you evaluate offers in context rather than in isolation. A $150K offer in Austin and a $200K offer in San Francisco may represent similar real purchasing power after housing and state tax adjustments.

Complete the cost of living analysis exercise for any market you are seriously targeting, using current rental data rather than averages. The gap between average and current rental market data in high-demand cities can be 20 to 30 percent and has a significant effect on your financial planning.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Exercise Extensions and the Multi-Year Location Strategy

At senior level, extend the relocation analysis exercises to a four-year horizon. Model: total compensation trajectory over four years at each location, expected housing appreciation or rental cost trajectory, immigration timeline implications if relevant, and the career optionality value of being in a location with multiple employers in your domain versus a single-employer market.

The immigration timeline exercise is the highest-stakes exercise in this section for engineers on work visas. Model your current priority date position, the expected timeline to green card approval, the job change implications during that period, and the specific milestones (I-140 approval, 180-day rule eligibility) that create career flexibility. This timeline should be a living document that you update quarterly and factor into every major career decision.

---

## Summary

### Key Takeaways
- **Total Purchasing Power Modeling**: Never evaluate relocation offers purely on top-line compensation; factor in local cost-of-living indices, state/municipal income taxes, and net disposable income.
- **Relocation Package Negotiation**: Treat relocation packages as negotiable compensation components; insist on itemized full-service moving, temporary housing (60-90 days), and mandatory tax gross-up provisions.
- **Strategic Immigration Governance**: Proactively manage visa timelines (H-1B, O-1, EB-1/EB-2) and port priority dates under AC-21, establishing contingency pathways early in your engineering career.
