## 96D.5 Remote Work Considerations

### Remote-First vs Remote-Friendly

"Remote-friendly" and "remote-first" sound similar but describe very different work environments. Remote-first companies design all processes, meetings, and communication for distributed teams. Remote-friendly companies allow remote work but often default to in-office norms that disadvantage remote employees. This distinction matters for your career progression and daily experience.

```
UNDERSTANDING REMOTE POLICIES:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  REMOTE-FIRST                                                    │
│  ├── Company designed around remote work                         │
│  ├── No (or minimal) physical offices                            │
│  ├── Asynchronous communication default                          │
│  ├── Equal treatment regardless of location                      │
│  └── Examples: GitLab, Zapier, Automattic                        │
│                                                                  │
│  REMOTE-FRIENDLY                                                 │
│  ├── Has offices, but allows remote work                         │
│  ├── Some roles remote, others require office                    │
│  ├── May have in-person meeting requirements                     │
│  ├── Career progression may favor in-office                      │
│  └── Examples: Many big tech companies post-COVID                │
│                                                                  │
│  HYBRID                                                          │
│  ├── Mix of remote and in-office days                            │
│  ├── Typically 2-3 days in office required                       │
│  ├── Must live within commuting distance                         │
│  └── Most common current model at big tech                       │
│                                                                  │
│  REMOTE-POSSIBLE (aka "Distributed")                             │
│  ├── Can work remotely from approved locations                   │
│  ├── May have geographic restrictions                            │
│  ├── Compensation often location-adjusted                        │
│  └── May need to come to office quarterly/annually               │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Location-Based Compensation

Compensation negotiation is a professional skill that engineers often underprepare for, leaving significant value on the table. The framework below provides structured language and decision criteria for evaluating and responding to offers effectively.

```
REMOTE COMPENSATION MODELS:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  MODEL 1: LOCATION-AGNOSTIC (Same pay everywhere)                │
│  ├── Same compensation regardless of location                    │
│  ├── Rare, typically smaller companies                           │
│  └── Examples: Basecamp historically                             │
│                                                                  │
│  MODEL 2: LOCATION-BASED BANDS                                   │
│  ├── Different pay for different regions                         │
│  ├── Usually: SF/NYC = 100%, Other = 80-90%                      │
│  └── Examples: Most big tech companies                           │
│                                                                  │
│  MODEL 3: COST-OF-LABOR ADJUSTED                                 │
│  ├── Based on local market rates                                 │
│  ├── More granular than bands                                    │
│  ├── Transparent calculators sometimes available                 │
│  └── Examples: GitLab (public compensation calculator)           │
│                                                                  │
│  MODEL 4: COST-OF-LIVING ADJUSTED                                │
│  ├── Based on local cost of living                               │
│  ├── Similar to cost-of-labor but different methodology          │
│  └── Examples: Buffer (public salary formula)                    │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

EXAMPLE ADJUSTMENT:
SF Bay Area role: $200,000 base
Same role in Austin: $160,000-$180,000 (80-90%)
Same role in Boise: $140,000-$160,000 (70-80%)
```

### Remote Work Career Considerations

Remote work arrangements affect compensation benchmarking, tax obligations, communication overhead, and long-term career visibility in ways that are easy to underestimate from outside a role. The following framework helps candidates evaluate remote-friendly offers by clarifying the company's actual policy on async communication, travel expectations, and location-based compensation adjustments.

```
CAREER GROWTH WHILE REMOTE:

CHALLENGES:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ├── Reduced visibility to leadership                            │
│  ├── Fewer informal networking opportunities                     │
│  ├── May miss impromptu conversations                            │
│  ├── Building relationships takes more effort                    │
│  ├── Some managers biased toward in-office                       │
│  └── May be passed over for certain opportunities                │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

STRATEGIES FOR SUCCESS:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  VISIBILITY:                                                     │
│  ├── Over-communicate your work and impact                       │
│  ├── Document everything (creates paper trail)                   │
│  ├── Share progress proactively                                  │
│  └── Participate actively in meetings (camera on)                │
│                                                                  │
│  RELATIONSHIPS:                                                  │
│  ├── Schedule regular 1:1s with manager                          │
│  ├── Virtual coffee chats with colleagues                        │
│  ├── Attend team offsites when possible                          │
│  └── Build relationships across teams                            │
│                                                                  │
│  PRESENCE:                                                       │
│  ├── Be responsive during working hours                          │
│  ├── Overlap with team's core hours                              │
│  ├── Participate in company events                               │
│  └── Consider occasional office visits                           │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

QUESTIONS TO ASK ABOUT REMOTE ROLES:
- What percentage of the team is remote?
- How does performance evaluation work for remote workers?
- What's the promotion track record for remote employees?
- How often do teams meet in person?
- Is my manager remote or in-office?
```

### Home Office Setup

A home office you can work in for years is an investment in your health and your on-camera presence, and employers increasingly provide a stipend for it. The checklist below covers the equipment that matters most.
```
PROFESSIONAL HOME OFFICE:

ESSENTIAL EQUIPMENT:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  COMPUTING:                                                      │
│  ├── External monitor (24"+ recommended)                         │
│  ├── Keyboard (ergonomic if possible)                            │
│  ├── Mouse/trackpad                                              │
│  └── Laptop stand (if using laptop screen)                       │
│                                                                  │
│  VIDEO/AUDIO:                                                    │
│  ├── Quality webcam (built-in often insufficient)                │
│  ├── Good microphone or headset                                  │
│  ├── Ring light or good lighting                                 │
│  └── Clean background (or virtual background)                    │
│                                                                  │
│  FURNITURE:                                                      │
│  ├── Ergonomic chair (invest here)                               │
│  ├── Desk at proper height                                       │
│  └── Standing desk (optional but nice)                           │
│                                                                  │
│  ENVIRONMENT:                                                    │
│  ├── Dedicated workspace (not bedroom if possible)               │
│  ├── Good internet (redundancy if critical)                      │
│  └── Quiet space for calls                                       │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

TYPICAL EMPLOYER SUPPORT:
- One-time equipment stipend ($500-2000)
- Monthly internet/utilities stipend ($50-150)
- Co-working space allowance
- Company equipment provided
```

### Junior → FAANG: Remote Work at Early Career and the Learning Cost

Early-career engineers who work fully remotely from day one at a new company face a steeper ramp than equivalent engineers who have regular in-person interaction with their team. This is not a reason to avoid remote work, but it is a reason to invest significantly more effort in deliberate relationship-building and active communication when working remotely early in your career.

The specific challenge: junior engineers learn by osmosis as much as by explicit instruction. Watching how senior engineers approach a problem, overhearing design discussions, and absorbing team culture through physical proximity are all learning mechanisms that remote work eliminates. You need to replace these mechanisms with intentional practices: more frequent pairing sessions, more explicit requests for feedback, and more active participation in design discussions where you would otherwise be quiet.

The remote work home office investment is not cosmetic: it is professional infrastructure. A stable internet connection (wired if possible), high-quality audio, and adequate lighting are the minimum required for professional remote work. Asking your manager for equipment reimbursement for home office setup is legitimate and increasingly standard at FAANG companies.

### Senior @ FAANG: Remote Governance at Senior Level and the Influence Architecture

At senior level, the remote work decision is an influence architecture decision as much as a lifestyle decision. Senior engineers who are remote from their primary collaborators need to be more deliberate about maintaining influence across organizational boundaries than co-located counterparts.

The specific practices that sustain senior remote influence: regular video calls with key stakeholders (not just scheduled team meetings but bilateral one-on-ones with the people whose collaboration you need), high-quality asynchronous communication through well-written design documents and RFC proposals, and periodic in-person visits for high-stakes alignment conversations. The cadence of in-person visits depends on the organization and the role, but for staff-level engineers driving significant technical change, monthly or quarterly visits to the hub location are typically required to maintain the organizational influence needed to do the work effectively.

The governance consideration: if your company has a policy that requires office presence for senior or staff-level roles, understand the policy clearly before accepting the role. Some FAANG companies have informal expectations about office presence for staff engineers that are not stated in the job description but become relevant during the performance review process.
