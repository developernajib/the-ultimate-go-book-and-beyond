### Tax Implications of International Moves

International moves create complex tax situations. US citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live. Tax treaties, foreign tax credits, and the foreign earned income exclusion can reduce double taxation, but the rules are intricate. Consult an international tax advisor before committing to a move.


### Junior → FAANG: International Relocation Fundamentals for Early-Career Engineers

International relocation for a first FAANG role is becoming increasingly common as companies hire globally. The most important planning dimension is timeline: international visa processing can take weeks to months, and some countries require specific documentation from your employer months before your start date. Start the visa process immediately after signing your offer letter, before notifying anyone else.

The banking and financial setup challenges of international relocation are underestimated. Opening a bank account, establishing credit history, and setting up the financial infrastructure needed for housing and daily life in a new country can take weeks without proper preparation. Research the specific requirements for your destination country before your arrival and have a plan for the transition period.

For Go engineers specifically: if you are relocating to work on a Go-heavy team at a FAANG company, your language-specific skills travel with you and are not affected by the relocation. The adaptation challenge is organizational culture and professional norms, not technical skills.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior International Relocation and the Tax Architecture

At senior level, international relocation triggers tax complexity that requires specialist advice before the move. US citizens and permanent residents are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live, which creates potential double-taxation on foreign-sourced income that is mitigated (but not eliminated) by foreign tax credits and tax treaties. Understanding how your RSU vesting, signing bonus, and base salary will be taxed in both the US and your destination country requires a cross-border tax advisor before you accept the assignment.

The equity treatment issue is particularly complex for international moves: RSUs that vest while you are a tax resident of a foreign country may be subject to different withholding requirements than RSUs that vest in the US. Some countries impose additional tax on equity compensation at exercise or sale beyond the withholding at vest. Getting this wrong can result in significant unexpected tax liability.

The governance principle for senior international relocations: budget for specialist legal and tax advice as part of your relocation negotiation. A cross-border employment attorney and an international tax advisor should be retained before you sign anything, and their fees should be part of the relocation package you negotiate.
