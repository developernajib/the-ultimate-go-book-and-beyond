## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Understand and calculate total compensation packages
- Research market rates effectively using multiple data sources
- Apply negotiation psychology and tactics confidently
- Write effective negotiation emails and scripts
- Handle special negotiation situations
- Make informed decisions between competing offers

### Junior → FAANG: Translating Objectives Into Negotiation Confidence

The objectives in this list represent the gap between the average engineer's negotiation approach (accept the offer with mild gratitude) and the informed engineer's approach (evaluate the offer against market data, prepare a counter, and communicate it professionally). Each objective here is a specific skill that can be learned and practiced before you need it.

The highest-leverage objective for early career: researching market rates effectively. If you know the market range for your specific role, level, and location before the offer arrives, every other part of the negotiation is easier. You are not guessing at a number. You are presenting a position that is anchored in public data, which is much more defensible and much less anxiety-inducing than an arbitrary higher number.

### Senior @ FAANG: Advanced Objectives for Senior Negotiation Mastery

At senior level, the objectives in this list describe entry-level negotiation competencies. The advanced objectives for senior-level negotiation include: understanding equity strike price and waterfall mechanics well enough to evaluate startup equity accurately, knowing when a signing bonus is a negotiating tactic by the employer (used to reduce base salary, which affects equity refresh calculations), and understanding the tax implications of different compensation structures.

The objective "make informed decisions between competing offers" at senior level translates into a rigorous decision framework: a weighted scorecard that includes total compensation over four years, equity downside risk, role scope and organizational mandate, team quality, technical challenge, and career optionality. Applying this framework to every offer evaluation prevents the common senior failure of accepting a lower-compensation offer from a more prestigious company without explicitly accounting for the financial tradeoff.
