## 96B.3 Researching Market Rates

### Data Sources and How to Use Them

Negotiating without market data is guessing. Multiple compensation databases exist for software engineers, each with different strengths and coverage. Cross-reference at least two or three sources to build an accurate picture of what your target role pays at your target companies.


### Location-Based Adjustments

Compensation varies significantly by geography. A Senior Engineer offer in San Francisco may be 40-60% higher than the same role in a lower cost-of-living area. Understanding these differentials helps you evaluate offers across locations on an apples-to-apples basis.

```
GEOGRAPHIC PAY DIFFERENTIALS (Approximate):

San Francisco Bay Area:    100% (baseline)
Seattle:                   95-100%
New York City:             95-100%
Los Angeles:               85-90%
Boston:                    85-90%
Austin:                    80-85%
Denver/Boulder:            80-85%
Chicago:                   75-80%
Atlanta:                   70-75%
Remote (US):               75-90% (varies by company)

EXAMPLE:
Bay Area L5 Engineer: $400,000 TC
Same role in Austin: $320,000-$340,000 TC
Same role Remote: $300,000-$360,000 TC (policy dependent)

NOTE: Some companies (GitLab, Airbnb) have transparent
location-based pay calculators. Research company policy.
```

### Junior → FAANG: Building Market Rate Fluency Before You Need It

The engineers who negotiate best are the ones who already know the market before they enter a process, not the ones who scramble to research after receiving an offer. Build a habit of checking levels.fyi quarterly for your role, level, and location, even when you are not actively searching. This background awareness means that when an offer arrives, you already know the range and can evaluate it immediately without having to do emergency research under a 48-hour deadline.

The most important calibration: levels.fyi data is reported by self-selected engineers at FAANG companies. It overrepresents strong performers at top-tier companies and underrepresents engineers at smaller companies and in non-FAANG markets. If you are not targeting a major FAANG company, supplement with Glassdoor and direct conversations with engineers in your network who have recently accepted offers at comparable companies.

For Go engineers: search specifically for Go-specific roles (site reliability engineer, infrastructure engineer, platform engineer) rather than generic "software engineer" because the compensation ranges differ. Platform and infrastructure roles at FAANG companies sometimes carry a 10 to 20 percent premium over standard software engineering roles at the same level, reflecting the market scarcity of engineers with strong systems programming backgrounds.

### Senior @ FAANG: Market Intelligence as a Continuous Senior Career Practice

At senior level, market rate research is not just pre-negotiation preparation: it is an ongoing competitive intelligence practice that informs career decisions throughout the year. Knowing that the market for L6 infrastructure engineers at top-tier companies has moved significantly in the past two years is relevant information whether or not you are actively searching, because it tells you whether your current compensation is tracking the market or falling behind.

The senior-level market research practice includes conversations with recruiters even when not actively searching. Brief, honest conversations with senior technical recruiters at companies you might eventually target build the recruiter relationship for when you do search and also provide real-time market intelligence about compensation ranges and demand for specific skill profiles. Senior engineers who have maintained recruiter relationships over five or more years consistently report receiving better initial offers than peers who begin these relationships only when actively searching.

Governance consideration: when using market rate data in negotiations, cite specific sources. "Based on levels.fyi data for this role and level in this location, the market range is X to Y" is a more compelling negotiating position than "I think I'm worth more." Data-anchored negotiation removes the interpersonal tension from the conversation and frames the discussion as a market alignment exercise rather than an adversarial one.
