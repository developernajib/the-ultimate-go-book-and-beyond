## 96B.6 Negotiation Scripts

### The "Thank You for the Offer" Response

Scripts matter because negotiation happens under pressure, and prepared language keeps you from improvising into a mistake. Start with the acknowledgment that buys you time:

```
EMAIL TEMPLATE: INITIAL RESPONSE

Subject: Re: Offer - [Position] at [Company]

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you so much for the offer! I'm really excited about
the opportunity to join [Company] as a [Position].

I'd like to take a few days to review the details carefully.
Could you please send me the complete offer in writing,
including all compensation components and benefits?

Also, what is the deadline for making a decision?

Thanks again, and I look forward to discussing further.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Counter Offer Templates

Having a ready-made template prevents you from improvising under pressure. Adapt these scripts to your situation, but keep the core structure: express enthusiasm, present your data-backed counter, and leave room for the recruiter to respond.

```
TEMPLATE 1: WITH COMPETING OFFER

Subject: Re: Offer Discussion - [Position]

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for your patience as I reviewed the offer details.

I want to be transparent with you - I've received a competing
offer from [Company/another company] with a total compensation
of $X. While [Your Company] remains my top choice due to
[specific reason], I'm hoping we can close the gap.

Would it be possible to increase the offer to:
- Base: $X
- Sign-on: $X
- Equity: $X over 4 years

I'm very motivated to join and confident we can make this work.

Best,
[Your Name]

---

TEMPLATE 2: WITHOUT COMPETING OFFER

Subject: Re: Offer Discussion - [Position]

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you again for the offer to join [Company]. After careful
consideration and research, I'd like to discuss the compensation.

Based on market data from levels.fyi and conversations with
peers in similar roles, I believe a total compensation in the
range of $X-$Y would better reflect the market rate for this
position and my experience level.

Would there be flexibility to adjust the package? I'm
particularly interested in discussing:
- [Component 1]
- [Component 2]

I'm excited about this opportunity and hope we can find
an arrangement that works for both of us.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Timeline Extension Request

Recruiters sometimes set tight decision deadlines to pressure you into accepting before you have competing offers. Requesting a reasonable extension is normal and expected. Frame the request as wanting to make a fully committed decision rather than a rushed one.

```
Subject: Timeline Extension Request

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for the offer and for setting the deadline of [date].

I'm in final stages with [another company / completing my
evaluation], and I want to make a fully informed decision.
Would it be possible to extend the deadline to [new date]?

I want to be respectful of your timeline while also ensuring
I can give [Company] the consideration it deserves.

Please let me know if this is possible.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

### Acceptance Email

Once you have decided to accept, send a formal written acceptance promptly. Confirm the key terms in your acceptance email so there is no ambiguity about what you agreed to. This protects both you and the company.

```
Subject: Offer Acceptance - [Position]

Hi [Recruiter Name],

I'm thrilled to accept the offer to join [Company] as a
[Position]!

To confirm, my understanding of the offer is:
- Base Salary: $X
- Sign-on Bonus: $X
- Annual Bonus Target: X%
- RSU Grant: $X over 4 years
- Start Date: [Date]

Please send over any paperwork needed to formalize the offer.
I'm looking forward to completing the background check and
any other onboarding requirements.

Thank you for working with me throughout this process.
I'm excited to join the team!

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Graceful Decline

Declining an offer professionally preserves the relationship for the future. You may want to work at that company later, or the recruiter may move to a different company you are interested in. A brief, gracious decline costs nothing and keeps doors open.

```
Subject: Thank You - [Position] Decision

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you so much for the offer to join [Company] and for
your time throughout the interview process.

After careful consideration, I've decided to pursue another
opportunity that better aligns with my current career goals.

This was a difficult decision, and I was genuinely impressed
by [specific positive aspect - team, mission, technology].
I hope our paths cross again in the future.

Thank you again for the opportunity.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

---

Note: Keep it positive and brief. You may want to
return to this company later.
```

### Junior → FAANG: Using Scripts as a Foundation, Not a Script

The scripts in this section are effective not because they are magic phrases but because they have the right structure: they express genuine enthusiasm, they state a specific number anchored to a specific reason, and they frame the request as a market alignment exercise rather than a personal demand. Use them as a structure template and customize the specific details to your situation.

The most important script to practice is the initial counter. Most early-career engineers either accept immediately, which leaves money on the table, or produce a rambling counter that sounds unconfident and reduces their leverage. The written email format is better than a phone call for the counter at early career because it gives you time to craft the language precisely and eliminates real-time anxiety.

Read your email counter aloud before sending it. If it sounds apologetic, revise it. If it sounds aggressive, revise it. The tone you are targeting is professional confidence: "I'm excited about this role and I want to make this work. Based on my research, I was expecting something closer to X. Is there flexibility to get there?"

### Senior @ FAANG: Script Adaptation for Senior Negotiation Contexts

At senior level, scripts need to be adapted for the specific context and relationship you have with the recruiter. If you have been through multiple rounds and have established a collegial relationship, an overly formal script will feel incongruent. If you are in early stages and do not know the recruiter well, a structured professional script is more appropriate.

The senior-level adaptation that matters most: when you have a competing offer, the script should name the company, the level, and the total compensation specifically. "I have a competing offer from Y at the L6/E6 level with $X total compensation over four years" is much more compelling than "I have a competing offer that is more competitive." Specificity is credibility in negotiation. Vague competing offers are discounted by experienced recruiters.

For staff-level negotiations that include non-compensation elements: the script should address scope expectations explicitly. "I'm excited about the technical direction, and I want to confirm that the role will include [specific mandate or scope element] before I make my final decision." This is a professional request that is entirely appropriate and that signals you are evaluating the role carefully, not just the compensation.
