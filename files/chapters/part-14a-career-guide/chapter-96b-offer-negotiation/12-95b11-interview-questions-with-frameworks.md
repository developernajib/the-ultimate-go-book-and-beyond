## 96B.11 Interview Questions with Frameworks

### Question 1: "What are your salary expectations?"

**Interview Question:** Early in the process, the recruiter asks "What are your salary expectations?"

**Framework for Answering:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              SALARY EXPECTATIONS DEFLECTION FRAMEWORK                   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                         │
│  GOAL: Get them to reveal their range first without being evasive       │
│                                                                         │
│  STEP 1: ACKNOWLEDGE THE QUESTION                                       │
│  ─────────────────────────────────                                      │
│  Show you heard them and are engaging, not deflecting rudely            │
│                                                                         │
│  STEP 2: REDIRECT TO THEIR BUDGET                                       │
│  ───────────────────────────────────                                    │
│  Turn it around professionally - you need their info too                │
│                                                                         │
│  STEP 3: EXPRESS FLEXIBILITY                                            │
│  ───────────────────────────────                                        │
│  Show you're reasonable, not rigid                                      │
│                                                                         │
│  STEP 4: IF PRESSED, GIVE A RESEARCHED RANGE                            │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────────────                          │
│  Never give a single number; always a range from market data            │
│                                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Complete Answer Scripts:**

```markdown

## First Deflection (Preferred)
"That's a great question. I'm flexible on compensation and more focused
on finding the right fit. I'd love to better understand the role first
and then discuss compensation. What's the salary range you've budgeted
for this position?"

## Second Deflection (If Pressed)
"I want to make sure we're aligned. I'm interested in total compensation,
not just base salary. Could you share the full compensation structure - base,
equity, bonus - so I can give you a meaningful answer?"

## If Forced to Answer
"Based on my research of market rates for [Senior/Staff] Engineers in
[location] at companies like [similar companies], I understand the range
is typically $X-$Y for total compensation. I'm comfortable somewhere in
that range depending on the full package and growth opportunity."

## In States Where It's Illegal to Ask Current Salary
"I don't believe you're able to ask about my current salary in [California/
New York/etc.]. I'm happy to discuss your salary range for this position."

## Key Principles:
1. Never give a single number (always a range)
2. Research market rates BEFORE the call (levels.fyi)
3. Frame around total comp, not base
4. Express flexibility while anchoring high
5. In illegal states, know your rights
```

### Question 2: "We've extended an offer. Do you have any questions?"

**Interview Question:** You've received an offer over the phone. The recruiter asks if you have questions.

**Framework for Answering:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  INITIAL OFFER RESPONSE FRAMEWORK                       │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                         │
│  TIMING: This is NOT when you negotiate                                 │
│  PURPOSE: Gather information, express enthusiasm, buy time              │
│                                                                         │
│  1. EXPRESS GENUINE ENTHUSIASM (10 seconds)                             │
│     "Thank you so much! I'm really excited about this opportunity."     │
│                                                                         │
│  2. REQUEST TIME (30 seconds)                                           │
│     "I'd like to take some time to review the full details."            │
│                                                                         │
│  3. ASK CLARIFYING QUESTIONS                                            │
│     Get all details you need for comparison                             │
│                                                                         │
│  4. CONFIRM NEXT STEPS                                                  │
│     "When do you need a decision by?"                                   │
│                                                                         │
│  DO NOT:                                                                │
│  ✗ Accept or reject on the spot                                         │
│  ✗ Start negotiating immediately                                        │
│  ✗ Show disappointment at numbers                                       │
│  ✗ Reveal competing offers unprompted                                   │
│                                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Complete Answer Script:**

```markdown

## The Perfect Initial Response

"Thank you so much for the offer! I'm really excited about the opportunity
to join [Company] and work with the team I met.

I'd like to take a few days to review all the details carefully. Could you
help me understand a few things?

**Questions to Ask:**

1. EQUITY DETAILS
   "Can you walk me through the equity structure? Is it RSUs or options?
   What's the vesting schedule?"

2. TOTAL COMPENSATION
   "What would the total Year 1 compensation look like, including bonus
   targets and any sign-on?"

3. LEVEL AND BAND
   "What level is this offer for? Where does it fall within the band
   for this level?"

4. BENEFITS HIGHLIGHTS
   "Are there any notable benefits I should factor into my decision?"

5. TIMING
   "When do you need a final decision? I want to give this the
   consideration it deserves."

6. NEXT STEPS
   "Can you send me the full offer details in writing so I can
   review everything carefully?"

Then: Thank them, end the call, and prepare your counter.
```

### Question 3: "Why do you feel you deserve more than we've offered?"

**Interview Question:** During negotiation, the recruiter asks you to justify your counter.

**Framework for Answering:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              COUNTER OFFER JUSTIFICATION FRAMEWORK                      │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                         │
│  STRUCTURE: Market Data → Your Value → Collaborative Close              │
│                                                                         │
│  1. LEAD WITH MARKET DATA (Not Entitlement)                             │
│     "Based on my research of market rates..."                           │
│     - Cite specific sources (levels.fyi, Blind)                         │
│     - Reference comparable roles and companies                          │
│     - Shows professionalism, not greed                                  │
│                                                                         │
│  2. HIGHLIGHT YOUR UNIQUE VALUE                                         │
│     "Given my experience with..."                                       │
│     - Specific skills relevant to this role                             │
│     - Past achievements with quantified impact                          │
│     - Rare expertise they specifically need                             │
│                                                                         │
│  3. MENTION COMPETING OFFERS (If You Have Them)                         │
│     "I'm also considering an opportunity at..."                         │
│     - Only if true and you'd actually take it                           │
│     - Be specific enough to be credible                                 │
│     - Don't use as a threat                                             │
│                                                                         │
│  4. COLLABORATIVE CLOSE                                                 │
│     "I'm confident we can find something that works."                   │
│     - You're solving a problem together                                 │
│     - You WANT to join them                                             │
│                                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Complete Answer:**

```markdown

## Sample Justification

"Great question. A few things inform my ask:

**Market Data:**
I've researched compensation for Senior Engineers in [location] extensively.
Based on levels.fyi data and conversations with peers in similar roles at
[peer companies], the market range is $X-$Y. My ask is within that range.

**My Value:**
I bring [X years] of experience specifically in [skill relevant to their
problem]. At [previous company], I [specific achievement with numbers - 
led project that saved $X, improved performance by Y%]. This directly
applies to the [specific initiative] your team mentioned.

**Competing Opportunity:**
I should mention I'm also considering an offer from [Company] at $X.
[Your Company] is genuinely my top choice because [specific reason - 
team, mission, technology]. I'm hoping we can get closer to align.

**Closing:**
I'm really excited about this role and believe I can add significant
value quickly. Is there flexibility to get to $X?"

## Then: STOP TALKING. Let them respond.

The most common negotiation mistake is filling the silence after making a counter-offer. Once you state your number, any additional words you add create pressure on yourself: you start justifying, softening, or pre-emptively conceding. The recruiter's job at that moment is to wait you out; silence is psychologically uncomfortable, and the first person to speak after a counter is almost always the one who makes the next concession. Deliver the counter clearly, then stop.

```

### Question 4: "This is our best and final offer."

**Interview Question:** The recruiter says the offer is final. How do you respond?

**Framework for Answering:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│              "FINAL OFFER" RESPONSE FRAMEWORK                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                         │
│  REALITY CHECK:                                                         │
│  - "Best and final" is often a negotiation tactic                       │
│  - It's rarely truly final for strong candidates                        │
│  - But sometimes it IS final - know how to read the situation           │
│                                                                         │
│  RESPONSE OPTIONS:                                                      │
│                                                                         │
│  IF YOU HAVE LEVERAGE (Competing offers, rare skills):                  │
│  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────                  │
│  "I appreciate that. I'm in a tough position because [Company] has      │
│  offered $X. I genuinely prefer [your company] but I have to weigh      │
│  the numbers. Is there anything creative we can do?"                    │
│                                                                         │
│  IF YOU'RE WILLING TO WALK AWAY:                                        │
│  ───────────────────────────────                                        │
│  "I understand. I'll need to think about whether this works for me.     │
│  Can I have until [date] to decide?"                                    │
│                                                                         │
│  IF THIS IS YOUR ONLY/BEST OPTION:                                      │
│  ─────────────────────────────────                                      │
│  "I appreciate you working with me. Before I accept, could we           │
│  explore [non-salary items: start date, remote work, title]?"           │
│                                                                         │
│  KEY INSIGHT:                                                           │
│  If they say "final" and you have real leverage, a polite pause         │
│  often reopens the conversation. Silence is powerful.                   │
│                                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Complete Response Options:**

```markdown

## Option 1: The Pause (Most Effective)
"I see. Let me think about this and get back to you tomorrow."

Then: Wait. Often they'll come back with more room.

## Option 2: Explore Alternatives
"I understand the base and equity may be fixed. Are there other
components we could adjust? I'm thinking:
- A higher sign-on bonus to bridge the gap
- Additional RSUs that vest in Year 1
- A 6-month performance review with opportunity for adjustment
- A one-time retention bonus"

## Option 3: Ask Why
"Help me understand - what's preventing movement on this? Is it the
level, the budget, internal equity? Sometimes understanding the
constraint reveals a solution."

## Option 4: If Truly Final
"I appreciate your transparency. I'm going to need a day to make
this decision. Can you confirm everything in writing? I want to
make sure I'm comparing the full picture."

## Red Flags It's Actually Final:
- Multiple rounds of negotiation already
- They've matched competing offers once
- HR has explicitly explained band limits
- Manager says hands are tied

## Signs There May Be Room:
- First time hearing "final"
- They haven't asked about competing offers
- The recruiter sounds uncomfortable saying it
- Fast response suggests automated pushback
```

### Junior → FAANG: Answering Compensation Questions Without Anchoring Yourself Low

The salary expectation question is one of the most important questions in the interview process and one of the most commonly mishandled by early-career candidates. The standard advice is to deflect or provide a range rather than a specific number, because the first number stated in a negotiation anchors the conversation. If you anchor too low, you have limited the ceiling of the final offer regardless of how well the rest of the process goes.

The specific deflection that works best at early career: "I'm focusing on finding the right role and team first. That said, I've done my research on the market for this level and location and I expect to be compensated competitively. What is the band for this role?" Turning the question back to the company often produces useful information about the band without requiring you to anchor first.

If pressed for a number, provide a range with the bottom of your range at or slightly above the market median you researched. "Based on my research on levels.fyi for this role at this company and location, I'm expecting somewhere in the range of X to Y" is a professional, data-anchored response that does not anchor you below market.

### Senior @ FAANG: Compensation Questions as Strategic Positioning Moments

At senior level, the salary expectation question is an opportunity to demonstrate your market awareness and your negotiating sophistication simultaneously. Providing a specific, data-anchored number with explicit reasoning is more effective than deflection because it signals that you have done the research, you know your market value, and you are in a position to have a substantive conversation rather than a guessing game.

The senior-level answer framework: "I've been tracking compensation data for L6/E6 roles in my domain and location on levels.fyi. For this role and level, the market range is X to Y total compensation over four years. Given my specific background in [domain], I'm targeting the upper half of that range. Does that align with what you're able to offer?" This answer is specific, evidence-based, and invites the recruiter to engage substantively rather than deflecting.

Bar-raiser level signal: a senior candidate who cannot answer compensation questions with market-anchored specificity has either not done the research or is uncomfortable with the negotiation process. Both signals are visible and neither is positive. Prepare this answer with the same rigor you prepare technical interview answers.
