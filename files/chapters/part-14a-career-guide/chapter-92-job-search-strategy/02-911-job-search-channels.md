## 92.1 Job Search Channels

Not all job search channels produce the same results. Understanding which channels have the highest conversion rates helps you allocate your limited time and energy where they will have the greatest impact. The data consistently shows that personal connections and referrals dramatically outperform cold applications, yet most engineers spend the majority of their time on the lowest-yield channels.

### The Effectiveness Hierarchy

The following hierarchy ranks job search channels by their typical interview-to-offer conversion rates. These percentages are approximate and based on aggregated industry data, but the relative ordering is consistent across company sizes and markets. As a beginner job seeker, focus your efforts on the top of this hierarchy first, and use the lower channels as supplementary rather than primary strategies.

├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                  │

│     ├── Referred by current employee                             │
│     ├── Fastest path through the funnel                          │
│     ├── Resume actually gets read                                │
│     └── Often have insider advocate                              │
│                                                                  │

### Time Allocation Strategy

How you divide your daily job search hours matters as much as how many hours you put in. Many beginners make the mistake of spending all their time browsing job boards and clicking "apply," when the highest-return activity is networking and seeking referrals. The split below reflects what experienced job seekers have found produces the best results over a multi-week search.

### Junior → FAANG: Channel Selection for Maximum Conversion

The channel conversion data in this section should immediately redirect where you spend your job search time. If you are spending four hours per day on job board applications and thirty minutes per week on network building, you are optimizing in the wrong direction. The conversion rate differential between referrals (20-40% phone screen rate) and cold applications (2-5%) is large enough that rebalancing toward network-first activity is almost always the most impactful change an early-career candidate can make.

For Go engineers specifically: the Go community has concentrated, accessible venues for relationship building. The Gopher Slack, the Go GitHub repository, GopherCon, and regional Go meetups are all spaces where engineers from FAANG companies participate alongside everyone else. A thoughtful comment in a GitHub issue discussion, a well-received question in a Slack channel, or a brief talk at a local meetup creates visibility in exactly the community where hiring managers are looking.

The job board channel is still worth using, but treat it as a supplementary channel rather than a primary one. Apply only to roles where you can customize your resume to the specific job description, and do not spend more than 20% of your total job search time on it.

### Senior @ FAANG: Channel Strategy at Senior Level

At L5 and above, the channel hierarchy inverts further. The most productive senior job search channels, in roughly descending order of yield: direct recruiter outreach from FAANG technical sourcers (which requires your LinkedIn and public profile to be discoverable and compelling), warm introductions from trusted peers to hiring managers, referrals from engineers at target companies you have worked with professionally, and conference networking with engineering leaders at target companies. Cold applications through career pages are at the bottom of this list for senior candidates.

The passive inbound channel becomes significant at senior level: a well-maintained public profile with recent technical writing, open-source contributions, and conference talks will generate consistent recruiter outreach from FAANG companies without any active effort. This is the long-term payoff of the brand investment described in Chapter 91. Senior engineers who have built this inbound channel report spending far less time on active job searching because they have more unsolicited conversations than they can pursue at any given time.

Platform thinking for senior job search: your goal is to be in a position where you are choosing between opportunities, not competing for them. This requires a two to three year investment in the brand and network infrastructure described in this chapter and Chapter 91. Engineers who have made this investment consistently report that their senior job searches take three to four weeks from "I've decided to explore" to "I have four competing offers." Engineers who have not made this investment report three to five months for the same outcome.
