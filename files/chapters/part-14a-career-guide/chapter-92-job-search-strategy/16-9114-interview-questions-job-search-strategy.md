## 92.15 Interview Questions: Job Search Strategy

These are the most common non-technical questions you will face during the early stages of the interview process. They seem simple, but your answers reveal a lot about your professionalism, self-awareness, and whether you are a thoughtful candidate or someone who is just spraying applications. Preparing clear, honest answers to these questions ahead of time prevents you from rambling or saying something you will regret.

### Question 1: "Why are you looking to leave your current role?"

**What They're Evaluating:**
- Are you running away or running toward?
- Will the same issues arise here?
- Are you being honest?
- Is your reason reasonable?

**Strong Answer Framework:**
```markdown

## The Formula:

1. Brief neutral statement about current role
2. What you're looking FOR (not running from)
3. Connect to this specific opportunity
4. Positive tone throughout

## Example Answers:

### Growth-Focused:
"I've learned a lot at [Current Co] over 3 years, but
I've grown into a role where I want more ownership
over architecture decisions. Your team's work on
[specific challenge] is exactly the scale of problem
I'm excited to tackle."

### Opportunity-Focused:
"My current company is great, but they've shifted
focus away from [domain]. I'm passionate about
[domain] and [Company] is doing some of the most
interesting work in that space."

### Challenge-Focused:
"I've built strong fundamentals at [Current Co], and
now I'm ready for a larger scale challenge. The
opportunity to work with systems serving [X users]
is what drew me to this role."

## Red Flags in Answers:
- Badmouthing current employer
- Vague or evasive
- "It's complicated"
- Listing grievances
```

### Question 2: "What are you looking for in your next role?"

**What They're Evaluating:**
- Do you know what you want?
- Are we a good fit?
- Are your expectations realistic?
- What motivates you?

**Strong Answer Framework:**
```markdown

## Structure:

1. Primary driver (growth, challenge, domain)
2. Secondary factors (team, culture, compensation)
3. How this role aligns
4. What you bring

## Example Answer:

"Three things are most important to me:

First, I want to work on technical challenges at
scale. I've loved building systems that handle
millions of requests, and I want to push that
further.

Second, I'm looking for a team that values both
craft and speed. I've seen the extremes - moving
too fast with debt, or over-engineering everything.
The balance matters to me.

Third, I want to continue growing toward a staff
engineering track. That means I'm looking for
mentorship and opportunities to lead technical
initiatives.

From what I've learned about [Company], particularly
[specific thing], this seems like a strong fit for
all three."

## Show Self-Awareness:
- Know what you want and don't want
- Be specific, not generic
- Have thought about it
```

### Question 3: "What's your timeline for making a decision?"

**What They're Evaluating:**
- How serious are you?
- Do we need to move fast?
- Are you playing companies against each other?
- Are you honest about your situation?

**Strong Answer Framework:**
```markdown

## Be Honest About Your Situation:

### If You Have Other Offers:
"I have an offer from [Company] with a deadline of
[date]. I'm very interested in this opportunity,
so I wanted to be transparent about timing. Is it
possible to align with that timeline?"

### If You're Actively Interviewing:
"I'm in various stages with a few other companies.
I don't have any deadlines yet, but I expect to be
making a decision in the next 3-4 weeks. This role
is a top choice for me."

### If You're Just Starting:
"I'm early in my search. I'm not in a rush and want
to find the right fit. That said, I'm excited about
this opportunity and ready to move forward on your
timeline."

### If You Need to Move Fast:
"I'm being proactive because my team is being
reorganized in [timeframe]. I'm looking to have
something confirmed in the next [X weeks] if possible."

## Strategic Considerations:
- Honest > strategic (usually)
- Companies respect transparency
- Don't create fake urgency
- But real urgency is useful leverage
```

### Question 4: "Do you have any questions for me about the company/role?"

**What They're Evaluating:**
- Have you done your research?
- What do you care about?
- Are you genuinely interested?
- Can you have an intelligent conversation?

**Strong Questions:**
```markdown

## By Interview Stage:

### Recruiter Screen:
- "What makes this role high priority right now?"
- "What's the interview process like?"
- "What's the team structure?"
- "What's the compensation range?"

### Technical Interview:
- "What's a typical project someone in this role would own?"
- "How are technical decisions made on the team?"
- "What's the biggest technical challenge you're facing?"
- "What does code review look like here?"

### Hiring Manager:
- "What does success look like in 6 months?"
- "What's the growth path for someone in this role?"
- "How would you describe your management style?"
- "What's the team's biggest weakness right now?"

### Final Round:
- "What's your biggest concern about me as a candidate?"
- "How does the team's work connect to company strategy?"
- "What would you change about the team if you could?"
- "Why did you join [Company]?"

## Questions to Avoid:
- Easily Googled information
- Salary (save for negotiation)
- "How soon can I get promoted?"
- "What do you do here?" (should know)
```
