## Exercises

These exercises walk you through the practical mechanics of running a job search. Rather than just reading about strategies, you will practice building a network map, managing an application pipeline, and creating a company target list. Working through these exercises before you start your actual job search gives you the systems and templates you need to hit the ground running.

### Exercise 1: Network Mapping and Outreach

**Scenario:**
You want to work at Stripe, Airbnb, or Datadog. Map your network to find potential referrals and create outreach messages.

**Your Task:**
1. List 10 people who might connect you to these companies
2. Write a referral request for someone you know well
3. Write a cold outreach for someone you don't know
4. Create a follow-up sequence

### Exercise 3: Company Research and Targeting

**Scenario:**

1. Define your criteria

3. Build a tiered target list
4. Plan your approach for each tier

- Distributed systems work
- Remote-friendly or in my location
- Has open Senior+ roles
- Stable (not about to run out of money)

### Nice to Haves:
- Domain I care about (fintech, infra, dev tools)

## Summary

- **Targeted vs spray-and-pray job search**: A focused list of 15-20 well-researched companies outperforms blasting 200 generic applications - quality of fit and preparation depth drives offer rates far more than volume.
- **Warm introductions**: A referral from an internal employee dramatically increases resume visibility and pass-through rates at most companies. Invest time cultivating these before you need them.
- **LinkedIn profile optimization**: Your LinkedIn headline, About section, and experience bullets should speak to the role you want next, not just describe what you did - recruiters search for outcomes and keywords.
- **Resume as marketing document**: A resume is not a biography. Every line should communicate impact and relevance to the target role, using metrics and action verbs to demonstrate scope and results.
- **STAR stories for behavioral prep**: Structure behavioral answers with Situation, Task, Action, Result - prepare a bank of 8-10 stories that can flex across leadership, conflict, failure, and impact questions.
- **ATS optimization**: Many applications are filtered before human review. Mirror the job description's language in your resume to pass automated keyword screening without fabricating experience.
- **Timing the job search**: January-March and September-October are peak hiring seasons. Starting your search with this calendar in mind maximizes the number of open roles and available interviewers.
- **Managing multiple pipelines**: Track every application, contact, and stage in a spreadsheet or tool. Juggling 10-15 active conversations requires discipline to avoid missed follow-ups and scheduling conflicts.
- **Company research depth**: Go beyond the website - read engineering blogs, recent tech talks, Glassdoor reviews, and news. This depth surfaces in interviews and signals genuine interest to hiring teams.
- **Importance of post-interview follow-up**: A prompt, specific thank-you email reinforces your candidacy, demonstrates professionalism, and gives you one more opportunity to address anything left unsaid in the interview.
