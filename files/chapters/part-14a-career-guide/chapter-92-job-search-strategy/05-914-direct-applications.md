## 92.4 Direct Applications

While referrals are the highest-yield channel, direct applications are still an important part of a balanced job search strategy. The key difference between effective direct applications and wasted effort is targeting: applying to roles where you are a genuine match, at companies you have researched, through channels that maximize your visibility. This section covers where to find the best roles and how to make your direct applications stand out from the pile.

### Finding the Right Roles

Not all job boards are created equal. Tech-specific job boards tend to have better-quality listings and less competition than general platforms like Indeed. Company career pages are another underutilized resource, since applying directly shows genuine interest and avoids the crowded applicant pools on aggregator sites. Spread your efforts across multiple sources so you do not miss opportunities that are only posted in one place.

```
WHERE TO FIND JOBS:

TECH-SPECIFIC JOB BOARDS:
• levels.fyi/jobs (comp data included)
• Hacker News "Who's Hiring" (monthly)
• Golang Weekly jobs section
• Work at a Startup (YC companies)

GENERAL JOB BOARDS:
• LinkedIn Jobs
• Indeed
• Glassdoor
• Built In (city-specific)

COMPANY-SPECIFIC:
• Target company career pages
• Company engineering blogs (often link to roles)
• Twitter/X (companies post roles)

NICHE:
• Remote OK (remote roles)
• We Work Remotely
• RemoteOK
• Toptal (contract/freelance)
```

### Application Best Practices

Submitting a high volume of generic applications is one of the most common mistakes in a job search. A single well-crafted application that matches keywords from the job description and highlights relevant experience will outperform dozens of copy-paste submissions. Timing also matters: roles that have been posted for weeks already have hundreds of applicants, so applying early in the first few days gives you the best chance of being seen.

```
QUALITY OVER QUANTITY:
• Apply to roles you're genuinely qualified for
• Customize your resume for each application
• Research the company before applying
• Target 5-10 quality applications per week

CUSTOMIZATION:
• Match resume keywords to job description
• Reorder experience bullets for relevance
• Adjust summary/objective if you have one
• Highlight relevant projects

COVER LETTER (when required):
• Keep it short (3-4 paragraphs max)
• Explain why THIS company
• Connect your experience to their needs
• Show you've done research

APPLICATION TIMING:
• Apply early (roles get filled)
• Avoid Friday afternoons
• Monday-Tuesday mornings are best
• Apply within first week of posting
```

### Sample Cover Letter

A cover letter is your opportunity to explain why you are interested in this specific company and role, not just repeat what is on your resume. The best cover letters are short, specific, and demonstrate that you have done your homework. If the job posting does not require a cover letter, including a brief one can still help you stand out, especially at smaller companies where hiring managers read every application personally.

```
Dear Hiring Team,

I'm excited to apply for the Senior Go Engineer position
at [Company]. I've been following your engineering blog,
and [specific post] about your approach to [topic]
particularly resonated with my experience at [Current Co].

At [Current Co], I've been building distributed systems
that handle [scale]. Most recently, I led the redesign
of our [system], achieving [result]. This experience
aligns directly with the challenges you're tackling
around [specific challenge from job description].

I'm particularly drawn to [Company] because [specific
reason - mission, product, technical approach]. The
opportunity to work on [specific thing] while learning
from your team's expertise in [area] is exactly what
I'm looking for in my next role.

I'd welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience
with [relevant skills] could contribute to your team.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Junior → FAANG: Making Direct Applications Work When You Have No Network

For early-career candidates with a small professional network, direct applications are a larger part of the strategy than they will be later. The difference between effective and ineffective direct applications is the quality of the job description match and the tailoring of the materials.

Before applying to any role directly: read the job description carefully and identify the two or three requirements where your experience most closely matches. Rewrite your resume summary to reflect exactly those requirements, using the same language the job description uses. Write a three-sentence cover note if the application allows it, naming the specific match between the role's stated needs and your demonstrable experience. This takes 20-30 minutes per application and meaningfully increases callback rates compared to submitting the same materials to every role.

For Go roles specifically: look for job descriptions that mention Go as a primary language rather than "experience with Go preferred." Roles where Go is the primary language have a smaller qualified candidate pool and are more likely to advance Go-fluent candidates who meet the threshold requirements, even without a referral.

### Senior @ FAANG: Direct Applications as a Data Collection Tool

At senior level, direct applications serve a secondary purpose beyond generating interviews: they are a real-time market research tool. The job descriptions for senior roles at companies you are targeting tell you what technical problems those teams are currently trying to solve, what skills and experience they believe they need, and implicitly what gaps exist on the team. Reading ten senior job descriptions at your target companies over a single afternoon gives you a much richer picture of the market than any salary aggregator.

For senior candidates, a direct application that does not generate a response within two weeks is a signal worth investigating. If your profile is strong and the role appears to be a clear match, the absence of a response often means the role is already filled through an internal candidate, the headcount was frozen, or the ATS filtered your resume for a format or keyword reason. Reaching out directly to the engineering manager for the team (via LinkedIn, not the recruiter) with a brief, specific message about your background is appropriate and often effective at senior level.
