## 92.9 Job Search Execution Checklist & Mid-Chapter Synthesis

This section distills the most important lessons from the chapter into actionable takeaways and a practical checklist. Review these regularly during your job search to make sure you are staying on track and not falling into the common traps that derail otherwise strong candidates.

### Key Takeaways

1. **Referrals are the most effective channel** - Invest time in your network
2. **Quality over quantity in applications** - Customize for each role
3. **Build recruiter relationships** - They can be valuable allies
4. **Stay organized** - Track everything in a spreadsheet
5. **Manage your pipeline** - Keep multiple opportunities moving
6. **Protect your mental health** - Job searching is hard

### Job Search Checklist

Use this checklist as a starting point before you begin your active search. Completing all of these items before you send your first application ensures you are presenting yourself professionally and have the infrastructure in place to manage the process efficiently.

- [ ] Resume updated and tailored
- [ ] LinkedIn optimized
- [ ] Target company list created
- [ ] Network outreach started
- [ ] Application tracker set up
- [ ] Practice schedule established
- [ ] Support system in place

### Junior → FAANG: Weekly Review Ritual for the Active Job Search

Use this summary checklist as a weekly review tool during your search, not a one-time setup guide. At the end of each week, review each item and identify the one with the biggest gap. Spend the first 30 minutes of the following week closing that gap before doing anything else. This weekly review ritual prevents the common pattern where candidates do the easy tasks (submitting applications) while neglecting the hard but more effective tasks (network building, technical preparation depth, follow-up tracking).

The "support system in place" item is underrated. A job search is emotionally demanding in ways that most engineers do not anticipate. Identify one or two people in your life who you can debrief with after difficult interview experiences, who will hold you accountable to your weekly action plan, and who will celebrate the wins with you. This is not soft. It is a practical mechanism for maintaining the consistency that produces results over a multi-month process.

### Senior @ FAANG: The Summary as a Readiness Assessment

At senior level, use this checklist before you make first contact with any company. If more than two items are incomplete, you are not ready to start the active search phase. Wait two weeks, complete those items, and then begin. The opportunity cost of entering the market before you are ready is not just a wasted interview loop. It is a damaged relationship with a company you might want to target again in two years, and a missed opportunity to benchmark your performance against your actual calibrated preparation level.

The most important checklist item for senior candidates is the one not explicitly listed: level clarity. Before you apply to any role, you should be able to answer "what level would I expect to receive at [company X] based on my experience, and what evidence do I have for that assessment?" If you cannot answer this for your top three target companies, your first network conversations should be level calibration conversations with recently hired peers, not application submissions.
