## 92.10 Cold Outreach Strategies

Cold outreach means contacting someone you have no prior relationship with, typically to learn about a company or express interest in a role. While it has lower conversion rates than warm introductions, cold outreach can be highly effective when done with personalization and genuine curiosity. This is especially true for startups and smaller companies where formal recruiting processes are less established.

### When Cold Outreach Works

Cold outreach is not equally effective in all situations. It works best when you have something specific and genuine to say to the person you are contacting, such as a comment on their recent blog post or a shared technical interest. It works worst when your message is generic and could have been sent to anyone. Understanding when cold outreach is worth your time helps you avoid wasting effort on low-probability channels.

### The Perfect Cold Outreach Message

A great cold outreach message has four elements: a personalized hook that shows you are not spamming, a brief credibility statement, a specific and easy-to-fulfill ask, and an easy out so the person does not feel pressured. The templates below demonstrate this structure for different audiences. Keep your messages short. Long cold messages are rarely read in full.

```markdown
# Cold Outreach Template

## ELEMENTS OF EFFECTIVE COLD MESSAGES:

### 1. PERSONALIZED HOOK (First Line)
Why you're reaching out to THEM specifically:
- "I read your post about [topic] and..."
- "I saw you worked on [project] at [company]..."
- "We have [mutual connection/school/interest] in common..."
- "Your talk at [conference] about [topic] resonated..."

### 2. CREDIBILITY ESTABLISHMENT (1-2 sentences)
Who you are and why you're worth talking to:
- Current role and company
- Relevant accomplishment
- Shared background or expertise

### 3. SPECIFIC ASK (1 sentence)
Make it easy to say yes:
- "Would you have 15 minutes for a quick call?"
- "Could you point me to the right person?"
- "Would you be open to a virtual coffee?"

### 4. EASY OUT (Optional but appreciated)
- "No worries if you're too busy!"
- "Even a one-line reply would help!"

---

## EXAMPLE COLD MESSAGES:

### To a Hiring Manager:
"""
Subject: Senior Go Engineer interested in [Company]

Hi [Name],

I saw your recent post about scaling [Company]'s payment
infrastructure - the approach you took with event sourcing
is similar to what we built at [My Company], where I led
a team that processes $50M daily.

I'm exploring new opportunities and would love to learn
more about the engineering challenges you're facing. Would
you have 15 minutes for a quick call this week?

Either way, keep up the great content!

Best,
[Your Name]
"""

### To an Engineer at Target Company:
"""
Hi [Name],

I came across your GitHub repo [project-name] while
researching distributed systems approaches - really clean
implementation of the consensus protocol.

I'm a Senior Go Engineer at [Company] exploring
opportunities, and I'm particularly interested in [Company]'s
work on [specific area]. Would you be open to a quick chat
about your experience there?

No pressure if you're too busy - I'd understand!

Thanks,
[Your Name]
"""

### To a Startup Founder/CTO:
"""
Subject: Go engineer who's built [relevant thing]

Hi [Name],

Congrats on the recent [funding/launch/milestone]! I've
been following [Company] since [event], and the approach
you're taking to [problem] is exactly the kind of
challenge I'm looking for.

Background: I'm a Senior Engineer at [Company] where I
[specific accomplishment]. Before that, I [relevant
experience].

Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation about
engineering opportunities? I'd love to share some ideas
I have about [specific area].

Best,
[Your Name]
"""
```

### Cold Email Infrastructure

Beyond crafting a good message, the practical logistics of cold outreach matter significantly. Finding the right email address, timing your message for when it is most likely to be read, and following up at appropriate intervals all affect your response rate. Treat this like any other engineering problem: measure what works, iterate on your approach, and do not waste time on strategies that are not producing results.

```markdown
# Cold Outreach Best Practices

## FINDING EMAILS:

### Tools:
- Hunter.io (find patterns and verify)
- Clearbit Connect (Gmail plugin)
- LinkedIn (message directly)
- Company email patterns (firstname@company.com)
- Personal websites/blogs

### Email Patterns to Try:
- firstname@company.com
- firstname.lastname@company.com
- first.last@company.com
- flastname@company.com

## TIMING:

### Best Times:
- Tuesday-Thursday
- 9-11 AM in recipient's timezone
- Avoid: Monday morning, Friday afternoon

### Follow-Up Schedule:
- Day 0: Initial message
- Day 3-4: First follow-up (add value)
- Day 7-8: Second follow-up (final)
- After: Move on

## FOLLOW-UP TEMPLATE:

### First Follow-Up:
"""
Hi [Name],

Just floating this back to the top of your inbox. I came
across [new relevant thing] that reminded me of [Company]'s
work.

Would love to connect if you have time!
"""

### Final Follow-Up:
"""
Hi [Name],

I'll stop bothering you after this! If you know someone
else at [Company] who might be good to talk to, I'd
appreciate the introduction.

Either way, wishing you well with [current initiative].
"""

## TRACKING:

### Track These Metrics:
- Messages sent
- Response rate
- Conversations started
- Interviews from cold outreach

### Optimize Based on:
- Which subject lines work
- Which hooks get responses
- Which companies respond
- Which seniority levels engage
```

### LinkedIn Outreach Strategy

LinkedIn is the most common platform for professional cold outreach because it lets you see mutual connections, shared backgrounds, and the person's current role before you reach out. However, LinkedIn messages have strict character limits for connection requests, and many people receive dozens of generic messages per week. Standing out requires doing some engagement work before you send the request, such as commenting on the person's posts, so your name is already familiar when your message arrives.

```markdown
# LinkedIn Cold Outreach Playbook

## BEFORE REACHING OUT:

### Profile Optimization:
- Professional photo
- Compelling headline
- Rich About section
- Visible accomplishments
- Mutual connections displayed

### Engagement First (If Possible):
- Like and comment on their posts
- Share their content
- Join same groups
- Become a familiar name

## CONNECTION REQUEST:

### With Note (300 character limit):
"""
Hi [Name], I'm a Senior Go Engineer interested in
[Company]'s approach to [topic]. Would love to connect
and potentially chat about opportunities there. Thanks!
"""

### After Connecting (Full Message):
"""
Thanks for connecting! I'm exploring opportunities in
[domain] and [Company] caught my attention because of
[specific reason].

Would you have 15 minutes for a quick virtual coffee?
I'd love to learn about your experience there.
"""

## INMAIL (Premium Feature):

### When to Use:
- Can't connect through network
- High-value target
- Second-degree connection

### InMail Template:
"""
Subject: Fellow [school/company/interest] interested in [Company]

Hi [Name],

I noticed we both [shared experience]. I'm currently a
[title] at [company] where I [accomplishment].

I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific topic]
and would love to learn more about engineering opportunities
there. Would you be open to a brief conversation?

Thanks for considering!
[Your Name]
"""

## RESPONSE HANDLING:

### If They Say Yes:
- Respond immediately
- Suggest specific times
- Be flexible
- Send calendar invite promptly

### If They Redirect:
- Thank them graciously
- Ask for introduction if appropriate
- Follow up with the new contact

### If No Response:
- One follow-up after 1 week
- Then move on
- Stay connected for future
```

### Junior → FAANG: Cold Outreach Templates That Actually Work at Early Career

Cold outreach works when it demonstrates that you have done specific research and that you have something specific to say. The two most effective cold outreach formats for early-career engineers targeting FAANG companies are the technical question and the specific role inquiry.

The technical question format: reach out to an engineer whose work you have studied, ask a specific technical question about a blog post, open-source project, or talk they have given, and make it clear you have genuinely engaged with their work. Do not ask for a referral or a job. This is a relationship-building step, not an immediate job search move. After two or three genuine technical exchanges, you have a relationship that makes a later referral ask appropriate.

The specific role inquiry format: "I'm a Go backend engineer with three years of experience building high-throughput event processing systems. I saw [specific role] on your careers page. I've read [specific blog post or project] from your team and I have direct experience with [specific relevant technology]. Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation about the role and what the team is working on?" This format is specific, low-pressure, and signals genuine research.

### Senior @ FAANG: Cold Outreach as Peer Conversation at Senior Level

At senior level, cold outreach to engineering managers and staff engineers at target companies is not outreach from a candidate to a gatekeeper. It is a peer conversation between technical leaders with potentially overlapping professional interests. This framing change affects both the tone and the content of the outreach.

A senior cold outreach that works: "I've been following your team's work on distributed tracing infrastructure, particularly the blog post on sampling strategy tradeoffs from last year. I've been working on similar problems at [current company] at [scale]. I'm exploring new opportunities and your team's work in this space is one of the reasons I'm reaching out. Would you be open to a conversation?" This works because it establishes peer credibility, demonstrates specific knowledge, and makes a clear but low-pressure ask.

What does not work at senior level: outreach that reads as supplication, that lists your accomplishments without demonstrating knowledge of their specific work, or that asks the recipient to do work on your behalf (reviewing your resume, forwarding your application) before they have any context about why you are worth their time. Senior engineers receive a lot of recruiter and cold outreach. The ones that stand out are from people who have clearly done their homework and have something specific and relevant to say.
