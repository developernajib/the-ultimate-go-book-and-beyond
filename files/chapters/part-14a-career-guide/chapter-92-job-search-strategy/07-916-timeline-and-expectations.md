## 92.6 Timeline and Expectations

One of the biggest sources of stress during a job search is not knowing how long it should take. Having realistic expectations about the timeline helps you plan financially, pace your energy, and avoid making panicked decisions. The timelines below are averages. Your specific search may be shorter or longer depending on your seniority, the market, and how targeted your approach is.

### Realistic Timeline

Most engineering job searches take between six and twelve weeks from start to accepted offer. This includes preparation time, active searching and interviewing, and the offer negotiation phase. Understanding how long each phase typically lasts helps you plan your schedule and avoid the common mistake of expecting results in the first week.

```
TYPICAL JOB SEARCH TIMELINE:

PREPARATION (1-2 weeks):
• Update resume
• Optimize LinkedIn
• Research target companies
• Practice interview basics

ACTIVE SEARCH (4-8 weeks):
• Network and apply
• Phone screens
• Technical interviews
• Onsites

OFFER PHASE (1-2 weeks):
• Negotiate offers
• Make decision
• Give notice

TOTAL: 6-12 weeks for most searches

FACTORS THAT AFFECT TIMELINE:
• Seniority (more senior = longer)
• Specificity (narrow search = longer)
• Market conditions
• Luck and timing
```

### Mental Health During Job Search

Job searching is emotionally draining in ways that many engineers underestimate. Repeated rejection, uncertainty about the future, and the pressure to perform in interviews can take a serious toll on your mental health. It is important to acknowledge this upfront and build coping strategies into your routine from the beginning, not as an afterthought when you are already burned out.

```
COPING STRATEGIES:

MAINTAIN ROUTINE:
• Set working hours for job search
• Exercise regularly
• Keep social connections

MANAGE EXPECTATIONS:
• Rejection is normal (even for great candidates)
• Most applications won't get responses
• It's a numbers game

CELEBRATE SMALL WINS:
• Screen completed
• Made it to onsite
• Positive feedback received

TAKE BREAKS:
• Don't job search 24/7
• Have days completely off
• Pursue hobbies

LEAN ON SUPPORT:
• Share with trusted friends/family
• Join job search communities
• Consider career coaching

REFRAME REJECTION:
• Not a reflection of your worth
• Fit matters on both sides
• Each interview is practice
• Right role will come
```

### Junior → FAANG: Setting Realistic Timelines to Avoid Panic Decisions

The timeline data in this section is crucial for financial and psychological planning. If you start a job search with the expectation that it will take two weeks and it takes eight, you will make increasingly poor decisions as the deadline pressure mounts. Start your search with a three-month financial runway and a mental model that accounts for the realistic timeline range.

The sequencing recommendation is: spend weeks one and two on preparation (materials update, interview study, target list), weeks three and four on network activation and application submission, weeks five through ten on interview loops, and weeks eleven and twelve on offer evaluation and negotiation. This sequencing front-loads the non-interview work so that when interviews begin, you are fully prepared rather than scrambling to update your resume between coding assessments.

For engineers who are currently employed and searching discretely: the realistic timeline extends to four to six months at FAANG because of the scheduling constraints of interviewing while working. Plan accordingly, including having a conversation with yourself about how long you can sustain the dual workload of a current job and an active job search.

### Senior @ FAANG: Timeline Compression Strategies for Senior Searches

Senior FAANG searches have a documented tendency to either compress dramatically (two to four weeks from first conversation to offer for a highly sought-after candidate with a strong network) or extend unexpectedly (three to five months for a candidate navigating a complex multi-company process while employed). Understanding which pattern your search is likely to follow helps you plan resources appropriately.

The primary driver of timeline compression for senior candidates is preparation completeness before reaching out to any company. Senior engineers who have updated their materials, completed their system design review, and identified their target company list before making any contact can move quickly through each company's process because they are not doing preparation work in parallel with interviewing. Senior engineers who begin reaching out while still preparing almost always have a longer search because early interviews reveal preparation gaps that require additional study time.

The offer deadline management skill is critical at senior level. When a company makes an offer, they typically give a one-to-two-week decision deadline. If your other processes are more than two weeks behind, you have three options: ask for a deadline extension (typically granted once for one to two weeks at senior level), ask your other companies to accelerate (typically granted for senior candidates with a competing offer), or make a decision with incomplete information. The engineers who consistently get the best outcomes are the ones who have thought through this decision tree before any offer arrives, so they are not making it under pressure.
