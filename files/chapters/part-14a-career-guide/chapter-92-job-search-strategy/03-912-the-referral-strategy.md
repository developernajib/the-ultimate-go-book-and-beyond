## 92.2 The Referral Strategy

A referral is when someone who already works at a company submits your name and resume through the company's internal referral system. This is the most reliable shortcut in a job search because it gets your application past the automated filters and onto a real person's desk. Building and activating a referral network is not about being transactional or manipulative. It is about maintaining genuine professional relationships that benefit both sides over time.

### Building Your Referral Network

Your referral network is broader than you think. It includes former colleagues, classmates, people you have met at conferences or meetups, and even second-degree connections who share a mutual contact with you. The key is to map out who you already know at your target companies and who might introduce you to someone there. Different levels of familiarity call for different approaches in how you ask for help.

```
WHO CAN REFER YOU:

FIRST DEGREE:
• Former colleagues (strongest)
• Former managers
• Current friends at target companies
• Classmates from school

SECOND DEGREE:
• Connections of your connections
• Alumni from same school
• People you've met at meetups
• Online community members

HOW TO ASK FOR REFERRALS:

DIRECT (Someone you know well):
"Hey [Name], I saw there's a Senior Engineer role at
[Company]. I'm really interested in the payments team's
work. Would you be willing to refer me? I can send you
my resume and any details you need."

WARM (Connection but not close):
"Hi [Name], hope you're doing well! I'm exploring new
opportunities and noticed you work at [Company]. I'm
really interested in their work on [specific thing].
Would you be open to a quick call to learn more about
your experience there? And if appropriate, potentially
put in a referral?"

COLD (No prior relationship):
"Hi [Name], I came across your profile while researching
[Company]. I'm a Senior Go Engineer interested in joining
the [team] team. I'd love to learn more about the culture
and work. Would you have 15 minutes for a virtual coffee?"
```

### The Referral Ask Template

When asking for a referral over email, you want to make it as easy as possible for the other person to say yes. Provide everything they need in one message: the specific role you are interested in, a brief explanation of why you are a good fit, and your updated resume. Keep the tone warm and appreciative, and always give them an easy way to decline without awkwardness.

```
Subject: Referral request for [Position] at [Company]

Hi [Name],

Hope you're doing well! I saw [Company] has a [Position]
opening and I'm really excited about it because [specific
reason - their technical challenges, product, team].

I've been working as a [Your title] at [Current company],
where I [brief highlight]. Here's my resume - [link].

If you feel comfortable referring me, that would be
incredibly helpful. Happy to send any additional info
you need for the referral form.

If you'd like to chat first, I'm free [times].

Either way, I'd love to hear how things are going at
[Company] for you!

Thanks so much,
[Your name]
```

### Following Up After Referral

After someone submits a referral on your behalf, your job is not done. You need to follow up at the right intervals to keep the process moving without being pushy. Most importantly, always thank your referrer regardless of the outcome. People who feel appreciated are far more likely to help you again in the future or refer you to other opportunities.

```
TIMELINE:
Day 0: Referral submitted
Day 3-5: Send thank you to referrer
Day 7-10: If no response, ask referrer if they can check status
Day 14: Apply directly as backup (mention referral)

THANK YOU MESSAGE:
"Thanks so much for putting in the referral! I really
appreciate you vouching for me. I'll keep you posted on
how it goes. Let me know if I can ever return the favor!"

STATUS CHECK:
"Hey! Just wanted to check in - any word on the referral?
I haven't heard from the recruiter yet. No worries if you
don't know, just thought I'd ask!"
```

### Junior → FAANG: Referral Mechanics for First-Job and Early-Career Searches

At early career, the referral network is thin because you have not yet accumulated the professional relationships that produce it. The fastest way to build it from a small base is to give before you ask. Contribute to an open-source project that an engineer at your target company maintains. Answer questions in a technical forum where that company's engineers are active. Engage substantively with their technical writing. Over weeks, not days, these interactions create the kind of minimal relationship that makes a referral ask appropriate.

When you ask, make it easy and low-cost for the person you are asking. A well-crafted referral ask includes: a specific role link, a two-sentence explanation of why you are a strong fit for that specific role, your current resume, and explicit permission for them to decline with no awkwardness. Do not ask a near-stranger to figure out how to vouch for your entire background. Give them a specific, narrow claim to support.

After the referral is submitted: follow up once to confirm it was submitted, thank the person regardless of outcome, and update them when you hear back. This closes the loop and maintains the relationship for future referrals in either direction.

### Senior @ FAANG: Referrals as a Reciprocal Network Asset

At senior level, the referral dynamic is fundamentally different from early career. Strong senior engineers receive referral requests from peers, and the management of those requests is itself a reputation signal. Giving thoughtful, well-prepared referrals to engineers you can credibly vouch for builds your reputation as someone with good technical judgment. Giving referrals indiscriminately or for engineers whose work you do not actually know damages that reputation.

The reciprocity principle at senior level: be generous with referrals for engineers you genuinely believe in, and maintain the expectation that this generosity is not transactional. Do not give referrals as favors to friends whose technical work you cannot personally vouch for. The referral is a statement about your professional judgment, and it will be interpreted as one by the hiring committee.

For senior candidates asking for referrals: the mechanics are identical to early career, but the context differs. You have a larger network, so you likely know someone at most target companies. The ask should be more specific at senior level: not just "can you refer me to a role" but "I'm specifically interested in the distributed systems team's work on X, and I know you worked adjacent to that team last year. Would you be comfortable making an introduction to the hiring manager for that area?" This level of specificity demonstrates that you have done your research and respects the referrer's time.
