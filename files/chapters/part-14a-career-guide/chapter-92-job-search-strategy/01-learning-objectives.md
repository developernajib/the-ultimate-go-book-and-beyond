## Learning Objectives

This chapter covers the full lifecycle of a software engineering job search, from building your target list and activating your network through managing a parallel pipeline of applications and interviews. These objectives focus on the practical skills that separate an organized, high-yield job search from a frustrating spray-and-pray approach.

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Develop an effective job search strategy
- Use your network for referrals
- Navigate job boards and applications effectively
- Work with recruiters productively
- Manage multiple applications simultaneously
- Stay organized throughout the process

### Junior → FAANG: Turning Objectives into a Weekly Action Plan

Each objective in this list maps to a concrete weekly action at early career. Network referrals: identify three engineers at target companies to engage with this week through open-source, meetups, or LinkedIn comments. Job boards: spend 30 minutes per day reviewing new postings and applying to two to three well-matched roles with tailored resumes. Recruiter management: respond to every recruiter outreach within 24 hours, even if only to decline politely and note the company for future consideration. Pipeline tracking: update your spreadsheet at the end of every day you take a job search action.

The organization objective is the one most early-career candidates underinvest in. A job search that spans 60 days and 20 companies generates hundreds of email threads, dozens of phone calls, and numerous scheduling interactions. Without a tracking system, you will miss follow-up deadlines, forget recruiter names, and lose context on your status at each company. Set up the tracking system on day one.

### Senior @ FAANG: Objective Reframing for Senior Job Search Strategy

At senior level, "use your network for referrals" becomes "activate your professional network strategically before you enter the market." The difference is timing and intentionality. A senior engineer who tells three trusted peers they are exploring new opportunities three months before they are ready to interview will often receive pre-screened introductions to hiring managers, advance notice of upcoming roles, and intelligence about team dynamics and culture that is unavailable through any public channel.

"Work with recruiters productively" at senior level includes knowing when to work with external agency recruiters versus internal FAANG sourcers, how to use recruiter conversations to calibrate market rate before you have offers, and how to manage the recruiter relationship so that they are helping you achieve your timeline rather than theirs. Senior candidates who let recruiters control the timeline consistently end up with less leverage than those who manage their own schedule deliberately.
