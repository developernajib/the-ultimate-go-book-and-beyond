## 92.3 Working with Recruiters

Recruiters are a major part of the hiring ecosystem, and understanding how to work with them effectively can significantly accelerate your job search. However, not all recruiters operate the same way or have the same incentives. Knowing the difference between internal recruiters, external agency recruiters, and sourcers helps you set the right expectations and communicate in a way that gets you the best results.

### Types of Recruiters

There are three main types of recruiters you will encounter during your job search. Each type has different goals, different levels of influence over the hiring decision, and different ways you should interact with them. Understanding these distinctions from the start prevents common mistakes like sharing salary expectations too early with an agency recruiter or ignoring a sourcer who could open doors to multiple roles.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  RECRUITER TYPES                                 │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                  │
│  INTERNAL RECRUITERS                                             │
│  Work for: The hiring company                                    │
│  Incentive: Fill roles, build long-term pipeline                 │
│  How to work with:                                               │
│  • Be responsive (they're managing many candidates)              │
│  • Ask questions about role and team                             │
│  • Build relationship even if this role doesn't work out         │
│                                                                  │
│  EXTERNAL/AGENCY RECRUITERS                                      │
│  Work for: Recruiting firms (paid by companies)                  │
│  Incentive: Commission on placement (15-25% of salary)           │
│  How to work with:                                               │
│  • Verify the role is real and exclusive                         │
│  • Get company name before sending resume                        │
│  • Don't share salary expectations prematurely                   │
│  • They can provide market intel                                 │
│                                                                  │
│  SOURCERS                                                        │
│  Work for: Company (junior to recruiters)                        │
│  Incentive: Fill top of funnel, hit outreach targets             │
│  How to work with:                                               │
│  • Respond even if not interested (relationship building)        │
│  • Be clear about what you're looking for                        │
│  • They may have multiple relevant roles                         │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Responding to Recruiter Outreach

How you respond to a recruiter's initial message sets the tone for the entire relationship. Even if you are not currently looking for a new role, responding politely keeps the door open for future opportunities. The templates below cover the three most common scenarios: you are interested, you are open but not actively looking, and you are not interested at all. Adapt the language to your own voice, but keep the structure.

```
IF INTERESTED:
"Thanks for reaching out! This role sounds interesting.
I'd love to learn more about the team and the specific
technical challenges. Do you have time for a call this week?"

IF NOT INTERESTED BUT OPEN:
"Thanks for thinking of me! I'm not actively looking right
now, but I'm always interested in hearing about great
opportunities. Could you share more about the role and team?
What makes this position special?"

IF NOT INTERESTED:
"Thank you for reaching out! I'm not looking to make a move
right now. Would it be okay if I reached out in the future
when I am looking?"

SETTING EXPECTATIONS:
"I'm actively interviewing and expect to make a decision in
the next 3-4 weeks. Is that timeline workable for your
process?"
```

### Questions to Ask Recruiters

The recruiter screen is not just an evaluation of you. It is also your chance to evaluate the role and the company. Asking thoughtful questions demonstrates genuine interest and helps you decide early whether this opportunity is worth pursuing. Pay attention to red flags in how the recruiter answers, as vague or evasive responses often signal problems with the role or the company.

```
ABOUT THE ROLE:
• What's the day-to-day like for this role?
• Why is this position open?
• What does success look like in 6 months?
• What's the team structure?
• Who would I report to?

ABOUT THE PROCESS:
• What does the interview process look like?
• How many interviews total?
• What's the typical timeline?
• What are you looking for in candidates?

ABOUT COMPENSATION:
• What's the salary range for this role?
• What's the equity structure?
• How are bonuses determined?
• What are the benefits like?

RED FLAGS:
⚠️ Won't share company name
⚠️ Can't explain the role clearly
⚠️ Pressures you to decide quickly
⚠️ Vague on compensation
⚠️ Role has been open "forever"
```

### Junior → FAANG: Recruiter Conversations as Information Gathering

Early-career engineers often treat recruiter calls as gatekeeping interactions to survive rather than information-gathering opportunities to use actively. This framing leaves value on the table. A recruiter who is enthusiastic about your background is a resource: they can tell you what the team is actually looking for, what the compensation range is, how long the role has been open, and what the timeline looks like for hiring. Ask all of these questions in the first recruiter call.

The compensation question deserves specific attention at early career. Many candidates avoid discussing compensation in the recruiter screen because they are worried about anchoring low. The opposite risk is more common: anchoring to a number before you have competing offers means you cannot negotiate upward later. The right approach is to give a range based on your market research: "Based on my research for this role and level in this market, I'm targeting $X to $Y. Does that align with the band for this position?" This establishes that you have done your homework and invites the recruiter to confirm or correct your range without committing you to a specific number.

### Senior @ FAANG: Managing Recruiters as a Pipeline Partner

At senior level, the recruiter relationship is more complex because you are simultaneously a candidate for their role and a potential future hiring partner when you join the company. The way you conduct yourself in recruiter interactions at senior level is observable beyond the immediate hiring decision.

The most effective senior approach to recruiter management: be direct about your timeline, your competing offers, and your priorities. Recruiters at FAANG companies work with senior candidates regularly and respond well to professional directness. "I have two other processes in progress and expect to have decisions by the end of the month. I'm interested in moving quickly with you if the role is a strong fit. What does your timeline look like?" is a normal and well-received opening for a senior recruiter conversation.

For external agency recruiters: their incentive is to place you, which is generally aligned with your interests but not perfectly. They may push you toward a company or role that generates a higher fee or closes faster. Maintain your own view of which opportunities align with your criteria, and do not let recruiter enthusiasm override your own judgment about fit. The "role has been open forever" red flag is especially important at senior level: a senior role that has been open for more than 90 days usually has either a very high bar, an unclear team charter, or a difficult hiring manager. All three are worth investigating before investing interview time.
