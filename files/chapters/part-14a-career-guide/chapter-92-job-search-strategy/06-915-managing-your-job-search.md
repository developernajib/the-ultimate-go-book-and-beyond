## 92.5 Managing Your Job Search

A job search with multiple applications in flight quickly becomes unmanageable without a system. Forgetting which company you applied to, missing a follow-up deadline, or double-booking interviews are common problems that cost candidates opportunities. Treating your job search like a project with proper tracking, pipeline management, and regular reviews keeps you organized and ensures nothing falls through the cracks.

### The Application Tracker

A simple spreadsheet is all you need to track your job search, but the discipline of updating it consistently is what makes it work. Every time you apply, have a conversation with a recruiter, or move to a new interview stage, record it immediately. This tracker becomes your single source of truth and prevents embarrassing situations like forgetting which role you applied for when a recruiter calls you back.

```
SPREADSHEET COLUMNS:

| Company | Role | Date Applied | Status | Next Step | Notes |
|---------|------|--------------|--------|-----------|-------|
| Stripe | Sr Eng | 2026-01-10 | Screen | Call 1/15 | Ref:Bob |
| Meta | Staff | 2026-01-12 | Applied | Follow up | Comp: 400k |
| Startup | Lead | 2026-01-08 | Onsite | Loop 1/20 | Small team |

STATUS OPTIONS:
• Applied
• Recruiter Screen Scheduled
• Recruiter Screen Complete
• Technical Screen Scheduled
• Technical Screen Complete
• Onsite Scheduled
• Onsite Complete
• Offer
• Rejected
• Withdrew
```

### Pipeline Management

Think of your job search like a sales funnel: you need enough opportunities at every stage to ensure that at least one converts into an offer. If you stop applying once you have a few interviews scheduled, you risk ending up with nothing if those interviews do not work out. The goal is to maintain a healthy pipeline where new applications are entering the top while existing ones progress through interviews toward offers.

```
IDEAL PIPELINE (for active search):

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Applied: 10-15 open applications                             │
│ Screen: 3-5 in recruiter screen stage                        │
│ Technical: 2-3 in technical interview stage                  │
│ Onsite: 1-2 in onsite/final stage                            │
│ Offer: 1+ (goal)                                             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

PACING:
• Apply to 5-10 new roles per week
• Aim for 3-5 recruiter screens per week
• Schedule onsites strategically (not all at once)
• Keep pipeline full even when interviews are going well

AVOIDING BURNOUT:
• Limit to 2 onsites per week max
• Take breaks between intensive interview days
• Don't interview when you're not at your best
• Have non-job-search activities scheduled
```

### When Things Go Slow

Every job search hits rough patches where applications seem to vanish into a void and rejections pile up. This is normal and does not mean you are a bad engineer. The important thing is to diagnose where in the funnel you are getting stuck and adjust your approach accordingly. Each stage of the process has different failure modes and different fixes.

```
IF NOT GETTING RESPONSES:
• Review your resume (get feedback)
• Check if applications are actually submitting
• Are you applying to appropriate level roles?
• Try more referrals, fewer cold applications
• Consider resume review services

IF NOT PASSING SCREENS:
• Practice your pitch
• Research companies more before calls
• Be more specific about what you want
• Ask for feedback when rejected

IF NOT PASSING TECHNICALS:
• Practice coding interviews (LeetCode, etc.)
• Do mock interviews
• Review fundamental data structures
• Study system design

IF NOT GETTING OFFERS AFTER ONSITES:
• Request feedback
• May be culture fit issues
• Consider interview coaching
• Reflect on which parts went poorly
```

### Junior → FAANG: Building the Tracking Habit from Day One

The pipeline management system described in this section is not optional. It is the infrastructure that makes everything else in the job search work. Early-career candidates who do not track their pipeline consistently make specific, avoidable mistakes: they forget to follow up after interviews, they miss recruiter-imposed deadlines, they cannot answer "where are you in your job search?" when a new recruiter asks, and they end up making offer decisions under time pressure because they did not manage their pipeline to produce simultaneous decisions.

The minimum viable tracking system is a Google Sheet with eight columns: company, role link, application date, contact name, current stage, next action required, action deadline, and notes. Update it every time any action occurs. Set a recurring 15-minute weekly review to check for overdue actions. This system costs almost nothing to set up and prevents the most common job search failures.

When you receive a rejection, log it with a brief note about where in the process it happened and any feedback received. Over a 60-day search, this log becomes a dataset that tells you whether your rejections are clustering at the phone screen (resume/profile issue), the technical screen (coding preparation gap), or the onsite (system design or behavioral gap). Each cluster points to a specific improvement.

### Senior @ FAANG: Pipeline Management as Strategic Resource Allocation

At senior level, pipeline management is complicated by the higher interview investment each company requires. A FAANG senior engineer loop is typically five to six hours of interviews plus preparation. Managing four to six simultaneous loops without letting any one company's timeline collapse is a genuine project management challenge that deserves explicit planning.

The senior pipeline management principle: schedule your tier-one companies (highest priority, most competitive) for the middle of your search, not the beginning. Use tier-three companies (strongest fit, most likely offer) in the first two weeks to calibrate your interviewing, identify your weakest areas, and get your loop into flow before your most important conversations. This sequencing requires courage because it means potentially declining or delaying early outreach from your preferred companies, but it consistently produces better outcomes than interviewing at top targets before you are fully calibrated.

Timeline compression is a legitimate senior tactic. When you have an offer with a deadline and another company's process is three weeks behind, it is appropriate to tell that second company: "I have an offer with a decision deadline of [date]. I'm very interested in your team and would like to complete the process with you before that deadline if possible. Can we accelerate?" Most FAANG companies have explicit processes for timeline acceleration and will accommodate this request for senior candidates they are excited about.
