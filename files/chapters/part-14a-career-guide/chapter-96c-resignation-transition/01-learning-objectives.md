## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Prepare thoroughly before submitting your resignation
- Handle the resignation conversation professionally
- Write an effective resignation letter
- Execute an exemplary notice period
- Navigate counter offers confidently
- Maintain valuable professional relationships

### Junior → FAANG: What Each Objective Means in Practice

"Prepare thoroughly before submitting your resignation" means completing three specific steps: confirm your new offer in writing, review your current employment agreement, and have a transition plan ready before the conversation. These are not procedural formalities: each one protects you in a specific way. The confirmed offer protects against situations where a verbal offer falls through after you have resigned. The employment agreement review protects against accidentally violating non-compete or IP clauses. The transition plan demonstrates professionalism and protects the relationships you are leaving behind.

"Navigate counter offers confidently" is the objective that most engineers feel unprepared for because it requires clarity about your own motivations for leaving. If you are leaving because the new role offers fundamentally better scope or compensation, a counter offer from your current employer addresses a symptom rather than the cause. Most engineers who accept counter offers leave within 18 months because the underlying reasons for looking persist.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior-Level Objectives and the Organizational Legacy Frame

At senior level, each objective scales to organizational scope. "Execute an exemplary notice period" means not just completing your current work but ensuring the organization is in a better position to operate after your departure than it would be without structured transition. For a staff engineer, this can include training a successor, documenting architectural decisions that exist only in your head, and introducing key external relationships to your manager so those connections persist.

"Maintain valuable professional relationships" at senior level means explicit, systematic effort. In the week before your last day, have individual conversations with the six to ten people whose professional relationship matters most to you. Exchange personal contact information. Tell them specifically what you valued about working with them. This takes three hours and produces relationships that can sustain your career for decades.
