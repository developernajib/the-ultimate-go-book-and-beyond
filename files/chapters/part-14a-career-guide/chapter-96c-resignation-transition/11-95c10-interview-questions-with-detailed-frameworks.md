## 96C.10 Interview Questions with Detailed Frameworks

### Question 1: "Tell me about a time you left a job. How did you handle the transition?"

**What Interviewers Are Testing**:
- Professional maturity and ethics
- Ability to maintain relationships during difficult transitions
- Organizational skills (knowledge transfer)
- Self-awareness about career decisions

**Framework for Answering (STAR+ Method)**:

```
SITUATION (30 seconds):
- What company were you at?
- How long had you been there?
- What was your role?

TASK (20 seconds):
- Why were you leaving? (Frame positively)
- What was the timeline pressure?
- What responsibilities needed transition?

ACTION - THE TRANSITION (1-2 minutes):
Focus on WHAT YOU DID to ensure smooth handoff:
1. Communication approach
   - Who did you tell and in what order?
   - How did you handle the conversation?
2. Knowledge transfer
   - What documentation did you create?
   - How did you train your replacement/team?
3. Final deliverables
   - What did you complete before leaving?
   - How did you tie up loose ends?
4. Relationship maintenance
   - How did you preserve professional connections?

RESULT (30 seconds):
- How did the team fare after you left?
- What feedback did you receive?
- What relationships have you maintained?

LEARNING (15 seconds):
- What would you do differently?
- What did you learn about transitions?
```

**Example Strong Answer**:

"I was at a Series B startup for two years as a senior engineer when I received an offer from Google. My team owned two critical services, so I knew the transition was important.

First, I told my manager in a private meeting before anyone else. I explained my decision was about career growth at a larger company, not dissatisfaction - which was true. I offered three weeks notice instead of two because we were mid-release.

For knowledge transfer, I created a 'Departure Runbook' for each system I owned. Each included architecture diagrams, common issues and fixes, deployment procedures, and ongoing work status. I scheduled three knowledge transfer sessions with the team, recorded for those who couldn't attend.

I also identified and recommended promotions for two engineers who could take over my responsibilities. I spent my last week pairing with them on the most complex parts of the codebase.

The result was that both services continued without incidents for the next quarter - my former manager told me that later. I'm still in touch with several colleagues from that team, and one joined Google last year partly based on my recommendation.

What I learned was that thorough documentation during the notice period isn't optional - it's part of being a professional. How you leave a company is as important as how you performed there."

**What to Avoid**:
- "I just gave my two weeks and left" (shows poor professionalism)
- "They were upset but that's their problem" (lacks empathy)
- "I don't stay in touch with people from old jobs" (poor networking)
- Complaints about the company you left

### Question 2: "Have you ever received a counter offer? How did you handle it?"

**What Interviewers Are Testing**:
- Decision-making process
- Understanding of your own motivations
- Ability to handle pressure situations
- Professional maturity

**Framework for Answering**:

```
IF YOU DECLINED THE COUNTER:
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. ACKNOWLEDGE THE OFFER                                         │
│     "They offered [briefly what], which I appreciated"            │
│                                                                   │
│  2. EXPLAIN YOUR REASONING (Frame as positive)                    │
│     "My decision wasn't about compensation - it was about         │
│      [growth/opportunity/direction]"                              │
│                                                                   │
│  3. SHOW PROFESSIONAL HANDLING                                    │
│     "I thanked them sincerely and reaffirmed my commitment        │
│      to a strong transition"                                      │
│                                                                   │
│  4. DEMONSTRATE LEARNING                                          │
│     "The experience taught me to be clear about my                │
│      motivations before starting a job search"                    │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

IF YOU ACCEPTED THE COUNTER (Be honest but careful):
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. BE HONEST ABOUT IT                                            │
│     "Yes, I once accepted a counter offer"                        │
│                                                                   │
│  2. EXPLAIN THE CONTEXT                                           │
│     "My only issue was compensation, and the counter              │
│      addressed that directly with structural changes"             │
│                                                                   │
│  3. DISCUSS THE OUTCOME                                           │
│     If it worked: "I stayed another [X] years productively"       │
│     If it didn't: "I learned that counter offers rarely           │
│      address root issues - I left [time] later"                   │
│                                                                   │
│  4. SHOW LEARNING                                                 │
│     "Now I understand that 50%+ of counter accepters leave        │
│      within 18 months. I'd be more cautious today."               │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Example Strong Answer (Declined)**:

"Yes, when I left my last company, they offered a significant counter - about 25% raise plus an immediate promotion to Tech Lead.

I was genuinely grateful for the recognition. But I'd thought carefully about why I was leaving, and it wasn't about money. I wanted exposure to larger-scale systems and a different problem domain. Those weren't things a raise could provide.

I declined respectfully. I said something like: 'This means a lot to me, and I'm grateful you value my contributions. But my decision is about where I want to grow my career, not compensation. I'm confident this is the right move for me.'

My manager understood. The fact that we could have that conversation professionally is part of why I'd work with him again. I actually referred someone to him later.

What I learned is that if you're clear about your reasons for leaving before you start looking, counter offers become easier to evaluate. The money is rarely the root cause."

### Question 3: "Tell me about a time you had to deliver difficult news to a stakeholder."

**Why This Question Relates to Resignation**:
- Resignation IS delivering difficult news
- Shows professional communication skills
- Demonstrates ability to handle emotional situations
- Reveals how you maintain relationships through conflict

**Framework for Answering**:

```
STRUCTURE FOR DIFFICULT CONVERSATIONS:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  1. PREPARATION (What you did before the conversation)           │
│     - How did you think through the message?                     │
│     - How did you anticipate their reaction?                     │
│     - What alternatives did you consider?                        │
│                                                                  │
│  2. DELIVERY (How you communicated)                              │
│     - Where and when did you have the conversation?              │
│     - What exact words did you use?                              │
│     - How did you frame it (direct, empathetic)?                 │
│                                                                  │
│  3. RESPONSE HANDLING (How you managed their reaction)           │
│     - What was their reaction?                                   │
│     - How did you respond to emotion or pushback?                │
│     - How did you stay calm and professional?                    │
│                                                                  │
│  4. FOLLOW-THROUGH (What happened next)                          │
│     - How did you support them after the news?                   │
│     - What did you do to maintain the relationship?              │
│     - What was the outcome?                                      │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Example Strong Answer (Using Resignation as Context)**:

"One of the hardest things I've had to do was tell my manager I was leaving. He had invested significantly in my growth - sponsored my promotion, advocated for my projects, and been a genuine mentor.

I prepared by writing down exactly what I wanted to say. I scheduled a private meeting mid-week, mid-morning - not Friday, not end of day. I didn't want it to feel like I was running away.

I was direct: 'I want you to know before anyone else that I've decided to accept an opportunity at another company. My last day will be [date].' Then I stopped talking and let him respond.

He was disappointed but gracious. He asked where I was going and why. I kept my answer positive: 'It's a chance to work on [specific area] at a larger scale, which aligns with my long-term goals. This isn't about anything negative here - I've learned so much from you.'

He tried to counter-offer, which I expected. I thanked him and said I'd made my decision. We spent the rest of the meeting discussing transition - which was the best use of the time.

The relationship stayed strong. He's been a reference for me since, and I've referred candidates to him. How I handled that conversation is why we're still in each other's professional networks.

The key learning: difficult news delivered with respect and clarity preserves relationships. Avoiding, delaying, or being unclear damages them."

### Question 4: "How do you maintain professional relationships after changing jobs?"

**What Interviewers Are Testing**:
- Networking skills
- Long-term thinking
- Professional maturity
- Understanding that industry relationships matter

**Framework for Answering**:

```
RELATIONSHIP MAINTENANCE STRATEGIES:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  IMMEDIATE (Before and during departure):                        │
│  ├── Connect on LinkedIn                                         │
│  ├── Exchange personal contact info                              │
│  ├── Send thoughtful farewell message                            │
│  └── Offer to stay available for questions                       │
│                                                                  │
│  FIRST 90 DAYS (After departure):                                │
│  ├── Send "landed safely" update                                 │
│  ├── Respond promptly to transition questions                    │
│  ├── Have coffee with 2-3 close colleagues                       │
│  └── Engage with their LinkedIn posts                            │
│                                                                  │
│  ONGOING (Long-term maintenance):                                │
│  ├── Annual catch-up with key contacts                           │
│  ├── Congratulate on achievements/promotions                     │
│  ├── Share relevant opportunities                                │
│  ├── Make referrals when appropriate                             │
│  └── Help when asked                                             │
│                                                                  │
│  THE "BIG FIVE" (Always maintain):                               │
│  1. Former managers (reference letter sources)                   │
│  2. Skip-level managers (executive network)                      │
│  3. Strong collaborators (referral sources)                      │
│  4. Mentors/sponsors (career advice)                             │
│  5. Rising stars (future leaders)                                │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Example Strong Answer**:

"I treat professional relationships as long-term investments. My network includes people from every company I've worked at, some going back fifteen years.

My system has three parts:

First, before leaving any job, I make sure I have personal contact information - not just work email - for people I want to stay connected with. I send each a personal note, not just the mass farewell email.

Second, I maintain a 'key contacts' list of about fifty people across my career. Every six months, I go through it and reach out to anyone I haven't connected with recently - even just a LinkedIn comment or quick email.

Third, I look for opportunities to add value. When I see a job posting that might interest a former colleague, I forward it. When someone publishes an article or gets promoted, I congratulate them. When someone's hiring and I know a good candidate, I make the introduction.

The return on this investment has been significant. My last two jobs came from referrals by former colleagues. I've hired three people through my network. And I've been able to help others advance their careers.

The mindset shift is this: every colleague is potentially a 20-year professional relationship. The industry is small. The person you leave well today might hire you - or be hired by you - five years from now."

### Junior → FAANG: Departure Interview Questions as Career Narrative Opportunities

"Why did you leave your last job" is the interview question that candidates most frequently handle poorly, and it is asked in nearly every interview at every level. The framework is simple: lead with something genuinely positive about what you learned or achieved in the role, then transition to what you are excited about in the new opportunity. Never lead with what was wrong with the company you left.

The answer should take 60 to 90 seconds. Longer answers start to sound like you are processing the departure rather than having a clear perspective on it. Shorter answers sound evasive.

Practice your answer to this question specifically. It should be the most polished answer in your repertoire because it is asked first and sets the tone for everything that follows.

### Senior @ FAANG: Departure Narrative at Senior Level and the Trajectory Signal

At senior level, "why did you leave your last role" is a question about organizational trajectory as much as personal motivation. Bar-raisers are listening for whether your departure signals anything concerning about the previous organization's health or your ability to operate within organizational constraints.

The strongest senior departure narrative frames the move as a deliberate progression toward a specific kind of scope or technical challenge that the previous role could not provide, not as a reaction to organizational problems. "I was looking for an opportunity to work at platform scale with a team that is building foundational infrastructure" is a forward-looking narrative. "My previous manager was difficult and the team was underresourced" is a backward-looking narrative that raises questions about your judgment and your ability to manage up.
