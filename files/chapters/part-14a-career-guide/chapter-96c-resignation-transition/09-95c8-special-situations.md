## 96C.8 Special Situations

### Immediate Departure Requested

Some companies escort departing employees out on the same day, especially for roles with access to sensitive systems or competitive intelligence. This is not personal, it is standard policy at many technology companies. Know your rights and be prepared for this possibility.

```
WHEN COMPANY SAYS "TODAY IS YOUR LAST DAY":

This can happen, especially:
- If you're going to a competitor
- In sensitive roles with confidential access
- During layoff-sensitive periods
- At companies with policy of immediate terms

YOUR RIGHTS:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  TYPICALLY YOU'RE ENTITLED TO:                                   │
│  ├── Pay through your intended last day                          │
│  │   (If you offered 2 weeks, they owe 2 weeks pay)              │
│  ├── Accrued vacation payout (state dependent)                   │
│  ├── COBRA notification and enrollment period                    │
│  └── Return of personal belongings                               │
│                                                                  │
│  WHAT TO DO:                                                     │
│  ├── Stay calm and professional                                  │
│  ├── Ask about pay and benefits in writing                       │
│  ├── Return equipment as requested                               │
│  ├── Request time to collect personal items                      │
│  └── Get confirmation of final paycheck date                     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

This isn't personal - it's often company policy. Don't burn
bridges over it.
```

### Garden Leave

Garden leave is a paid period between your resignation and your actual departure where you stop working but remain technically employed. It is common at companies with non-compete clauses or in roles with access to competitive strategy. Understand the terms before your resignation conversation.

```
GARDEN LEAVE: Paid time off between resignation and departure

WHAT IT IS:
- You're still employed (on payroll)
- But not required to work
- Usually can't start new job until it ends
- Common in finance, consulting, and senior roles

EXAMPLE:
You resign with 2 weeks notice.
Company says: "We'll pay you for the 2 weeks, but you don't
need to come in. Your last day is today."

BENEFITS:
- Paid vacation essentially
- Time to prepare for new role
- Mental break between jobs

CONSIDERATIONS:
- Can't start new job during garden leave
- May affect new employer's start date expectations
- Non-compete may be enforced during this period
```

### When Things Go Badly

Not every resignation goes smoothly. Managers sometimes react with anger, guilt-tripping, or threats. Knowing how to handle these situations calmly and professionally protects both your reputation and your legal rights.

```
IF YOUR RESIGNATION GOES POORLY:

MANAGER REACTS BADLY:
- Stay calm
- Don't engage in argument
- Document the interaction
- Escalate to HR if needed
- Continue being professional

ASKED TO LEAVE IMMEDIATELY WITHOUT PAY:
- Ask for written confirmation
- Check your employment agreement
- Consult employment lawyer if significant money involved
- Document everything

ATTEMPTS TO SABOTAGE:
- Document everything
- Keep communications professional
- Don't retaliate
- Let your work speak for itself
- If serious, consult HR or legal

REMEMBER:
The vast majority of resignations go smoothly.
But if yours doesn't, stay professional and document.
Your reputation in the long run matters more than
any short-term conflict.
```

### Junior → FAANG: Special Situations That Require Rapid Decision-Making

The most important special situation for early-career engineers is being asked to leave immediately upon resignation (escorted out). This happens at some companies as standard policy, at others only for specific roles with access to sensitive systems. If it happens, it is not personal: it is policy. Be prepared by having already backed up personal contacts and documents before giving notice.

The second most common special situation is being given a longer notice period than you offered. If your new employer's start date is fixed, you cannot accommodate a longer notice period without risking your new offer. Address this directly: "I appreciate the desire for a longer transition, and I'm happy to work intensively during the two weeks I have, but my start date at my new role is fixed." Most managers will accept this.

### Senior @ FAANG: Complex Special Situations and Organizational Risk Management

At senior level, special situations include leaving during a critical project (where your departure creates organizational risk), leaving for a direct competitor (where legal counsel may become relevant), and leaving amid organizational turmoil (where your departure may trigger additional departures).

The direct competitor situation requires immediate legal review. Non-compete clauses vary dramatically in enforceability by jurisdiction, but at senior levels where you have access to strategic product information, you may be subject to garden leave provisions or restrictions on specific customer contacts. Understanding your legal position before resigning is essential: consult an employment attorney if your employment agreement contains non-compete or non-solicitation clauses.

The organizational turmoil situation requires particular care. If you are leaving during a period when the organization is already destabilized, your departure may be read as a signal about the company's trajectory that could accelerate further departures. You are not responsible for managing organizational stability through your tenure decisions, but being aware of the organizational context lets you make more informed choices about timing, transition planning, and communication.
