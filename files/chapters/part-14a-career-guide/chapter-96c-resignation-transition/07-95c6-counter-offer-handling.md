## 96C.6 Counter Offer Handling

### Understanding Counter Offers

When you resign, your manager may present a counter offer. Understanding why companies make counter offers helps you evaluate them rationally rather than emotionally. Most counter offers are a short-term retention tactic, not a solution to whatever motivated you to leave in the first place.

```
WHY COUNTER OFFERS HAPPEN:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  COMPANY MOTIVATIONS:                                            │
│                                                                  │
│  ├── Replacement is expensive ($50k-200k+ to hire/train)         │
│  ├── Project timelines at risk                                   │
│  ├── Institutional knowledge loss                                │
│  ├── Team morale impact                                          │
│  ├── Manager's metrics affected                                  │
│  └── Sometimes genuine desire to keep good people                │
│                                                                  │
│  WHAT THEY MIGHT OFFER:                                          │
│                                                                  │
│  ├── Salary increase (10-30% typical)                            │
│  ├── Equity grant                                                │
│  ├── Promotion or title change                                   │
│  ├── Team or project change                                      │
│  ├── Remote work flexibility                                     │
│  └── Vague promises of "things will change"                      │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Statistics

The data on counter offer acceptance is sobering. Engineers who accept counter offers leave anyway within 6-12 months at high rates, often under worse circumstances than their original departure would have been.

```
COUNTER OFFER ACCEPTANCE OUTCOMES:

Industry statistics suggest:

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                  │
│  50-80% of people who accept counter offers                      │
│  leave within 18 months anyway.                                  │
│                                                                  │
│  WHY?                                                            │
│  ├── The reasons for leaving usually remain                      │
│  ├── Trust is damaged on both sides                              │
│  ├── You're now seen as a "flight risk"                          │
│  ├── Resentment builds ("why didn't you pay me this before?")    │
│  ├── Promotion path may be affected                              │
│  └── You may be first considered for layoffs                     │
│                                                                  │
│  THE UNCOMFORTABLE QUESTION:                                     │
│  If you're worth $X now, why weren't you being paid              │
│  $X before you threatened to leave?                              │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Counter Offer Evaluation Framework

Before automatically declining a counter offer, evaluate it objectively. In rare cases, a counter offer genuinely addresses the root cause of your departure. More commonly, it addresses only the salary component while leaving the fundamental issues unchanged.

```
WHEN A COUNTER OFFER MIGHT MAKE SENSE:

□ Your ONLY issue was compensation
  (And you genuinely believe that)

□ The counter includes real structural changes
  (Promotion, team change, not just money)

□ You were on the fence about leaving anyway
  (The other offer was just "good enough" to look)

□ You truly love the job, team, and company
  (Just wanted more money/recognition)

□ The new opportunity has red flags you're ignoring
  (Be honest with yourself)

WHEN TO DECLINE THE COUNTER:

□ You have cultural or values misalignment
  (Money won't fix this)

□ You have issues with your manager
  (They're still your manager)

□ You've lost trust in leadership
  (Counter offer doesn't change this)

□ You're burned out
  (More money doesn't prevent burnout)

□ The new opportunity is genuinely better for your career
  (Don't let fear hold you back)
```

### Declining the Counter Offer

If you decide to decline the counter offer, do so firmly but graciously. Wavering or leaving the door open creates false hope for your manager and makes the remaining notice period awkward for everyone.

```
HOW TO DECLINE GRACEFULLY:

SCRIPT:
"I really appreciate the counter offer, and it means a lot
that you want me to stay. I've thought about it carefully,
but I've decided to proceed with my resignation. My decision
is about [career direction/opportunity type], not compensation.

I hope you understand, and I want to make sure these final
[X weeks] are as productive as possible for the team."

KEY ELEMENTS:
✓ Express genuine appreciation
✓ Be firm (don't waver)
✓ Don't negotiate the counter offer (slippery slope)
✓ Redirect to transition
✓ Don't over-explain

WHAT NOT TO DO:
✗ Use counter offer to negotiate with new employer
  (Unethical, burns both bridges)

✗ Accept then renege later
  (Worst of all worlds)

✗ Drag out the decision
  (Creates uncertainty for everyone)

✗ Pit the two against each other for maximum extraction
  (Short-term gain, long-term reputation damage)
```

### Junior → FAANG: Counter Offer Decision Framework for Early Career

A counter offer from your current employer should trigger one specific question: would I have been satisfied with my current role if they had offered me this compensation six months ago, before I started looking. If the answer is yes, the counter offer addresses the underlying reason you were looking and is worth evaluating seriously. If the answer is no, the counter offer does not address the underlying reason and accepting it trades a short-term compensation gain for a delayed transition to a better opportunity.

The practical consideration: companies sometimes use counter offers as retention tactics with no genuine change to the conditions that drove you to look. If your reason for leaving was not compensation but scope, management, career trajectory, or culture, a counter offer that only addresses compensation does not solve your actual problem.

### Senior @ FAANG: Counter Offer Analysis at Senior Level and the Strategic Timing Question

At senior level, counter offers are more financially significant and more strategically complex. A counter offer at L6 may include a level change, an accelerated equity refresh, or a significant restructuring of your scope and reporting relationship, not just a compensation adjustment. These are meaningful changes that warrant serious evaluation rather than reflexive decline.

The analytical framework: evaluate the counter offer against the same dimensions as any external offer. Total compensation over four years, role scope and mandate, team quality, organizational trajectory, and career optionality. If the counter offer is superior on the dimensions that drove you to look, accepting it is a rational decision. If it addresses compensation but not scope or trajectory, accepting it is a financial optimization that delays a career optimization.

The governance consideration: accepting a counter offer and then leaving six to twelve months later anyway is a pattern that damages your relationship with your manager and your organizational reputation significantly. Before accepting a counter offer, assess honestly whether the reasons you were looking will persist. If you are uncertain, declining and moving forward is usually the cleaner outcome.
