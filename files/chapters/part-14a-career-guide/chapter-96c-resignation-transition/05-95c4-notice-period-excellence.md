## 96C.4 Notice Period Excellence

### Creating a Transition Plan

A well-structured transition plan is the most professional thing you can do during your notice period. It protects your reputation, helps your team, and ensures nothing falls through the cracks after you leave. Present this document to your manager within the first two days of your notice period.

### Notice Period Schedule

Counter-offers frequently trigger regret regardless of whether they are accepted, because the underlying reasons for leaving typically remain unaddressed. The following framework helps candidates evaluate counter-offers objectively and provides language for declining gracefully without prolonging the process.

```
SAMPLE 2-WEEK NOTICE SCHEDULE:

WEEK 1:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  DAY 1-2 (Mon-Tue):                                              │
│  ├── Complete transition document                                │
│  ├── Meet with manager to review transition plan                 │
│  ├── Identify knowledge transfer sessions needed                 │
│  └── Start documentation of undocumented processes               │
│                                                                  │
│  DAY 3-4 (Wed-Thu):                                              │
│  ├── Conduct knowledge transfer sessions                         │
│  ├── Document remaining projects                                 │
│  ├── Hand off ongoing work                                       │
│  └── Schedule final 1:1s with key people                         │
│                                                                  │
│  DAY 5 (Fri):                                                    │
│  ├── Team announcement (if not already done)                     │
│  ├── Answer questions from team                                  │
│  └── Continue documentation                                      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

WEEK 2:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  DAY 6-8 (Mon-Wed):                                              │
│  ├── Complete remaining knowledge transfer                       │
│  ├── Finish documentation                                        │
│  ├── Final stakeholder meetings                                  │
│  └── Tie up loose ends                                           │
│                                                                  │
│  DAY 9 (Thu):                                                    │
│  ├── Exit interview (if scheduled)                               │
│  ├── Return equipment                                            │
│  ├── Final 1:1s                                                  │
│  └── Backup personal files (with permission)                     │
│                                                                  │
│  DAY 10 (Fri - Last Day):                                        │
│  ├── Final equipment return                                      │

│  ├── Exchange personal contact info                              │
│  └── Exit gracefully                                             │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Avoiding Short-Timer Syndrome

The temptation to mentally check out once you have given notice is strong. Resist it. How you perform during your last two weeks shapes your professional reputation more than you expect, because it is the most recent memory your colleagues will have of you.

```
SHORT-TIMER SYNDROME: The temptation to check out mentally

SYMPTOMS:
- Arriving late, leaving early
- Skipping meetings
- Ignoring emails
- Declining to help with new issues
- Excessive socializing
- Mental vacation before actual departure

WHY IT MATTERS:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  YOUR REPUTATION                                                 │
│  ├── Last impressions matter as much as first impressions        │
│  ├── People remember how you left                                │
│  ├── Former colleagues become future references                  │
│  ├── Industry is smaller than you think                          │
│  └── You might work with these people again                      │
│                                                                  │
│  PROFESSIONAL INTEGRITY                                          │
│  ├── You're being paid through your last day                     │
│  ├── Honor your commitments                                      │
│  └── Leave something better than you found it                    │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

STAY ENGAGED BY:
- Set specific goals for your notice period
- Focus on helping your team succeed after you leave
- Take pride in smooth handoffs
- Maintain your normal work standards
```

### Junior → FAANG: The Notice Period as Your Professional Exit Statement

Your behavior during the notice period is observed and remembered. Engineers who become disengaged, reduce their work standards, or become negative during their last two weeks are remembered that way, regardless of how strong their performance was before. The engineers remembered as excellent colleagues are the ones who maintained engagement, delivered thorough handoffs, and left every interaction on a positive note.

Treat your notice period as a focused sprint with a specific deliverable list. Document everything you own that your replacement or team will need. Update runbooks and documentation you have been meaning to improve. Pair program on your most complex responsibilities. This work has immediate value for your team and lasting value for your reputation.

### Senior @ FAANG: Notice Period as Organizational Investment at Senior Level

At senior level, the notice period is the execution window for the transition plan you designed before giving notice. Prioritize: knowledge transfer for the highest-risk items first (critical operational knowledge that only you hold), then relationship introductions (connecting key external contacts to your manager or successor), then documentation (design context, historical decisions, architectural rationale not captured elsewhere).

Have an explicit conversation with your manager about your transition priorities so time is allocated correctly. If your manager asks you to focus on shipping new features during your notice period, present the tradeoff directly: "I want to make sure the highest-risk knowledge transfer items are covered before I leave. Can we align on the priority between new feature work and transition documentation?" Most managers presented with this choice will prioritize transition work.
