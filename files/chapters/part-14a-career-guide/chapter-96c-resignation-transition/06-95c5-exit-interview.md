## 96C.5 Exit Interview

### Exit Interview Strategy

The exit interview has asymmetric risk: honesty helps the org, but burned bridges follow you. Play it deliberately:

```
THE EXIT INTERVIEW:

WHO CONDUCTS IT:
Usually HR, sometimes your manager, occasionally skip-level

PURPOSE (For Company):
- Understand why people leave
- Identify patterns/problems
- Legal protection
- Process improvement

PURPOSE (For You):
- Closure
- Provide constructive feedback
- Maintain good relationship
- Ensure smooth offboarding

GENERAL APPROACH:
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                  │
│  BE:                     DON'T BE:                               │
│  ├── Diplomatic          ├── Brutally honest                     │
│  ├── Constructive        ├── Vindictive                          │
│  ├── Forward-looking     ├── Focused on grievances               │
│  ├── Brief               ├── Rambling                            │
│  └── Professional        └── Emotional                           │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### What to Share (And What Not To)

```
SAFE TO SHARE:

✓ General career growth rationale
  "I found an opportunity that better aligns with my
  long-term career goals."

✓ Positive experiences
  "I really valued the mentorship I received from [person]."

✓ Constructive, systemic feedback
  "The on-call rotation is quite demanding. The team might
  benefit from additional headcount or tooling."

✓ Process improvement suggestions
  "The promotion process could be more transparent."

---

AVOID SHARING:

✗ Personal conflicts with specific individuals
  (This becomes documentation that could cause problems)

✗ Compensation details of new job
  (Not relevant, invites comparison)

✗ Harsh criticism of management
  (Burns bridges, rarely leads to change)

✗ Confidential information about new employer
  (Inappropriate, may violate new NDA)

✗ Anything you wouldn't want in writing
  (Assume it will be documented and potentially shared)
```

### Exit Interview Script

Prepared language keeps a charged conversation professional:

```
SAMPLE RESPONSES TO COMMON QUESTIONS:

"Why are you leaving?"
→ "I received an opportunity that aligns well with my
   long-term career goals. It was a difficult decision
   because I've enjoyed my time here."

"What could we have done to keep you?"
→ "Honestly, this was more about the pull of the new
   opportunity than any push from here."
   (Even if not entirely true, this is diplomatic)

"What did you like most about working here?"
→ Be genuine. Mention specific positives: people,
   projects, learning, culture aspects you appreciated.

"What could the company improve?"
→ Choose ONE constructive item, frame it helpfully:
   "One thing that might help retention is [specific,
   actionable suggestion]."

"Would you recommend this company to others?"
→ "Yes, I've had a positive experience and grown a lot
   here. I'd recommend it to the right candidate."
   (Be honest but diplomatic)

"Is there anything else you'd like to share?"
→ "Just gratitude for the experience. I've learned a
   lot and wish everyone the best."
```

### Junior → FAANG: Exit Interview Strategy for Early Career

Exit interviews at FAANG companies are conducted by HR and are designed to collect honest feedback for organizational improvement. They are not adversarial conversations, but they are also not the place to process every grievance from your tenure. The strategic approach: be honest and constructive, not comprehensive and candid. Identify one or two genuine improvement opportunities framed positively ("I found X challenging, and I think a structural change to Y might help future engineers") and leave the rest unsaid.

Your exit interview feedback may be surfaced to your manager. Most engineers discover this after the fact and wish they had been more measured. Assume that anything you say could be shared, and calibrate your feedback accordingly.

### Senior @ FAANG: Exit Interview as Organizational Contribution at Senior Level

At senior level, the exit interview is an opportunity to provide genuinely useful organizational feedback from a position of experience and perspective. Senior engineers who have been at a company for three or more years have developed views about systemic challenges that junior engineers do not have the context to articulate. This feedback, given constructively and specifically, can produce real organizational improvements.

The governance principle: if you have significant negative feedback about a manager, a team, or an organizational process, assess whether the exit interview is the right venue or whether the feedback would be more actionable addressed directly with the relevant party while you are still employed. Feedback given in an exit interview produces HR documentation. Feedback given directly to a manager or skip-level during your notice period produces more immediate organizational change.

For senior engineers with strong existing relationships with organizational leadership: a separate conversation with a director or VP during your notice period is often more impactful than the formal exit interview. The conversation should be framed explicitly as a senior peer-to-peer debrief rather than a complaint, and it should focus on organizational system issues rather than individual people.
