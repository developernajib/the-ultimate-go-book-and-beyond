## 93.6 After the Interview

### Following Up

```
SAME DAY:
Send thank-you email to recruiter (who can forward to team)

---

Subject: Thank you - [Role] Interview

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for arranging today's interviews for the
[Position] role. I really enjoyed meeting the team
and learning more about [specific thing discussed].

The conversation with [Interviewer Name] about [topic]
was particularly interesting, and it reinforced my
excitement about the opportunity.

Please let me know if there's any additional information
I can provide. I look forward to hearing about next steps.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Handling the Wait

```
TYPICAL TIMELINE:
• Same week: Sometimes
• 1 week: Common
• 2 weeks: Still normal
• 3+ weeks: Follow up

FOLLOW-UP EMAIL (after 1 week):

Subject: Following up - [Role] Interview

Hi [Recruiter Name],

I hope you're doing well! I wanted to follow up on
my interview last [day] for the [Position] role.

I'm still very excited about the opportunity and
would love to hear about next steps when you have
an update.

Please let me know if there's anything else you
need from my side.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

### Handling Rejection

```
IF REJECTED:

1. THANK THEM
   "Thank you for letting me know. I appreciated the
   opportunity to interview and learn about the team."

2. ASK FOR FEEDBACK
   "If possible, could you share any feedback on areas
   where I could improve?"

3. KEEP THE DOOR OPEN
   "I'd love to stay in touch for future opportunities.
   Is it okay if I check back in 6 months?"

4. REFLECT
   • What went well?
   • What could have gone better?
   • What will you do differently next time?

5. MOVE ON
   • Don't take it personally
   • Right fit matters on both sides
   • Use it as learning experience
```

### Junior → FAANG: Post-Interview Habits That Build Compounding Advantage

Most early-career candidates treat the post-interview period as passive waiting. The candidates who consistently land offers treat it as an active phase with specific tasks.

Within 24 hours: send a brief, specific thank-you note to your recruiter for each loop. Reference one concrete thing from the conversation to make it non-generic. This is read by the recruiter and sometimes forwarded. It costs five minutes.

Within 48 hours: write down every question you were asked and whether your answer felt strong or weak. This is your study guide for the next loop. The questions that felt weak are the ones to rehearse before the next interview, while the problem is fresh and the gap is clear.

On rejection: ask your recruiter for feedback. FAANG companies have policies that limit the specificity of feedback they can provide, but the recruiter will sometimes tell you which round was weakest, which is enough to direct your next preparation cycle. Treating rejection as data rather than judgment is the habit that separates candidates who get offers on the second or third attempt from candidates who repeat the same preparation and get the same result.

### Senior @ FAANG: Debrief Dynamics and the Hiring Committee Process

After a senior-level loop, the debrief and hiring committee process operates in ways most candidates do not understand. Each interviewer submits a written assessment and a hire/no-hire recommendation before the debrief meeting. Strong hire from one interviewer cannot override no-hire from another in a simple averaging model: the hiring committee discusses each round, and a single strong no-hire with clear reasoning can sink an otherwise positive packet.

Understanding this changes what matters in each round. You cannot afford a round where an interviewer walks away genuinely uncertain about your level. Ambiguity at senior level is resolved downward. If an interviewer says "I think this candidate is strong but I'm not sure they're L6," the hiring committee will level you at L5. The implication is that every round needs to produce enough evidence that the interviewer can write a confident positive assessment, not just a neutral one.

Post-loop follow-up at senior level includes asking your recruiter directly: "Can you share whether the committee had any concerns about scope or level?" This is a standard question and recruiters will often tell you whether there is a leveling discussion happening, which gives you an opportunity to provide additional context via the recruiter before the committee makes a final decision.
