## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Navigate each stage of the interview process
- Prepare effectively for different interview types
- Perform your best under pressure
- Follow up appropriately after interviews
- Handle common challenges and surprises

### Junior → FAANG: Translating Objectives Into Concrete Preparation Milestones

Each objective in this list represents a distinct preparation track. Navigating the pipeline requires understanding stage sequencing. Preparing for different interview types means building separate practice habits: coding problems under time pressure, system design narrated aloud, and behavioral stories structured with specific outcomes. Performing under pressure requires repeated rehearsal in conditions that simulate real pressure, including mock interviews with strangers, not just self-review.

The most commonly skipped objective is "follow up appropriately." A brief, specific thank-you email sent within 24 hours of each interview round is standard at FAANG companies and noticed when absent. It should reference something specific from the conversation, not a generic template. This takes five minutes and has a measurable positive effect on how interviewers feel about the interaction when they write their feedback.

### Senior @ FAANG: Reframing Objectives for L6 Evaluation Criteria

At senior level, "navigating the pipeline" means understanding how the hiring committee reads the packet, not just how to pass each round. A strong candidate at L6 understands that written interviewer feedback is scored on a calibrated rubric, and that "strong hire" is only achievable when you demonstrate scope, judgment, and cross-team influence in your answers. Performing well in one dimension while being average in others will cap your leveling even if no round is outright poor.

"Handling common challenges and surprises" at senior level specifically includes being leveled down during the offer process. This happens when the hiring committee sees a candidate with strong technical execution but no evidence of organizational scope. Candidates who are surprised by a level-down typically did not prepare behavioral stories that demonstrated impact beyond their immediate team. The objective to prepare for is not managing logistics surprises but managing the leveling conversation, including how to push back with evidence when the proposed level does not match the scope you have operated at.
