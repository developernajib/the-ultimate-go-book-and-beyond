## 93.5 Interview Day Logistics

### Day Before

The evening before your interview is not the time for last-minute study, it is the time to consolidate what you already know and eliminate logistical uncertainty. Running through this checklist the night before means that on interview day your mental energy goes entirely to performing, not to remembering whether you confirmed the meeting link or charged your laptop.

```
PREPARATION CHECKLIST:

□ Review company research notes
□ Review your prepared stories
□ Prepare questions for each interviewer
□ Confirm time and logistics
□ Test technical setup (if virtual)
□ Get good sleep (8 hours)
□ Prepare clothes (if in-person)
□ Plan transportation (if in-person)
```

### Day Of

Interview performance degrades sharply when basic physical needs, sleep, food, hydration, are neglected, so treat the morning routine as part of the preparation itself. For virtual interviews, small environmental details like camera height and background lighting have an outsized effect on how interviewers perceive your professionalism and engagement. Check them before the first call starts rather than adjusting mid-conversation.

```
MORNING:
□ Wake up with time to spare
□ Eat a good breakfast
□ Review key notes briefly
□ Don't try to cram new material
□ Arrive 10-15 minutes early

DURING:
□ Breathe - nerves are normal
□ Take your time answering
□ Ask for clarification when needed
□ Stay hydrated
□ Use breaks to reset mentally

VIRTUAL SPECIFICS:
□ Camera at eye level
□ Good lighting on your face
□ Neutral background
□ Close other applications
□ Have water nearby
□ Keep notes accessible but don't read from them
```

### Between Sessions

Each interview in a loop is evaluated independently, so carrying anxiety or frustration from one round into the next is one of the most common ways candidates undermine an otherwise strong performance. Use every break as a hard reset, a brief physical routine (water, a few deep breaths, a short walk) is more effective than mentally replaying what you just said.

```
USE BREAKS TO:
• Use the bathroom
• Drink water
• Take a few deep breaths
• Clear your mind from previous interview
• Don't dwell on mistakes

DON'T:
• Check your phone for work
• Stress about previous interviews
• Try to look up answers
• Complain or gossip about interviewers
```

### Junior → FAANG: Interview Day Operations and Energy Management

Interview day logistics are not trivial and treating them as an afterthought costs candidates performance in the first round. The most common preventable failures are: technical setup issues in virtual interviews that consume the first five minutes and create visible stress, unfamiliarity with the coding environment that slows down an otherwise capable candidate, and poor energy management that leads to a strong start and a weak finish.

For virtual onsites specifically: test your camera, microphone, and shared coding environment the day before. Have a wired internet connection if possible. Keep a glass of water within reach. Know how to share your screen before the interview starts. These sound trivial because they are, but forgetting them on interview day is not.

Between rounds in a virtual onsite: stand up, walk around the room for two minutes, do not scroll social media or re-read your previous answers. Physical movement is the most effective cognitive reset available. Mental review of what went wrong in the previous round is the least effective and the most common mistake.

### Senior @ FAANG: Logistics at Senior Level and the Multi-Company Loop

Senior candidates often run multiple onsite loops simultaneously, which creates scheduling and energy management challenges that require explicit planning. Running three FAANG onsites in the same two-week window is possible but requires intentional sequencing: start with companies where you are least excited to build practice reps, finish with your top choice when your performance is most polished.

The governance consideration at senior level is offer timeline management. FAANG offer windows are typically 48 to 72 hours once made. If you have loops at multiple companies and receive one offer before others are complete, you need to negotiate timeline extensions with the offering company while accelerating the remaining pipelines. This requires explicit communication with each recruiting contact about your timeline, which is a standard and accepted practice. Asking for a one-week extension on an offer while waiting for a competing loop to complete is routine at L6. Doing it without any prior communication about your timeline is not.
