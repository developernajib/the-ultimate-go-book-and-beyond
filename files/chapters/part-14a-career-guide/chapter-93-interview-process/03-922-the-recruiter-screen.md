## 93.2 The Recruiter Screen

### What to Expect

```
TYPICAL FLOW:

1. INTRODUCTION (5 min)
   Recruiter introduces themselves and company

2. YOUR BACKGROUND (10-15 min)
   "Tell me about yourself"
   "Walk me through your experience"

3. ROLE DETAILS (5-10 min)
   Team, responsibilities, challenges

4. LOGISTICS (5-10 min)
   Compensation, timeline, work authorization

5. YOUR QUESTIONS (5-10 min)
   Opportunity to learn about role/company
```

### Common Questions and Answers

```
Q: "Tell me about yourself"

STRUCTURE (2 minutes max):
Present → Past → Future

"I'm currently a Senior Engineer at [Company] where I lead
the payments team. We build systems handling $50M annually.

Before this, I spent 3 years at [Previous], where I grew
from mid to senior level, leading the migration to microservices.

I'm now looking for a role where I can work on larger-scale
distributed systems and grow toward staff level. [Company]'s
work on [specific thing] is really exciting to me because..."

---

Q: "Why are you looking to leave?"

GOOD:
• "I'm looking for larger scale challenges"
• "I want to work on [specific technology/domain]"
• "Ready for the next step in my career"

AVOID:
• Badmouthing current employer
• "I'm bored" (sounds disengaged)
• Pure salary motivation

---

Q: "What are you looking for in your next role?"

BE SPECIFIC:
"I'm looking for three main things: First, technical
challenges at scale - I want to work on systems handling
millions of users. Second, a strong engineering culture
with emphasis on quality and learning. Third, growth
opportunity toward staff-level responsibilities."
```

### Compensation Discussion

Compensation negotiation is a professional skill that engineers often underprepare for, leaving significant value on the table. The framework below provides structured language and decision criteria for evaluating and responding to offers effectively.

```
IF ASKED ABOUT EXPECTATIONS:

DEFLECT IF POSSIBLE:
"I'm flexible on compensation and more focused on finding
the right role. Can you share the range for this position?"

IF PRESSED:
"Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting the
$X-Y range for total compensation. I'm open to discussing
how that breaks down between base, equity, and bonus."

RESEARCH BEFORE:
• levels.fyi for role at company
• Glassdoor for ranges
• Talk to people in network
```

### Junior → FAANG: What the Recruiter Screen Is Actually Testing

The recruiter screen is not a technical evaluation, but it is a credibility filter. Recruiters are assessing three things: whether your background is plausible for the role, whether you can articulate your experience clearly, and whether you are serious about the company specifically. Candidates who stumble on "tell me about yourself" or who cannot explain why they want this role at this company are screened out before any technical evaluation begins.

Prepare compensation research before the call using levels.fyi, not just Glassdoor. Giving a range anchored to market data for the specific role and level signals that you are a serious candidate who understands how the market works. Saying "I'm flexible, whatever is fair" signals inexperience and often results in a lower initial offer even if you advance.

Practical habit: write a three-sentence version of your background and practice it until it comes out naturally. It should cover your current role and impact, what you are looking for next, and one specific reason you are excited about this company. Three sentences, practiced, sounds confident. Twenty unstructured sentences sounds like anxiety.

### Senior @ FAANG: Recruiter Screen Signals at L6 and the Leveling Conversation

At senior level, the recruiter screen often includes a leveling conversation that most candidates are unprepared for. The recruiter will ask questions like "what scope of impact have you had at your current role" and "are you looking for an IC or management track." Your answers to these questions determine how the role and level are described to the hiring manager before the technical loop begins, which has downstream effects on how you are evaluated.

The governance principle here is that leveling is negotiated, not assigned. A recruiter who believes you are a strong L5 will frame you as an L5 candidate to the hiring team. If you know you have operated at L6 scope, you need to communicate that clearly and specifically during the screen, with evidence: cross-team projects you drove, technical decisions that affected organizational direction, engineers you mentored who grew in scope as a result. If you do not make this case during the screen, you will be evaluated at the level the recruiter assumed.

Senior candidates should also treat the recruiter as a source of intelligence about the team and role. Ask what problems the team is trying to solve in the next six to twelve months, what the engineering culture is like relative to other teams at the company, and whether the role is backfill or new headcount. This information shapes how you frame your experience in subsequent rounds.
