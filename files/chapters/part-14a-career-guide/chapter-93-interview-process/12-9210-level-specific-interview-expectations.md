## 93.11 Level-Specific Interview Expectations

What interviewers expect varies significantly by level. Understanding these expectations helps you calibrate your preparation and responses.

### Junior Engineer (0-2 Years)

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         JUNIOR ENGINEER INTERVIEW EXPECTATIONS                   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                  │
│  CODING INTERVIEWS:                                              │
│  ├── Focus: Easy to medium LeetCode-style problems               │
│  ├── Expected: Solve with guidance, communicate thought process  │
│  ├── Acceptable: Need hints, not fully optimal solution          │
│  ├── Good sign: Ask clarifying questions, test your code         │
│  └── Red flag: Can't solve easy problems even with hints         │
│                                                                  │
│  TECHNICAL KNOWLEDGE:                                            │
│  ├── Fundamentals: data structures, algorithms basics            │
│  ├── Language: basic syntax and idioms of chosen language        │
│  ├── Depth: not expected to know system design                   │
│  └── Growth: willingness to learn valued highly                  │
│                                                                  │
│  BEHAVIORAL:                                                     │
│  ├── School projects count as experience                         │
│  ├── Internships, bootcamp projects, personal projects           │
│  ├── Focus on: learning ability, collaboration, enthusiasm       │
│  └── Less emphasis on leadership experience                      │
│                                                                  │
│  WHAT INTERVIEWERS FORGIVE:                                      │
│  ├── Not knowing advanced topics                                 │
│  ├── Needing hints on problems                                   │
│  ├── Limited professional experience                             │
│  ├── Nervousness (especially first job)                          │
│  └── Not knowing company-specific technologies                   │
│                                                                  │
│  WHAT INTERVIEWERS LOOK FOR:                                     │
│  ├── Strong fundamentals                                         │
│  ├── Ability to learn quickly                                    │
│  ├── Clear communication                                         │
│  ├── Enthusiasm for engineering                                  │
│  └── Culture fit and coachability                                │
│                                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

PREPARATION FOCUS:
- LeetCode easy and medium problems (100-150 problems)
- Data structures: arrays, hash maps, trees, graphs
- Algorithms: sorting, searching, BFS/DFS, basic DP
- One language deeply (for Go: goroutines, channels, error handling)
- 3-4 behavioral stories (school, internship, personal projects)
```

│                                                                  │

│  └── Handling ambiguity and making decisions                     │

### Junior → FAANG: Using Level Expectations to Calibrate Your Target

The level expectations in this section are your calibration tool, not just your preparation guide. If you read the L4 expectations and feel confident, and you read the L5 expectations and feel uncertain, you have identified where your preparation gap is. The gap is usually not in technical knowledge: it is in the articulation of impact and scope. An L4-to-L5 candidate gap at most FAANG companies is the difference between "I implemented this feature" and "I owned this feature end-to-end, including defining the requirements, designing the implementation, shipping it, and measuring the outcome."

Use this information to calibrate which level to target in each application. If you are currently at mid-level and targeting L5, focus your behavioral preparation on scope expansion: stories where you went beyond what was asked, proposed improvements to adjacent systems, or took ownership of something that was not clearly assigned. These are the signals that move a candidate from solid L4 to credible L5.

### Senior @ FAANG: Level Expectations as a Governance Framework for Career Planning

At senior level, the level expectation table is a governance document for your career trajectory, not just an interview reference. The specific expectations at each level describe the operating model for that role within the organization. L6 expectations describe an engineer who operates as a technical authority across a team domain. L7 expectations describe an engineer who defines technical direction across multiple teams and influences organizational strategy. Understanding these expectations lets you assess whether your current role gives you the scope to develop and demonstrate L7-level capabilities, or whether you need to change your scope within your current company before targeting a staff-level role externally.

A practical maturity model exercise: for each L6 or L7 expectation in the table, write down a specific example from your recent work that demonstrates it. If you cannot produce an example for a particular expectation, that expectation represents a genuine gap. The choice is whether to develop that capability in your current role before interviewing, or to interview now and accept that you may be leveled at the lower tier. Both are valid strategies depending on your goals and timeline. The key is making the choice explicitly rather than being surprised during the offer process.
