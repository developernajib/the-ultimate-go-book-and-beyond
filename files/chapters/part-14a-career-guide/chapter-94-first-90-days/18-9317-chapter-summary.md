## Summary

Your first 90 days are a unique window of opportunity that does not come again at the same company. The habits you build, the relationships you form, and the reputation you establish during this period will shape your trajectory for years to come. Use the frameworks and checklists below as a quick reference to keep yourself on track throughout each phase of your onboarding journey.

### Key Takeaways

1. **Have a plan** - Structure your first 90 days intentionally
2. **Build relationships** - People matter as much as code
3. **Learn before changing** - Understand before proposing
4. **Communicate proactively** - Don't wait to be asked
5. **Seek feedback early** - Course correct quickly

### First 90 Days Checklist

**Month 1:**
- [ ] Development environment working
- [ ] Met everyone on team
- [ ] Understand key systems
- [ ] Completed first task

**Month 2:**
- [ ] Regular contributor
- [ ] Key stakeholder relationships
- [ ] Participating in reviews
- [ ] Comfortable with workflow

**Month 3:**
- [ ] Delivered significant work
- [ ] Positive feedback received
- [ ] Clear on expectations
- [ ] Feeling like part of the team

---

### Junior → FAANG: Summary as a Habit Baseline for the Rest of Year One

Use the checklist in this summary as a weekly review tool for your entire first year, not just the first 90 days. The habits it describes (delivering work, getting feedback, building relationships, feeling integrated) do not stop mattering after day 90. They are the operational baseline for a sustainable first-year trajectory.

The most important single habit to carry from this chapter: start your brag document on day one and update it weekly. A brag document is a running list of everything you have contributed, learned, and shipped, with enough specificity to be useful as performance review material. Engineers who maintain this document throughout year one enter their first performance review with a complete record. Engineers who do not maintain it spend the week before their review trying to reconstruct what they did six months ago from memory and commit logs.

### Senior @ FAANG: Summary as a Platform-Building Framework for Years One Through Three

At senior level, the first-90-days summary is the starting point for a multi-year organizational building arc. The relationships you built, the technical assessments you formed, and the early wins you shipped in the first 90 days are the foundation for the technical leadership work you will drive in the following two to three years.

The governance principle that connects first-90-days work to long-term senior effectiveness: the credibility you build in your first 90 days is the organizational capital you spend in your second year when you drive significant technical changes. Engineers who invest deeply in the first-90-days relationship and credibility building have more organizational capital to spend on ambitious technical work in year two. Engineers who skip this investment and try to drive change from day one without organizational context typically encounter resistance that compounds over time.

The maturity model for senior first-year outcomes: by the end of month 12, a successful L6 first year should include having driven at least one significant technical decision to completion with cross-team alignment, having produced at least one artifact (design document, platform capability, or technical standard) that other teams use without your direct involvement, and having established a visible reputation for sound judgment in your technical domain. If these outcomes are not on track by month nine, the time to change course is before the first annual review, not after it.
