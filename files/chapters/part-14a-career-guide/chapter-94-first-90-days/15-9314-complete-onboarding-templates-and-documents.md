## 94.14 Complete Onboarding Templates and Documents

Having ready-made templates saves you time and ensures you do not miss important steps during your onboarding. The templates in this section are designed to be copied and customized for your specific role and company. They cover your personal 30-60-90 day plan, guidance for onboarding buddies, and a learning log for tracking your progress. Even if your company provides its own onboarding documents, these templates fill in the gaps that most formal programs leave out.

### 30-60-90 Day Plan Template

A 30-60-90 day plan transforms vague onboarding expectations into concrete, time-boxed commitments that both you and your manager can reference. Structuring goals across three phases forces you to distinguish between learning (days 1-30), contributing (days 31-60), and leading (days 61-90), which prevents the common mistake of trying to drive impact before you understand the system. Sharing a written version of this plan with your manager in your first week signals ownership and creates an early feedback loop.

```markdown
# 30-60-90 Day Plan Template

## Personal Information
- Name: [Your Name]
- Role: [Your Title]
- Manager: [Manager Name]
- Start Date: [Date]
- Buddy: [Buddy Name]

---

## Days 1-30: LEARN

### Goals:
1. Fully functional development environment
2. Understand team's domain and responsibilities
3. Build relationships with immediate team
4. Complete first small contribution
5. Understand key systems and processes

### Week 1 Objectives:
| Day | Focus | Deliverables |
|-----|-------|--------------|
| 1 | Setup | Laptop, accounts, first team meeting |
| 2 | Access | Repositories, tools, documentation |
| 3 | Learning | Read team docs, explore codebase |
| 4 | Building | Dev environment working locally |
| 5 | Connection | 1:1s scheduled, first PR started |

### Week 2-4 Objectives:
| Week | Focus | Key Activities |
|------|-------|----------------|
| 2 | Deepen | First PR merged, code reviews started |
| 3 | Contribute | Second task, team meeting participation |
| 4 | Assess | 30-day check-in, gather feedback |

### 30-Day Checklist:
- [ ] Development environment fully working
- [ ] Met all immediate team members (1:1)
- [ ] Understand team's main responsibilities
- [ ] Completed first small task/PR
- [ ] Know who to ask for different questions
- [ ] Attended all recurring team meetings
- [ ] Understand sprint/development process
- [ ] Can navigate key systems

### 30-Day Check-in Questions:
1. How am I tracking against expectations?
2. What should I focus on next?
3. Any concerns about my ramp-up?
4. What feedback do you have?

---

## Days 31-60: CONTRIBUTE

### Goals:
1. Regular, independent contributions
2. Active code review participation
3. Deeper domain understanding
4. Broader organizational relationships
5. Identify first improvement opportunity

### Week 5-8 Focus Areas:
| Week | Focus | Key Activities |
|------|-------|----------------|
| 5 | Independence | Take on medium task independently |
| 6 | Review | Meaningful code review contributions |
| 7 | Breadth | Meet stakeholders outside team |
| 8 | Assessment | 60-day check-in, feedback loop |

### 60-Day Checklist:
- [ ] Completed 3+ meaningful PRs
- [ ] Giving useful code reviews
- [ ] Met key stakeholders (PM, adjacent teams)
- [ ] Skip-level 1:1 completed
- [ ] Comfortable with team workflow
- [ ] Can estimate work reasonably
- [ ] Understand on-call responsibilities
- [ ] Identified one improvement to propose

### 60-Day Check-in Questions:
1. Am I contributing at expected level?
2. What's my biggest growth area?
3. Am I working on the right things?
4. What should success look like at 90 days?

---

## Days 61-90: IMPACT

### Goals:
1. Deliver significant project or feature
2. Propose and begin improvement initiative
3. Mentor or help newer team members
4. Clear plan for next quarter
5. Positive feedback from stakeholders

### Week 9-12 Focus Areas:
| Week | Focus | Key Activities |
|------|-------|----------------|
| 9 | Ownership | Lead or significantly contribute to project |
| 10 | Improvement | Propose documented improvement |
| 11 | Leadership | Help others, share learnings |
| 12 | Reflection | 90-day review, next quarter goals |

### 90-Day Checklist:
- [ ] Significant deliverable completed
- [ ] Proposed or started improvement initiative
- [ ] Helped at least one teammate
- [ ] Positive stakeholder feedback
- [ ] Clear goals for next quarter
- [ ] Feel integrated into team
- [ ] On-call capable
- [ ] Can work independently

### 90-Day Review Discussion:
1. Summary of first 90 days
2. Key accomplishments
3. Areas for growth
4. Goals for next quarter
5. Career development discussion
```

### Onboarding Buddy Guide

```markdown
# Onboarding Buddy Guide

## What Is a Buddy?
You're NOT:
- Their manager
- Responsible for their work
- Required to be available 24/7

You ARE:
- A friendly face and safe space
- Someone to ask "dumb" questions
- A guide to unwritten rules
- A social connector

## Your Responsibilities

### Week 1:
- [ ] Meet on Day 1 (before anything else)
- [ ] Give informal tour (virtual or in-person)
- [ ] Explain team norms and culture
- [ ] Introduce them to key people
- [ ] Daily check-in (15 min)

### Week 2-4:
- [ ] 2-3 check-ins per week
- [ ] Answer questions they're embarrassed to ask
- [ ] Introduce them to your network
- [ ] Give honest cultural guidance

### Month 2-3:
- [ ] Weekly check-in
- [ ] Be available for questions
- [ ] Provide feedback if asked
- [ ] Celebrate their wins

## Questions to Ask Your New Hire:

### Week 1:
- "What's confusing so far?"
- "What do you need that you don't have?"
- "Who should you meet that you haven't?"
- "What's stressing you out?"

### Ongoing:
- "What's going well? What isn't?"
- "Any cultural things that seem weird?"
- "How's your relationship with [manager]?"
- "What do you wish you knew sooner?"

## Things to Explain:

### The Unwritten Rules:
- How decisions really get made
- Who has informal influence
- What behaviors are rewarded
- What mistakes to avoid
- Team inside jokes and history

### Practical Stuff:
- Best time to reach different people
- How to get things done quickly
- Where to find information
- When to ask vs. when to figure out

## Buddy Do's and Don'ts:

### DO:
✓ Be available and responsive
✓ Share honest perspectives
✓ Make introductions
✓ Remember what it was like to be new
✓ Proactively check in

### DON'T:
✗ Overwhelm with information
✗ Share excessive negativity
✗ Gossip about colleagues
✗ Make them dependent on you
✗ Take over their relationship with manager
```

### Junior → FAANG: Using Templates as Structure, Not Compliance

The templates in this section are most useful when you treat them as a structure to be adapted, not a checklist to be completed. The 30-60-90 day plan template is valuable because it forces you to make your goals explicit and time-bounded before you start. An explicit plan shared with your manager in your first week is almost universally received positively, because it signals that you are taking ownership of your own onboarding rather than waiting to be told what to do.

The learning log template is the most commonly skipped and highest-value artifact. Engineers who maintain a learning log through their first 90 days have a written record of what they understood and when, which is directly useful when they need to help onboard the next new hire, when they are asked to improve the team's documentation, and when they are preparing for their first performance review and need to recall what they accomplished.

### Senior @ FAANG: Onboarding Templates as Artifacts of Organizational Maturity

At senior level, the onboarding templates in this section represent both a personal tool and a governance artifact. A senior engineer who customizes these templates and shares them with their manager as a structured onboarding agreement is demonstrating the documentation discipline and stakeholder communication habits that L6 requires.

The more significant opportunity: if your new team does not have formal onboarding templates, the process of creating and refining them for your own onboarding is simultaneously personal preparation and organizational improvement. By the end of your first 90 days, you will have produced an onboarding artifact that the team can use for future hires. This is the kind of institutional improvement investment that performance reviews at senior level recognize and reward, and it is available to any new senior hire who approaches onboarding with this framing.
