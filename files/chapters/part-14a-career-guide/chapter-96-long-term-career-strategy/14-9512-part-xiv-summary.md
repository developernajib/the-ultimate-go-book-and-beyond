### Career Success Principles

Sustainable career growth in software engineering is not accidental, it results from applying a consistent set of operating principles across every role and transition. These principles work together as a system: continuous skill-building makes you visible, visibility compounds your reputation, and strong relationships amplify every opportunity that reputation creates. Neglecting any one principle, especially sustainability, tends to collapse the others over time.

```
1. BUILD SKILLS CONTINUOUSLY
   Never stop learning

2. BUILD RELATIONSHIPS INTENTIONALLY
   Your network is your net worth

3. MAKE YOUR WORK VISIBLE
   Impact unseen is impact lost

4. TAKE CARE OF YOURSELF
   Sustainable pace beats burnout

5. THINK LONG-TERM
   Career is a marathon, not a sprint

6. STAY FLEXIBLE
   Plans change, be adaptable

7. HELP OTHERS
   Success is amplified when shared
```

### Senior @ FAANG: Part XIV as a Platform for Career Governance at Senior Level

At senior level, Part XIV describes the operating model for sustainable, high-impact career management across a 20 to 30 year horizon. The themes that run through every chapter are consistent: make decisions with explicit analysis rather than intuition, build organizational capital through genuine contribution, invest in relationships that are maintained rather than activated only at need, and plan for financial independence as a career freedom multiplier.

The governance principle that unifies everything in Part XIV: a career is a complex system with compounding dynamics, and the most important decisions are the ones made earliest in the system. The choices you make in your first two years at a FAANG company (which domain to go deep in, which relationships to invest in, how to manage your compensation and equity, how to build your professional reputation) have effects that compound over decades. Senior engineers who understand this treat early-career investment decisions with the same rigor they bring to system architecture decisions: with explicit consideration of compounding effects, failure modes, and long-term maintainability.
