## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Choose between IC and management career paths
- Develop a long-term career vision
- Build a sustainable career in tech
- Navigate career transitions
- Plan for financial independence

### Junior → FAANG: Making These Objectives Concrete at Early Career

Each objective here is a long-term investment. "Build a sustainable career in tech" at early career means establishing habits now that prevent burnout later: protecting your recovery time, maintaining relationships outside work, and building financial runway that gives you career choices. These habits are much easier to establish at year two than to recover at year seven after burnout.

"Navigate career transitions" at early career means understanding that your first FAANG role will not be your last, and that the skills you build, the relationships you form, and the evidence you generate in each role are the currency for the next transition. Every decision about what to work on should be evaluated partly in terms of what it contributes to your ability to make the next move you want to make.

### Senior @ FAANG: Reframing Objectives for Sustained L6-L7 Career Longevity

At senior level, "develop a long-term career vision" is an organizational strategy exercise as much as a personal one. Your career vision determines what you say yes and no to, and at senior level you are saying yes and no to things that affect teams and organizations. An explicit long-term vision makes these decisions faster and more consistent because they can be evaluated against a stated direction rather than decided case by case.

"Plan for financial independence" at senior level is not abstract: it is a specific timeline with specific milestones. At L6 total compensation, financial independence is achievable within ten to fifteen years for most engineers who invest consistently. Knowing this timeline and working toward it explicitly gives you the career freedom that makes the second half of your career sustainable and fulfilling rather than constrained by economic necessity.
