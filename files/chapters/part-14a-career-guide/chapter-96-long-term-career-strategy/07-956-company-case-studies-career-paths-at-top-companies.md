## 96.6 Company Case Studies: Career Paths at Top Companies

### Google Career Trajectories

Google's engineering ladder is one of the most well-defined in the industry, with clear level expectations from L3 (entry) through L10 (Senior Fellow). Understanding the typical timelines and what distinguishes each level helps you plan your career progression whether you are at Google or benchmarking against it.

```
GOOGLE CAREER PATHS:

IC LADDER:
L3 (SWE II) → L4 (SWE III) → L5 (Senior) → L6 (Staff) →
L7 (Senior Staff) → L8 (Principal) → L9+ (Distinguished/Fellow)

MANAGEMENT LADDER:
TL → Manager (M1) → Senior Manager (M2) → Director →
Senior Director → VP → SVP

TYPICAL GOOGLE CAREER PATTERNS:
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  PATTERN 1: Deep Technical Expert                              │
│  New grad → L3-6 over 8-10 years → Principal (rare)            │
│  Focus: Technical depth, influential systems                   │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 2: Technical Leader + Manager                         │
│  L3-5 → TL → Manager → back to IC (common)                     │
│  Many try management, some return to IC                        │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 3: Career Googler                                     │
│  20+ year career at Google                                     │
│  Multiple teams, multiple roles                                │
│  Deep institutional knowledge                                  │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 4: Exit to Startup                                    │
│  L5-6 → startup co-founder/early engineer                      │
│  Common path after 3-5 years at Google                         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

GOOGLE CAREER TIPS:
- Internal mobility is high - change teams easily
- 20% time exists but varies by team
- Manager 1:1s crucial for growth
- Promo committee is the gatekeeper
```

### Meta Career Evolution

Meta's career paths emphasize impact and velocity over tenure. The E3-E9 ladder rewards engineers who ship high-impact projects and demonstrate increasing scope of influence. Meta is known for faster-than-average promotions at early levels and a steeper bar at Staff and above.

```
META CAREER PATTERNS:

E3 → E4 → E5 → E6 → E7 → E8+

META UNIQUE ASPECTS:
- Bootcamp allows team exploration
- Internal mobility encouraged
- Hackathon projects can become careers
- Fast growth creates opportunities

COMMON META TRAJECTORIES:
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  PATTERN 1: Product Engineer                                   │
│  E3-E5 on product teams → E6 product tech lead                 │
│  Focus: Ship features, user impact                             │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 2: Infrastructure Path                                │
│  E4-E6 on infra → Principal/E7 infrastructure architect        │
│  Focus: Scale, reliability, foundational systems               │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 3: Research → Production                              │
│  Research role → Production ML → Applied science               │
│  AI/ML specific path common at Meta                            │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 4: IC to Founder                                      │
│  E5-E6 → startup founder                                       │
│  Meta alumni often funded by Meta networks                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

META CAREER TIPS:
- Impact is everything
- Visibility to leadership matters
- Bootcamp is a career opportunity
- Network within is valuable
```

### Amazon Career Landscape

Amazon's career structure is built around Leadership Principles and operational ownership. Engineers at Amazon are expected to own systems end-to-end, and career advancement is measured by scope of ownership and demonstrated LP alignment rather than technical specialization alone.

```
AMAZON CAREER STRUCTURE:

SDE I → SDE II → SDE III → Principal → Senior Principal →
Distinguished Engineer → VP/Fellow

AMAZON UNIQUE ASPECTS:
- "Two pizza team" autonomy
- Write documents, not slide decks
- Operational excellence expected
- Leadership Principles are career framework

AMAZON CAREER PATTERNS:
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  PATTERN 1: Service Owner                                      │
│  SDE II → SDE III owning critical service                      │
│  Build expertise in your area, expand scope                    │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 2: Principal Path                                     │
│  SDE III → Principal (5+ years at level is common)             │
│  Requires org-wide impact, very selective                      │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 3: SDM (Management)                                   │
│  SDE II/III → SDM → Senior SDM → Director                      │
│  Strong LP demonstration required                              │
│                                                                │
│  PATTERN 4: AWS Specialist                                     │
│  Deep AWS service expertise                                    │
│  Customer-facing or internal systems                           │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

AMAZON CAREER TIPS:
- LPs are your career framework
- Operational excellence matters
- Writing skills are essential
- Cross-org visibility for Principal+
```

### Startup vs Big Tech Tradeoffs

The choice between startup and big tech is not permanent, and the optimal path depends on your career stage, financial situation, and learning goals. Each environment builds different skills: startups teach breadth and speed, big tech teaches depth and scale. Many successful engineers alternate between the two.

```
CAREER STAGE CONSIDERATIONS:

EARLY CAREER (0-5 years):

│  BIG TECH ADVANTAGES:                                          │
│  - Structured learning and mentorship                          │
│  - Brand name on resume                                        │
│  - Clear ladder and expectations                               │
│  - Exposure to scale                                           │
│                                                                │
│  STARTUP ADVANTAGES:                                           │
│  - Broader responsibility faster                               │
│  - Direct impact visible                                       │
│  - Potential equity upside                                     │
│  - Learn multiple areas                                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

MID CAREER (5-10 years):
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  BIG TECH ADVANTAGES:                                          │

│  - Specialization opportunities                                │

│                                                                │
```

### Junior → FAANG: Reading Career Path Case Studies to Choose Your First Company

The career path case studies in this section are directly useful for choosing where to start your career, because the company you join first shapes the career patterns available to you for the following five to ten years. Google's strong internal mobility and deep technical culture makes it an excellent first company for engineers who want to build foundational systems expertise. Amazon's ownership culture and leadership principle framework makes it an excellent first company for engineers who want to develop strong business instincts alongside technical skills. Meta's high-velocity culture makes it an excellent first company for engineers who thrive under rapid iteration and high stakes.

The practical question: which of these career trajectory patterns aligns with your values and your strengths, and which company's culture would bring out your best work. Choosing based on brand prestige alone without this analysis produces engineers who are miserable at companies everyone else envies.

### Senior @ FAANG: Company Career Path Architecture as a Senior Strategic Variable

At senior level, the company career path case studies reveal the structural constraints that determine what is possible for your career at each company. Google's L6 to L7 promotion path requires organizational scope evidence across multiple teams. Amazon's L7 promotion path requires business impact evidence at scale. Meta's E7 promotion path requires cross-organizational technical influence. These are not interchangeable requirements: the career path that is most achievable at each company depends on the specific kind of scope and influence you have access to.

The governance principle: evaluate each company's career path architecture before committing to a senior-level role there. If the evidence required for the promotion you are targeting is not achievable in the organizational structure of the role you are being offered, you will spend two to three years generating evidence the promotion committee cannot use. Understanding this in advance lets you choose the company where your natural work style and available scope maps to the promotion evidence that is actually required.
