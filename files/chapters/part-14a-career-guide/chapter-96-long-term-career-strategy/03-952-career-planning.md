## 96.2 Career Planning

### The 5-Year Vision

Without a concrete target, career growth defaults to whatever opportunities happen to appear. A 5-year plan forces you to articulate the gap between your current state and your desired state, which in turn reveals the specific skills, roles, and relationships you need to pursue intentionally rather than reactively. The plan is not a rigid contract, it's a navigational tool you revisit annually to recalibrate based on new information about the industry, your interests, and your progress.

```
CAREER PLANNING FRAMEWORK:

1. WHERE ARE YOU NOW?
   • Current role and level
   • Skills and strengths
   • Gaps and development areas

2. WHERE DO YOU WANT TO BE?
   • Role/level in 5 years
   • Type of work
   • Company type/size
   • Work-life balance

3. WHAT'S THE PATH?
   • Skills to develop
   • Experience to gain
   • Relationships to build
   • Milestones to hit

EXAMPLE 5-YEAR PLAN:
Year 1: Senior → Staff at current company
Year 2-3: Staff, expand scope to multi-team
Year 4: Principal or Staff at larger company
Year 5: Distinguished/Principal, external visibility
```

### Skills to Develop Over Time

The skills that get you promoted to senior engineer are not the skills that get you to staff or principal. Technical depth is the primary currency early in a career, but organizational influence, strategic communication, and the ability to raise other engineers become the differentiating factors at higher levels. Deliberately mapping which skills belong to which stage prevents the common trap of continuing to optimize for deep individual contribution long after the leverage point has shifted to breadth and leadership.

```
CAREER STAGE:     KEY SKILLS TO DEVELOP:

Junior:
• Technical fundamentals
• Learning to learn
• Asking good questions
• Basic collaboration

Mid:
• Ownership and autonomy
• Technical breadth
• Mentoring others
• Communication

Senior:
• Technical leadership
• Influence without authority
• System design
• Project leadership

Staff+:
• Organizational impact
• Strategic thinking
• Building consensus
• Developing leaders
```

### Junior → FAANG: Career Planning With a Horizon You Can Actually See

Five-year career plans feel abstract at early career because the landscape changes too fast to plan five years in advance with any confidence. A more useful planning horizon is two years: what do you want to have accomplished and what do you want to have learned by the end of your second year at your current role, and what role would you want to be in three years from now.

This two-year frame produces concrete, actionable goals rather than aspirational visions. "I want to be a senior engineer who has designed and shipped a production distributed system end-to-end" is a two-year goal that you can plan backward from. "I want to be a technical leader" is a vision that does not produce an action plan.

Review your two-year goals quarterly and update them. Your goals at month six will be better informed than your goals at month one, and updating them regularly keeps the plan connected to your actual evolving situation.

### Senior @ FAANG: Career Planning as a Strategic Operating Model at Senior Level

At senior level, career planning becomes a strategic operating model rather than a personal aspiration framework. The specific planning elements that matter at L6 and above: what technical domain are you building foundational authority in, what organizational relationships are you developing that will sustain your influence over a five-year horizon, what external visibility are you building that would make you desirable to hire at a higher level than you currently occupy.

The governance principle for senior career planning: every major initiative you take on should serve at least two of the three dimensions above. An initiative that builds only domain authority without organizational influence or external visibility is an L4 or L5 contribution, regardless of its technical sophistication. Planning your work portfolio explicitly to serve multiple dimensions simultaneously is how L6 engineers generate the breadth of evidence required for L7 promotion.
