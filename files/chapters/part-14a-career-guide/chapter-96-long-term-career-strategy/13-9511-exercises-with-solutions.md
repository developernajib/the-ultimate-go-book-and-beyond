**Task:** Write a 2-page career vision document covering the next 10 years.

**Solution Template:**

```markdown

## Part 1: Where I Am Today

### Current Role:
[Title, Company, Team]

### Technical Strengths:
1. [Strength 1 with evidence]
2. [Strength 2 with evidence]
3. [Strength 3 with evidence]

### Growth Areas:
1. [Area 1 - what would improvement look like?]
2. [Area 2 - what would improvement look like?]

### What I Enjoy Most:
[Be specific about types of work that energize you]

### What I Enjoy Least:
[Be honest about what drains you]

---

## Part 2: Where I Want to Be in 10 Years

### Career Position:
[Specific level/role, not just "senior"]
Example: "Staff+ IC leading technical strategy for a platform
team, with reputation as expert in distributed systems"

### Work Environment:
[Type of company, culture, team size]
Example: "Mid-size tech company (500-5000), engineering-focused
culture, remote-friendly, working on infrastructure"

### Life Situation:
[How work fits into overall life]
Example: "Working sustainable hours, living in [location],
able to attend kids' events, financial independence in sight"

### Impact I Want to Have:
[What legacy do you want to leave?]
Example: "Built systems that handle significant scale,
mentored 5+ engineers to senior+, known for technical writing"

## Part 3: The Path

- Current company: [specific goals]
- Skills to develop: [specific skills]

- Milestone: [measurable outcome]

### Year 4-6: Growth

- New challenges: [what you'll take on]

- Milestone: [measurable outcome]

### Year 7-10: Impact
- Target position: [specific goal]
- Leadership scope: [teams, initiatives]

- Milestone: [measurable outcome]

## Part 4: Risks and Contingencies

### What Could Go Wrong:
1. [Risk 1]: Mitigation: [approach]
2. [Risk 2]: Mitigation: [approach]
3. [Risk 3]: Mitigation: [approach]

### Flexibility Points:
- IC vs Management: [when would you reconsider?]

## Part 5: Review Schedule

### Quarterly: Quick check
- Am I on track?
- What adjustments needed?

- Update entire document
- Revise timeline if needed
- Check if vision still resonates
```

### Junior → FAANG: Exercises as Career Architecture Practice

The exercises in this section are worth completing seriously and keeping as living documents. The career vision document from exercise 1, updated annually, becomes a longitudinal record of how your goals have evolved and a useful input for career conversations with mentors and managers. Engineers who have maintained such documents over five-year periods consistently report that reviewing them provides valuable perspective on their growth and clarifies their current priorities.

The relationship maintenance plan is the exercise most likely to produce immediate tangible returns. Identify three people in your professional network whose relationship you have let go dormant, and send a genuine, specific message to each of them this week. The habit of regular network maintenance, done with genuine warmth rather than transactional intent, produces career dividends for decades.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Exercise Extensions and the 10-Year Career Architecture Review

At senior level, extend the career vision exercise to a 10-year horizon with two alternative scenarios: one where you pursue the staff IC path and one where you pursue the management path. For each scenario, describe the year-by-year evidence milestones, the financial trajectory, the organizational influence you would have, and the aspects of your work you would find most meaningful. The comparison often reveals a clear preference that the binary framing obscures.

The financial independence calculator exercise at senior level should be connected to your actual compensation data, not averages. Use your specific current total compensation, your actual investment rate, and realistic assumptions for your equity appreciation based on your specific RSU grant and vesting schedule. The output of this exercise is your specific financial independence horizon, which is a concrete input to career decisions ranging from when to take a risk on a startup to when you can afford to take a lower-compensation role with higher mission alignment.
