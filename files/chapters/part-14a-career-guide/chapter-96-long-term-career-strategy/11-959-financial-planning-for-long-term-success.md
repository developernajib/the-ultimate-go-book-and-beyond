## 96.10 Financial Planning for Long-Term Success

### Compensation Over a Career

Total compensation in software engineering is highly non-linear: the gap between an L4 and an L6 at a top-tier company often exceeds $300k annually once equity refreshes are factored in, which means promotion timing and company choice dwarf the impact of incremental salary negotiations. Understanding the typical trajectory, base salary, bonus targets, and RSU cliff/vesting schedules at each level, lets you model when switching companies accelerates wealth accumulation versus when staying to collect a vest is the better financial move. The data below provides a benchmark reference so you can evaluate your own offers against market rates with specificity.

```markdown
# TECH CAREER COMPENSATION TRAJECTORY

## Typical Big Tech Progression (2024 dollars):

| Level | Years Exp | Total Compensation |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Junior (L3) | 0-2 | $150-220K |
| Mid (L4) | 2-4 | $200-300K |
| Senior (L5) | 4-8 | $300-450K |
| Staff (L6) | 6-12 | $400-600K |
| Senior Staff (L7) | 10-20 | $500-800K |
| Principal (L8) | 15+ | $700K-1.2M |

Note: Ranges vary by company, location, and individual.
Top of band typically requires multiple strong years.

## Startup Compensation Trade-offs:

### Early Stage (Pre-Series A):
- Base: 50-80% of market
- Equity: 0.1-1%+ of company
- Risk: 90%+ of startups fail

### Growth Stage (Series B-D):
- Base: 80-95% of market
- Equity: 0.01-0.1% of company
- Risk: 50-70% of startups fail

### Late Stage (Pre-IPO):
- Base: Near market
- Equity: Smaller but more certain value
- Risk: Similar to public company

## Equity Wealth Building:

### RSU Strategy (Big Tech):
1. Don't over-concentrate in company stock
2. Sell on vest, diversify
3. Plan for tax implications
4. RSU refreshers compound over time

### Startup Equity Strategy:
1. Don't count it until liquid
2. Understand vesting and cliff
3. Know exercise costs for options
4. Plan for tax (83b, ISO vs NSO)
5. Diversify across multiple bets

### Example Career Wealth Trajectory:

Year 1-5: Save 20-30% of income, build foundation
Year 5-10: RSUs/equity start compounding
Year 10-15: Multiple equity events possible
Year 15-20: Financial independence achievable
Year 20+: Work becomes optional
```

# TAX OPTIMIZATION FOR ENGINEERS

## Annual Tax-Advantaged Accounts (2024):

| Account | Limit | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|

## RSU Tax Strategy:

### Vest Date:
- RSUs taxed as ordinary income when vested
- Withholding often insufficient (22-37% vs actual rate)
- Plan for additional Q4 estimated tax payment

### Sell vs Hold:
- Sell on vest: Avoid concentration risk
- Hold for long-term gains: Only if you believe in stock
- Diversification usually wins

### Exercise vs Hold (Options):
- ISO: Exercise early, start long-term cap gains clock
- NSO: Exercise creates ordinary income immediately
- 83(b) election: Pay tax now on lower value
- AMT implications: Consult tax professional

## State Tax Optimization:

### High-Tax States (CA, NY, etc.):
- Consider remote-friendly employer
- Some companies adjust pay for location
- Relocation before big equity event can save significant taxes

### No-Income-Tax States:
- TX, WA, FL, NV, WY, etc.
- Can save 10%+ on high income
- Trade-off: Different lifestyle, services

## Warning: Get Professional Help
Tax law is complex. The cost of a good CPA/tax attorney
pays for itself many times over at high income levels.

### Junior → FAANG: Building Financial Infrastructure That Scales With Your Compensation

The financial infrastructure you build in year one of your FAANG career will handle compensation that is two to four times larger by year five if you are promoted consistently. Build infrastructure that scales rather than infrastructure that is barely adequate for your current income.

The specific infrastructure items: a brokerage account for taxable investment beyond your retirement accounts, a relationship with a fee-only financial advisor who understands tech compensation (RSUs, options, equity planning), and a basic understanding of the tax treatment of your compensation components. The fee-only advisor relationship is the most important: fee-only means they are paid by you rather than by commissions, which eliminates the conflict of interest that makes commission-based financial advice unreliable.

Automate the financial behaviors that matter: automatic 401K contributions at the maximum, automatic transfers to investment accounts on payday, and automatic RSU vesting reinvestment into diversified index funds. Automation removes the decision point and the temptation to spend the money before investing it.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Financial Architecture and the Equity Optimization Layer

At senior L6 total compensation, the financial decisions that matter most are in the equity and tax optimization layers rather than in the basic investment principles. The difference between optimized and unoptimized equity handling at L6 compensation levels is measurable in hundreds of thousands of dollars over a 10-year tenure.

The specific optimization decisions at senior level: asset location strategy (which investments to hold in taxable versus tax-advantaged accounts to minimize tax drag), RSU diversification timing (selling RSUs systematically as they vest versus holding concentrated employer stock), and tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts to offset capital gains. Each of these requires specialist implementation but produces compounding returns.

The estate planning dimension at senior compensation: at L6 FAANG compensation over a multi-year tenure, engineers accumulate assets that require estate planning tools beyond a simple will. A revocable trust, beneficiary designations on all accounts, and basic estate planning documents (power of attorney, healthcare directive) are minimum baseline at this wealth level. Engage an estate planning attorney and review your documents every three to five years.
