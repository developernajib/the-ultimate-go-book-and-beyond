## 95.5 Company Case Studies: Promotion Processes

### Google's Promotion Process

Google uses a peer-committee review system where the promotion decision is made by engineers at the target level who do not know you personally. This design reduces bias but places enormous weight on your written promotion packet. Understanding how Google's committee reads and evaluates these packets is essential for timing your promotion push correctly.

```
GOOGLE PROMOTION MECHANICS:

1. PROMO COMMITTEE SYSTEM:
   - Self-nomination (you write the packet)
   - Manager nomination and support
   - Committee of peers at target level reviews
   - Committee doesn't know you personally (reduces bias)
   - Decision based on written evidence

2. PROMOTION PACKET STRUCTURE:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  GOOGLE PROMO PACKET                                       │
   │                                                            │
   │  1. SUMMARY: Why you're ready for next level               │
   │                                                            │
   │  2. COMPLEXITY & DIFFICULTY                                │
   │     - Technical challenges solved                          │
   │     - Scope and scale of work                              │
   │                                                            │
   │  3. IMPACT                                                 │
   │     - Quantified outcomes                                  │
   │     - Business/user impact                                 │
   │                                                            │
   │  4. LEADERSHIP                                             │
   │     - How you influenced others                            │
   │     - Mentoring and developing people                      │
   │                                                            │
   │  5. GOOGLE CITIZENSHIP                                     │
   │     - Contributions beyond your role                       │
   │     - Interviews, TL duties, etc.                          │
   │                                                            │
   │  6. PEER & MANAGER FEEDBACK                                │
   │     - Collected separately by committee                    │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

3. TIMELINE:
   - Annual primary cycle (some companies have semi-annual)
   - Packets due ~2 months before decisions
   - Committee reviews take weeks
   - Results communicated by managers

4. GOOGLE PROMO TIPS:
   - Start building your case 6 months before
   - Document impact as it happens
   - Get sponsorship from senior engineers
   - L5→L6 is notably difficult (Staff bar)
```

### Meta's Performance Summary Cycle

Meta runs a semi-annual Performance Summary Cycle (PSC) that combines manager assessment, peer feedback, and cross-team calibration into a single evaluation and promotion process. Understanding how PSC works helps you time your impact and frame your contributions in the language Meta's review system rewards.

```
META (PSC) PROCESS:

1. PERFORMANCE SUMMARY CYCLE:
   - Semi-annual reviews
   - Manager assessment + peer feedback
   - Calibration across organization
   - Promotion decisions included

2. PSC STRUCTURE:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  WHAT YOU DID:                                             │
   │  - Key accomplishments                                     │
   │  - Impact and outcomes                                     │
   │                                                            │
   │  HOW YOU DID IT:                                           │
   │  - Collaboration                                           │
   │  - Meta values alignment                                   │
   │  - Leadership behaviors                                    │
   │                                                            │
   │  RATINGS:                                                  │
   │  - Exceeded Expectations                                   │
   │  - Met Expectations                                        │
   │  - Below Expectations                                      │
   │  (+ variations like "Greatly Exceeded")                    │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

3. CALIBRATION:
   - Managers meet to calibrate ratings
   - Cross-team comparison
   - Stack ranking within level
   - Promotion slots limited

4. META PROMOTION PATH:
   E3 → E4: ~1-2 years (almost automatic)
   E4 → E5: ~2-3 years (selective)
   E5 → E6: ~3-5 years (Staff, hard)
   E6 → E7: ~5+ years (Senior Staff, rare)

5. META PROMO TIPS:
   - Impact is king - ship things that matter
   - "Move Fast" and "Be Bold" valued
   - Get visibility with skip-level
   - Build sponsorship across teams
```

### Amazon's Promotion Process

Amazon's promotion process is document-driven and centered entirely on Leadership Principles. Your written promotion document is reviewed by a committee that evaluates each LP with specific examples from your work. Engineers who write strong, LP-aligned promotion documents with quantified outcomes advance significantly faster than those who rely on verbal advocacy alone.

```
AMAZON PROMOTION APPROACH:

1. DOCUMENT-DRIVEN:
   - Written promotion document
   - Reviewed by promotion committee
   - Leadership Principles heavily weighted
   - Bar Raiser concept applies

2. AMAZON PROMO DOC:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  AMAZON PROMOTION DOCUMENT                                 │
   │                                                            │
   │  EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:                                        │
   │  One paragraph: Who, what level, why ready                 │
   │                                                            │
   │  ROLE SCOPE:                                               │
   │  What you own, team size, complexity                       │
   │                                                            │
   │  LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES (2-3 examples per LP):              │
   │  - Specific situation                                      │
   │  - What you did                                            │
   │  - Quantified result                                       │
   │                                                            │
   │  SUPERPOWERS:                                              │
   │  What you're exceptionally good at                         │
   │                                                            │
   │  GROWTH AREAS:                                             │
   │  Self-awareness about development needs                    │
   │                                                            │
   │  PEER FEEDBACK:                                            │
   │  Quotes from peers and stakeholders                        │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

3. AMAZON LEVEL EXPECTATIONS:
   SDE I → SDE II: ~2 years
   SDE II → SDE III: ~2-4 years
   SDE III → Principal: ~4+ years (very selective)

4. AMAZON PROMO TIPS:
   - Every LP must have strong examples
   - "Ownership" and "Deliver Results" crucial
   - Get multiple Principal endorsements for senior roles
   - Write well - documents are your voice
```

### Stripe's Craft-Based Advancement

Stripe evaluates promotion readiness through the lens of engineering craft and scope of impact. Technical excellence matters more here than at many other companies, and promotion decisions weigh code quality, design rigor, and communication clarity alongside business outcomes.

```
STRIPE PROMOTION PHILOSOPHY:

1. CRAFT + SCOPE:
   - Technical excellence matters
   - Scope of impact matters
   - Not just activity, but quality

2. STRIPE LEVELS:
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  L1: Junior - Learning, contributing with guidance         │
   │  L2: Mid - Independent contributor                         │
   │  L3: Senior - Leads projects, mentors others               │
   │  L4: Staff - Multi-team impact, technical leadership       │
   │  L5+: Principal+ - Organization/company impact             │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

3. STRIPE'S EMPHASIS:
   - Code quality and craft
   - Clear thinking and communication
   - User and business impact
   - Technical rigor

4. STRIPE PROMO TIPS:
   - Excellence in your domain matters
   - Documentation of impact
   - Design docs as demonstration
   - Build reputation through quality
```

Despite differences in terminology and process, the same core patterns drive promotion decisions at every major technology company. The principles below apply whether you are at a FAANG company, a well-funded startup, or a mid-size enterprise. If you focus on these fundamentals, the company-specific details become easier to navigate.

### Junior → FAANG: Using Company-Specific Promotion Knowledge to Prepare Earlier

The promotion process differences between Google, Meta, Amazon, and Stripe are not cosmetic. They determine which activities produce evidence and which do not. At Amazon, where promotion decisions are heavily influenced by "impact on customers and business," framing your technical work in terms of customer outcomes rather than technical accomplishments is a preparation requirement, not a style choice.

At Google, where peer feedback carries significant weight in the promotion calibration process, investing in relationships with engineers who can write credible, specific peer feedback about your work is directly connected to your promotion probability. This means proactively helping other engineers with problems in your technical domain, participating actively in code reviews outside your immediate team, and building the reputation that motivates colleagues to write strong peer recommendations.

Research your company's specific promotion process before your first review cycle, ideally from engineers who have been through it recently. The official documentation is less useful than first-hand accounts of what the calibration committee actually weighted most heavily in recent promotion decisions.

### Senior @ FAANG: Staff Promotion Process Comparison and Positioning Strategy

The staff-level promotion processes at major FAANG companies differ in ways that significantly affect what evidence you should prioritize generating. At Google, the L7 promotion is decided by a promotion committee that includes directors from outside your org, which means your work needs to be legible to people who do not know your team's context. Written design documents and technical proposals that can stand alone as evidence of L7-level thinking are essential.

At Amazon, the L7 bar is particularly high on "scope and ownership of significant business outcomes." Technical excellence is necessary but not sufficient: you need evidence of decisions that affected the business at scale, not just the technical architecture. Engineers who are strong L6 technical contributors but have not been connected to outcomes that business leaders care about often stall at L6 at Amazon.

At Meta, the L7 promotion (E7) process includes a peer-led review that weights how other senior engineers evaluate your judgment and influence. External visibility within the engineering community (conference talks, open-source contributions, published technical writing) is weighted more explicitly at Meta than at most other FAANG companies, because it provides external calibration of your senior-level judgment.
