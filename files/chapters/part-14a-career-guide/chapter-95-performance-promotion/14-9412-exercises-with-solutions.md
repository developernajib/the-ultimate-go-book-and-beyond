## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Write Your Own Promotion Packet

**Task:** Create a draft promotion packet for yourself or a hypothetical engineer going from L5 (Senior) to L6 (Staff).

**Solution Framework:**

```markdown
# EXERCISE: Build Your L6 Promotion Packet

## Part 1: Impact Summary (30 minutes)

Write 3-5 bullet points answering:
- What was your biggest technical accomplishment this year?
- What business impact did it have? (quantify)
- How did you influence beyond your immediate team?
- Who did you help grow?

Example:
"Led the payments platform re-architecture that reduced
latency by 60% and saved $1.2M annually. Influenced
adoption by 3 teams through RFC process. Mentored 2
engineers who received promotions."

## Part 2: Detailed Project Writeup (45 minutes)

For your most significant project, write:

### THE SITUATION (2-3 sentences):
What was the problem? Why did it matter?

### YOUR CONTRIBUTION (1 paragraph):
What specifically did YOU do? (not the team, YOU)

### THE COMPLEXITY (1 paragraph):
What made this hard? Technical challenges? Coordination?

### THE OUTCOME (2-3 sentences with numbers):
What was the result? How did you measure success?

Example:
"The payment processing system was hitting latency SLOs at
peak traffic, risking customer satisfaction and revenue.
I designed and led implementation of an event-driven
architecture, coordinating across Payments, Infrastructure,
and Risk teams. The challenge was maintaining consistency
during migration while serving 50K transactions/hour.
Result: Latency improved from 450ms p99 to 180ms p99.
Zero-downtime migration. System now handles 3x capacity."

## Part 3: Peer Feedback Requests (20 minutes)

Identify 5 people who could provide strong feedback:
- 2 peers (collaborated closely)
- 1 senior engineer (saw your technical judgment)
- 1 cross-team partner (saw your influence)
- 1 mentee (speaks to your leadership)

Draft your request email for one of them.

## Part 4: Gap Analysis (15 minutes)

For L6 criteria, rate yourself:
- Technical leadership: Strong / Developing / Gap
- Cross-team influence: Strong / Developing / Gap
- Mentoring others: Strong / Developing / Gap
- Strategic thinking: Strong / Developing / Gap
- Communication: Strong / Developing / Gap

For each "Gap" or "Developing": What will you do in
the next 3 months to demonstrate strength?

## EVALUATION RUBRIC:

Strong Packet:
✓ Specific examples, not general claims
✓ Quantified impact (numbers, metrics)
✓ Clear YOUR contribution vs. team contribution
✓ Evidence of L6 scope (multi-team, strategic)
✓ Honest about growth areas

Needs Work:
✗ Vague claims without evidence
✗ No metrics or quantification
✗ Team accomplishments without individual clarity
✗ Only L5-level scope
✗ 100% positive (lacks self-awareness)
```

### Exercise 2: Role-Play the Promotion Conversation

**Task:** Practice the conversation with your manager about getting promoted.

**Solution:**

```markdown
# ROLE-PLAY SCENARIOS

## SCENARIO 1: Initial Promotion Discussion

### Setup:
You want to discuss your path to L6 with your manager.
You've been performing at Senior (L5) level for 18 months.

### YOUR SCRIPT:

Opening:
"Thanks for meeting. I wanted to discuss my career development.
I'm interested in understanding the path to Staff Engineer
and getting your perspective on where I am relative to that bar."

If manager is supportive:
"That's great to hear. What do you see as my strongest areas
for L6? And where do I have the most room to grow?"

"Based on your feedback, here's what I'm thinking for focus areas:
1. [Gap area 1] - I plan to address this by [action]
2. [Gap area 2] - I'd like to find an opportunity to [action]
Does this align with what you see?"

If manager is hesitant:
"I appreciate the honesty. Can you help me understand more
specifically what 'not ready yet' looks like? I want to make
sure I'm addressing the right things."

Closing:
"Thank you for the guidance. Could we check in on this monthly
so I can ensure I'm making progress? I'd also appreciate your
support in finding opportunities to demonstrate [gap areas]."

---

## SCENARIO 2: Following Up on Missed Promotion

### Setup:
You were passed over for promotion last cycle. You need to
understand why and create a path forward.

### YOUR SCRIPT:

Opening (calm, not defensive):
"Thanks for meeting. I wanted to discuss the promotion decision
and understand what I need to focus on for next cycle."

Getting specific feedback:
"You mentioned [vague feedback]. Can you give me a specific
example of where this was lacking? What would 'meeting the bar'
have looked like in that situation?"

Clarifying expectations:
"So if I understand correctly, the gaps are:
1. [Gap 1] - Would [specific action] address this?
2. [Gap 2] - What would demonstration of this look like?
Is there anything else that wasn't mentioned?"

Committing to improvement:
"I'm committed to addressing these areas. Here's my plan:
[Brief outline]. Can we check in monthly to ensure I'm on track?
I'd also appreciate your advocacy when I'm ready."

If feedback seems unfair:
"I want to understand this better. In [project X], I thought
I demonstrated [behavior]. Can you help me understand how
that was evaluated? Was there something I missed?"

Closing:
"I appreciate your candor. I'm going to reflect on this and
may have follow-up questions. I'm committed to getting to
Staff level and I value your support in that journey."

---

## SCENARIO 3: When You Have an External Offer

### Setup:
You have a competing offer and want to use it appropriately.

### YOUR SCRIPT:

Opening (honest, not threatening):
"I need to share something with you. I've received an offer
from [Company] at the Staff level. I wasn't actively looking,
but I want to be transparent with you."

If you want to stay:
"My preference is to stay here. I value our team and the work
we're doing. But I need to be honest that the compensation
and leveling gap is significant. Is there anything we can do?"

If manager asks what it would take to stay:
"To stay, I'd need [specific ask - promotion, compensation, etc].
I know that might require escalation. I'm happy to wait for a
definitive answer before making my decision."

If they can't match:
"I understand there are constraints. I need to do what's right
for my career at this point. I want to ensure a smooth transition
and will do everything to set the team up for success."

If they match/counter:
"Thank you for working on this. I appreciate the company's
investment. I'd like to review the full details and give you
my decision within [timeframe]."

### KEY PRINCIPLES:
- Never bluff (only do this with a real offer you'd accept)
- Be honest about your preference
- Give them time to respond
- Be graceful regardless of outcome
```

### Exercise 3: Create Your Career Development Plan

**Task:** Write a 12-month career development plan targeting your next level.

**Solution:**

```markdown
# 12-MONTH CAREER DEVELOPMENT PLAN

## CURRENT STATE
Name: [Your Name]
Current Level: L5 (Senior Software Engineer)
Target Level: L6 (Staff Software Engineer)
Timeline: 12 months (targeting Q4 2025 review cycle)

## L6 REQUIREMENTS & SELF-ASSESSMENT

| Dimension | L6 Expectation | Current State | Gap |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|-----|
| Technical Leadership | Lead multi-team technical initiatives | Strong on own team, limited cross-team | MEDIUM |
| Scope of Impact | Organization-wide influence | Team + some adjacent | MEDIUM |
| Mentoring | Grow engineers to senior level | Informal mentoring, no promotions yet | HIGH |
| Communication | Influence through docs/presentations | Good docs, limited presentations | MEDIUM |
| Strategic Thinking | Anticipate and plan for future needs | Reactive, tactical focus | HIGH |

## QUARTER-BY-QUARTER PLAN

### Q1: FOUNDATION (Jan-Mar)

#### Goals:
1. Identify and commit to cross-team initiative
2. Formalize mentoring relationship
3. Begin strategic planning practice

#### Actions:
- [ ] Meet with manager to align on L6 path (Week 1)
- [ ] Identify 2-3 potential cross-team projects (Week 2)
- [ ] Propose and get buy-in for one initiative (Week 4)
- [ ] Select mentee and establish regular 1:1s (Week 3)
- [ ] Write first 6-month technical roadmap for team (Week 8)

#### Success Metrics:
- Cross-team project kicked off
- Mentee relationship established with goals
- Roadmap document completed and reviewed

---

### Q2: EXECUTION & VISIBILITY (Apr-Jun)

#### Goals:
1. Make significant progress on cross-team initiative
2. Demonstrate mentoring impact
3. Increase visibility through presentations

#### Actions:
- [ ] Deliver first milestone of cross-team project
- [ ] Mentee completes significant project with my guidance
- [ ] Give tech talk at org all-hands (April)
- [ ] Publish internal blog post on technical learnings
- [ ] Attend skip-level to share project progress

#### Success Metrics:
- Project milestone delivered on time
- Mentee feedback: "significantly helped my growth"
- Tech talk delivered with positive feedback
- Skip-level aware of my L6 trajectory

---

### Q3: DEMONSTRATE IMPACT (Jul-Sep)

#### Goals:
1. Complete cross-team initiative with measurable impact
2. Expand influence to second initiative
3. Mentee promotion-ready

#### Actions:
- [ ] Cross-team project fully deployed
- [ ] Impact metrics documented
- [ ] Propose second cross-team initiative based on learnings
- [ ] Help mentee prepare promotion packet
- [ ] Request peer feedback mid-cycle

#### Success Metrics:
- Project delivered with quantified impact
- New initiative approved and started
- Mentee's manager confirms promotion candidacy
- Positive peer feedback received

---

### Q4: PROMOTION PREPARATION (Oct-Dec)

#### Goals:
1. Complete promotion packet
2. Secure sponsorship
3. Address any remaining gaps

#### Actions:
- [ ] Draft promotion packet (Week 1)
- [ ] Manager review and feedback (Week 2)
- [ ] Request 5 peer feedback writeups (Week 2)
- [ ] Revise packet based on feedback (Week 3)
- [ ] Confirm skip-level sponsorship (Week 4)
- [ ] Final packet submission (per cycle deadline)

#### Success Metrics:
- Packet rated "strong" by manager
- 5 positive peer feedback received
- Skip-level commits to advocacy
- Packet submitted on time

---

## ONGOING PRACTICES

### Weekly:
- Update brag document (Friday, 15 min)
- Mentee 1:1 (30 min)
- Cross-team project sync as needed

### Monthly:
- Manager 1:1 career check-in
- Review progress against quarterly goals
- Adjust plan as needed

### Quarterly:
- Self-assessment against L6 criteria
- Skip-level meeting for visibility
- Update career development plan

### If cross-team project is canceled:
- Identify alternative within 2 weeks
- Document learnings from partial work
- Propose new initiative based on experience

### If mentee isn't progressing:

- Consider second mentee
- Focus on other L6 dimensions

### If promotion is declined:

- Create 6-month plan addressing gaps

## ACCOUNTABILITY

Monthly review of this plan with manager.

### Self-Check:
Quarterly honest assessment.
Am I executing or just planning?

### External Validation:
Mid-year: Informal check with skip-level.
"What would make me a strong L6 candidate?"
```

### Junior → FAANG: Using Exercises to Build the Promotion Muscle

The exercises in this section work best when approached with the discipline of a professional deliverable rather than a casual study activity. Write your promotion packet draft as if you were actually submitting it to a calibration committee. Write your 12-month plan as if you were sharing it with your manager. The rigor of treating these as real documents rather than practice documents produces output that is actually useful, not just demonstrably complete.

One specific exercise extension: after writing your promotion packet draft, share it with one senior engineer who knows your work and ask them to tell you honestly which impact statements are specific enough to be credible and which are too vague to evaluate. The gap between what feels specific to you and what reads as specific to an evaluator is consistently the largest revision opportunity in promotion packet writing.

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Exercise Extensions and the Strategic Planning Practice

At senior level, extend each exercise with a cross-organizational constraint: write your promotion packet as if the evaluators do not know your team's context, because at senior levels the calibration committee often includes engineers from outside your organization. Every impact statement needs to be legible without domain-specific insider knowledge.

The 12-month plan exercise at senior level should include explicit milestones for evidence generation in each of the dimensions the promotion committee evaluates: technical decision-making impact, cross-team influence, organizational capability building, and external visibility. For each milestone, identify the specific artifact or outcome that will serve as evidence, the timeline for generating it, and the stakeholders who need to observe or validate it. This level of planning is unusual and it is exactly what the senior-level promotion process rewards.
