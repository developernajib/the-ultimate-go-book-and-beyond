## 95.11 Handling Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

### Understanding PIPs

A Performance Improvement Plan is a formal HR instrument with two very different purposes depending on the organization and situation. In its genuine form it creates a structured, documented feedback loop, specific measurable goals, a defined timeframe, and active support from the manager, which gives an engineer a concrete roadmap to address gaps in delivery, communication, or technical quality. In its exit form it serves primarily as legal documentation that the company followed due process before termination, with goals that are either vague, unrealistic, or both. Correctly diagnosing which type you are facing within the first few days determines whether your energy should go into intensive improvement or into an accelerated job search.

### If You're Put on a PIP

```markdown
# PIP SURVIVAL GUIDE

## IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (First 48 Hours)

### 1. Stay Calm and Professional
- Don't react emotionally in the meeting
- Ask for time to review the document
- Don't sign immediately (you can usually wait 24 hours)
- Don't badmouth manager or company

### 2. Understand the Terms
- What specific goals must you meet?
- What is the timeline?
- How will progress be measured?
- What happens if you succeed/fail?

### 3. Assess the Situation Honestly
Ask yourself:
- Are these goals achievable?
- Is my manager genuinely trying to help me succeed?
- Have I received this feedback before?
- Is this fair or politically motivated?

### 4. Consult Externally
- Employment lawyer (know your rights)
- Trusted mentor outside company
- Don't vent to current coworkers

## IF YOU DECIDE TO FIGHT IT

### Document Everything
- Keep copies of all PIP communications
- Save emails showing positive feedback/success
- Note dates and details of any meetings
- Screenshots of accomplishments (if appropriate)

### Seek Clarity
"I want to succeed. Can we discuss specifically:
- What does success look like for [goal]?
- How will we measure this objectively?
- What resources/support will I have?
- Can we check in weekly to ensure I'm on track?"

### Execute Relentlessly
- Overcommunicate progress
- Ask for feedback frequently
- Document your accomplishments
- Show visible improvement immediately

### Build Allies
- Let skip-level know you're committed to improving
- Ask for help from peers (carefully)
- Consider if HR can be an ally

## IF YOU DECIDE TO LEAVE

### Start Job Search Immediately
- Update resume
- Activate network quietly
- Apply broadly
- Prepare your interview narrative

### Your PIP Narrative for Interviews
Don't lie, but frame appropriately:

"The role wasn't the right fit for my skills and career
direction, and we mutually agreed to part ways. I'm now
looking for [type of role] where I can [your strengths]."

If asked directly about PIP:
"There were some performance concerns that I took seriously.
Through that experience I learned [lesson]. I've since
[demonstration of improvement]."

### Negotiate Your Exit
If leaving is inevitable, you may be able to negotiate:
- Severance payment
- Continuation of benefits
- Neutral reference
- Resignation instead of termination

## PREVENTING PIPs

### Warning Signs:
- Negative feedback increasing
- Exclusion from important meetings
- Reduced responsibilities
- Manager seems frustrated/distant
- Skip-level becomes distant

### Proactive Steps:
- Address feedback immediately
- Over-communicate progress
- Ask directly: "Am I meeting expectations?"
- Seek allies and mentors
- Document your accomplishments

### If You Suspect It's Coming:
- Request explicit feedback NOW
- Create improvement plan yourself
- Start job search as backup
- Document your accomplishments
```

### Junior → FAANG: PIPs as Information and What to Do When One Arrives

A PIP is not automatically the end of your tenure at a company. At FAANG companies, PIPs are formal processes with defined timelines and criteria, and engineers who engage with them seriously and meet the specified milestones do exit PIPs and continue their careers at the company. The key word is "seriously": treating a PIP as bureaucratic process rather than as specific feedback about a real performance gap is the most reliable way to fail it.

The first thing to do when placed on a PIP: ask your manager for a written clarification of the specific behaviors or outcomes that triggered it and the specific metrics that would constitute successful completion. If the PIP criteria are vague, you cannot meet them. Getting them in writing, before the clock starts, is essential.

Begin a job search in parallel. Not because you expect to fail the PIP, but because your employment security should not depend entirely on a single outcome. Actively searching while demonstrating genuine improvement effort is the appropriate risk management response to a PIP at any level.

### Senior @ FAANG: PIPs at Senior Level and the Organizational Context

At senior level, PIPs are less common but more consequential, and they typically signal not just individual performance gaps but organizational dynamics that need to be understood. A senior engineer placed on a PIP is often experiencing one of three situations: a genuine performance gap relative to the expectations at their level, a manager-engineer relationship breakdown where expectations were not clearly communicated, or an organizational change (new manager, new priorities, team restructuring) that created misalignment between past performance and current requirements.

Understanding which situation applies is the most important diagnostic step. The genuine performance gap situation requires the same response as at junior level: acknowledge the gap, develop a concrete improvement plan, and execute it. The manager-engineer breakdown situation may require escalation to skip-level management or HR to establish whether the PIP criteria are appropriate and whether the process was correctly followed. The organizational change situation is the most complex: a PIP that follows an org change is often a symptom of a structural problem that the PIP process cannot resolve.

Governance principle for senior engineers facing a PIP: engage with HR directly, not just through your manager. Understand the company's PIP policies, the timeline, the criteria for success, and the escalation options available to you. Senior engineers who navigate a PIP successfully tend to treat it as a managed process with defined parties, documented criteria, and clear accountability on both sides.
