## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Navigate performance review processes effectively
- Build a compelling case for promotion
- Develop skills that lead to career advancement
- Manage your relationship with leadership
- Know when to push for promotion vs when to wait

### Junior → FAANG: Making These Objectives Concrete in Year One

Each objective here maps to a specific action. Navigating performance reviews means understanding your company's review cycle, the rubric used to calibrate levels, and the artifacts reviewers use to make decisions. Building a promotion case means maintaining a brag document, collecting peer feedback strategically, and framing your work in terms of impact rather than effort. Managing your relationship with leadership means having regular one-on-ones, proactively asking for feedback, and making sure your manager knows about your contributions before the review period.

The objective most early-career engineers underinvest in: knowing when to wait. Engineers who push for promotion too early damage their credibility with the promotion committee. If you are promoted before you are genuinely operating at the next level, you will spend the following two years performing at the level you were promoted from, which is the most common cause of performance improvement plan situations at FAANG companies.

### Senior @ FAANG: Promotion Timing and the Strategic Calendar

At senior level, "know when to push for promotion vs when to wait" is a strategic calendar decision with financial and organizational consequences. The timing of a promotion cycle affects your equity refreshes, your compensation benchmarks, and your organizational standing. Waiting too long is also costly: every cycle you operate at L6 scope without being compensated at L6 level is a financial loss that is difficult to recover even after promotion.

The governance practice: have a written promotion timeline in your brag document. What is the target promotion cycle, what evidence is needed by that date, and what is the current gap between your evidence and the bar. Review this plan with your manager quarterly. If the plan shows that you are on track, continue. If the plan shows that you are behind on evidence in a specific dimension, you have enough time to address it before the cycle closes.
