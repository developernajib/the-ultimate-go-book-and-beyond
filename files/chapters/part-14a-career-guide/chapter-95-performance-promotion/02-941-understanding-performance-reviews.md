## 95.1 Understanding Performance Reviews

### How Performance Reviews Work

Performance review cycles are not a single event but a pipeline with distinct stages, each of which you can influence independently. The critical leverage point most engineers miss is the calibration meeting, a cross-team session where managers compare ratings and a "Meets Expectations" at your team may be downgraded if peers at other teams demonstrated more visible impact, making documentation of your work throughout the year essential rather than optional.

### The Self-Assessment

The self-assessment is not a formality, it is the primary input your manager uses when writing their assessment and defending your rating in calibration. Managers cannot remember everything you did across a year, so a well-structured self-assessment with quantified outcomes (latency reduced by 70%, $200K saved) directly translates into the concrete justifications your manager needs to argue for a higher rating against peers who may have done equivalent work but documented it better.

```
STRUCTURE:
1. Impact summary (biggest accomplishments)
2. Key projects and outcomes
3. Growth and development
4. Areas for improvement
5. Goals for next period

WRITING TIPS:
• Use metrics and specific examples
• Connect work to team/company goals
• Be honest about challenges
• Show self-awareness

EXAMPLE IMPACT STATEMENT:
"Led the redesign of the payment processing pipeline,
reducing latency from 500ms to 150ms (70% improvement)
and saving $200K annually in infrastructure costs.
Mentored two junior engineers who both received
promotions. Authored the team's first technical
vision document, now adopted as template org-wide."
```

### Junior → FAANG: Reading the Review System to Your Advantage

Performance review systems at FAANG companies are calibration systems, not just evaluation systems. Calibration means that your rating is not just a function of your work: it is a function of your work relative to a calibration group of peers at similar tenure and level. Understanding this changes the preparation strategy. You are not trying to appear as good as possible in an absolute sense: you are trying to provide your manager with specific, quantifiable evidence that your work was more impactful than that of your peer group.

The most common early-career mistake in review preparation is writing a self-review that lists projects and technologies. The most effective self-review lists outcomes: what got better, by how much, and what evidence exists that your contribution was causally responsible. If you improved service p99 latency by 35 percent, say so. If you completed a migration that had been in the backlog for two years, say so. The calibration committee is comparing evidence, not impressions.

Start collecting this evidence at the beginning of each review cycle, not in the week before the review deadline. The specific numbers you will need are much easier to find when you record them contemporaneously than when you try to reconstruct them from dashboards and commit histories three months later.

### Senior @ FAANG: Performance Review Governance and the Calibration Process at L6

At senior level, the performance review process includes a calibration meeting where a group of senior managers and directors compares candidates for promotion. Your manager is your advocate in this meeting, and their ability to advocate for you depends entirely on the quality of evidence in your promotion packet. The strongest manager advocacy cannot compensate for thin evidence. The most comprehensive evidence packet cannot compensate for a manager who does not advocate for you.

Both sides of this equation require explicit management. On the evidence side: work with your manager twelve months before your target promotion cycle to understand exactly what the calibration committee will be looking for. On the advocacy side: make sure your manager has the context they need to be a compelling advocate, including specific stories, numbers, and cross-team impact statements that they can use in the calibration meeting without needing to look anything up.

The ADR framework applies to promotion readiness: document the decision criteria for promotion to the next level, assess your current evidence against each criterion, identify the gaps, and build a twelve-month plan to address them. This level of preparation is unusual and it signals exactly the kind of systematic thinking that FAANG companies are trying to promote at senior levels.
