## Summary

### Key Takeaways

1. **Operate at next level first** - Prove it before asking
2. **Document continuously** - Don't rely on memory
3. **Build relationships** - Sponsors matter
4. **Make work visible** - Impact unseen is impact lost
5. **Have the conversation** - Don't wait to be noticed

### Performance Checklist

- [ ] Understanding of level expectations
- [ ] Running accomplishment document
- [ ] Regular manager check-ins
- [ ] Clear development goals
- [ ] Feedback incorporated

### Junior → FAANG: Summary as a Year-One Promotion Readiness Checklist

Use the summary checklist as a monthly review tool. On the first Friday of each month, spend 20 minutes checking each item: is your brag document current, have you had a substantive one-on-one with your manager in the last two weeks, do you know your development goal for this quarter, and have you incorporated the most recent feedback you received.

The single most important item on this list is the running accomplishment document. Every other item depends on your ability to describe your contributions specifically and with measurable outcomes. If that document is incomplete, everything else about the promotion process is harder.

### Senior @ FAANG: Summary as a Quarterly Strategic Review Framework

At senior level, the summary checklist becomes a quarterly strategic review document rather than a monthly operational check. Each quarter, assess: have you generated new evidence in the cross-team influence dimension, have you made the promotion timeline explicit with your manager, and have you identified and addressed any gaps relative to the promotion bar.

The governance principle that connects this chapter to long-term career strategy: promotion at FAANG is not the end state of a career but a calibration point within an ongoing development arc. Engineers who treat each promotion as a goal to achieve and then relax typically plateau. Engineers who treat each promotion as a higher operating level from which they immediately begin building toward the next level consistently advance faster and arrive at senior levels with more organizational capability and credibility than their peers.
