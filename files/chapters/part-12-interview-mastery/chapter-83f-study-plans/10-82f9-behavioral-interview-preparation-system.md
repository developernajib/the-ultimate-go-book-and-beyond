## 83F.9 Behavioral Interview Preparation System

Behavioral preparation is the most underestimated and most impactful area. A strong behavioral round can compensate for a mediocre coding round (especially at Amazon). A weak behavioral round can sink an otherwise strong candidacy. See Chapter 81 for the complete behavioral interview guide.

### Mining Your Experience for 12-15 Stories

**The Story Mining Process (90 minutes):**

1. Open your resume and a blank document.
2. For each role on your resume, write down:
 - The 2-3 biggest projects you worked on
 - The 1-2 hardest technical challenges you faced
 - The 1-2 most significant interpersonal challenges
 - Any failures or setbacks
 - Any recognition or measurable impact
3. For each item, write a 3-sentence summary: Situation, what you did, what happened.
4. Select the 12-15 strongest stories (the ones with the most conflict, the clearest actions, and the most measurable results).

**Story quality criteria (each story should have at least 4 of these 6):**

| Criterion | What It Means | Example |
|-----------|--------------|---------|
| **Conflict/tension** | Something was at stake | "The release was blocked and the team disagreed on the fix" |
| **Your specific action** | What YOU did (not the team) | "I proposed and implemented a caching layer" |
| **Technical depth** | Shows engineering skill | "I used consistent hashing to distribute load" |
| **Measurable result** | Quantified impact | "Reduced latency from 800ms to 120ms (85% improvement)" |
| **Learning** | What you took away | "I learned to validate assumptions with load testing before launch" |
| **Cross-functional** | Involved other teams/roles | "Worked with product, design, and the data team" |

### STAR Format Refinement

For each story, write it in STAR(L) format:

**S - Situation (2-3 sentences):** Set the context. What was happening? What was at stake?

**T - Task (1-2 sentences):** What was your specific responsibility?

**A - Action (3-5 sentences):** What did YOU specifically do? Use "I" not "we." Be specific about the technical and interpersonal actions you took.

**R - Result (2-3 sentences):** What happened? Quantify the impact. What changed?

**L - Learning (1-2 sentences):** What did you learn? How did this change your approach?

**Timing:** A well-told STAR story should take 90 seconds to 3 minutes. Under 90 seconds feels thin. Over 3 minutes loses the interviewer's attention. Practice with a timer.

### LP Mapping Matrix

Map each of your stories to the leadership principles or values it best demonstrates. A good story usually maps to 2-3 principles.

| Story | Primary LP | Secondary LP | Tertiary LP |
|-------|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| "Reduced API latency by 85%" | Dive Deep | Deliver Results | Customer Obsession |
| "Proposed new caching architecture" | Invent and Simplify | Bias for Action | Ownership |
| "Resolved team conflict on API design" | Have Backbone | Earn Trust | Are Right, A Lot |
| "Mentored junior engineer through promotion" | Develop the Best | Earn Trust | Ownership |
| "Launched feature despite changing requirements" | Deliver Results | Bias for Action | Customer Obsession |

### The "Pivot" Technique

The pivot technique lets you adapt a single story to answer different questions. Your "reduced API latency" story can answer:

- "Tell me about a hard technical problem" (focus on the debugging and solution)
- "Tell me about a time you dived deep" (focus on the investigation and root cause)
- "Tell me about delivering results under pressure" (focus on the deadline and impact)
- "Tell me about customer obsession" (focus on the user impact and metrics)

Practice pivoting each story to 3 different question types. This means 12 stories can effectively answer 36+ different questions.

### Red Flags Interviewers Look For

Avoid these behaviors - each one triggers a negative signal:

| Red Flag | What the Interviewer Thinks | How to Avoid |
|----------|----------------------------|-------------|
| "We did..." (always using "we") | Candidate cannot describe their individual contribution | Use "I" for your actions, "we" for team outcomes |
| No quantified results | Candidate does not measure impact | Always include numbers (%, $, time saved, users affected) |
| Blaming others | Candidate does not take ownership | Own your part. Even in failures, focus on what YOU could have done differently |
| Perfect stories only | Candidate is hiding failures or has no self-awareness | Include at least 2 failure stories. Show genuine learning |
| Vague actions | Candidate may be exaggerating their role | Be specific: "I wrote the migration script" not "I helped with the migration" |
| No learning | Candidate does not grow | End every story with what you learned and how you changed |
| Rehearsed/robotic | Candidate memorized a script | Practice enough to be fluent but not scripted. Vary wording each time |


### Junior → FAANG: Behavioral Interview Preparation System

The behavioral interview preparation system converts the abstract goal of 'prepare for behavioral interviews' into a concrete set of activities. The four-step system: (1) build the story bank (8-10 STAR stories covering all major behavioral themes), (2) map stories to the target company's leadership principles or values, (3) practice each story out loud until it is fluent (not scripted, but natural), (4) conduct mock behavioral interviews with a partner and incorporate feedback. This system takes 2-3 weeks of consistent effort and should not be rushed.

### Senior @ FAANG: Behavioral Preparation System at the L6 Bar: Scope Upgrade and Authentic Rehearsal

Senior candidates running the behavioral preparation system should apply a scope upgrade filter to every story: after drafting the STAR story, ask 'does this sound like an L6 story or an L4/L5 story?' The scope indicators are: team size affected, business metric impacted, organizational boundary crossed, level of ambiguity navigated, and whether you made the decision or implemented someone else's decision. Stories that fail the scope upgrade test need either replacement (find a better story) or reframing (tell the same story from the organizational perspective rather than the individual perspective). Authentic rehearsal at L6 also means your stories should not sound like STAR templates. They should sound like real experiences told with specificity and genuine reflection. The rehearsal goal is fluency, not memorization.
