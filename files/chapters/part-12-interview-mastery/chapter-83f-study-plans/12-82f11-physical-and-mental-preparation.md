## 83F.11 Physical and Mental Preparation

Interview preparation is a marathon, not a sprint. Your physical and mental state directly affect your cognitive performance - the ability to think clearly, solve problems creatively, and communicate effectively under pressure. Neglecting this dimension is as costly as neglecting system design preparation.

### Sleep Hygiene During Preparation

Sleep is when your brain consolidates the patterns you practiced during the day. Chronic sleep deprivation (below 7 hours) reduces problem-solving ability by 20-30% and increases anxiety.

**Sleep rules during preparation:**

| Rule | Implementation |
|------|---------------|
| 7-8 hours minimum every night | Set a non-negotiable bedtime alarm |
| No screens 30 min before bed | Read a physical book, stretch, or journal instead |
| Consistent wake time | Same time every day, even weekends (variance <1 hour) |
| No studying in bed | Your bed is for sleep. Study at a desk. |
| No caffeine after 2 PM | Caffeine's half-life is 5-6 hours, afternoon coffee disrupts sleep |
| Cool, dark room | 65-68 degrees Fahrenheit, blackout curtains |

**The night before an interview:**
- Do not cram. Your last problem should be solved by 6 PM.
- Light dinner, no alcohol.
- Prepare everything you need (clothes, tech setup, water) the evening before.
- In bed by 10 PM. If you cannot sleep, listen to a sleep meditation - do not open LeetCode.

### Exercise and Cognitive Performance

Regular exercise improves working memory, attention, and stress resilience. Studies show 30 minutes of moderate cardio increases problem-solving performance for 2-3 hours afterward.

**Exercise prescription during preparation:**

| Frequency | Type | Duration | Timing |
|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| Daily | Walk (brisk) | 30 min | Between study sessions (break time) |
| 3x/week | Cardio (run, bike, swim) | 30-45 min | Morning, before studying |
| 2x/week | Strength training | 30-45 min | Afternoon break |

**Exercise on interview day:** A 20-minute walk or light jog in the morning before your interview improves cognitive performance and reduces anxiety. Do not try a new intense workout - stick to your routine.

### Nutrition for Peak Mental Performance

Your brain consumes 20% of your daily calories. What you eat directly affects your ability to concentrate and solve problems.

**General guidelines:**

| Guideline | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| Regular meals (do not skip) | Blood sugar crashes destroy concentration |
| Protein with every meal | Sustained energy vs. carb-only sugar spike/crash |
| Hydration (8+ glasses/day) | Even mild dehydration impairs cognitive function |
| Limit sugar | Sugar causes energy spikes followed by crashes |
| Omega-3s (fish, walnuts, flaxseed) | Support brain function and reduce inflammation |

**Interview day nutrition:**
- Balanced breakfast (protein + complex carbs): eggs + toast, or oatmeal + nuts
- Water throughout the day
- Light snack between rounds if possible (banana, nuts, dark chocolate)
- Avoid heavy meals, excessive caffeine, or anything that might cause digestive discomfort

### Managing Anxiety and Imposter Syndrome

Interview anxiety is universal. Even experienced FAANG engineers report anxiety when interviewing at other companies. The goal is not to eliminate anxiety - it is to manage it so it does not impair performance.

**Anxiety management techniques:**

| Technique | How It Works | When to Use |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Box breathing** | Inhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec, exhale 4 sec, hold 4 sec. Repeat 4x. | Before every mock interview and real interview |
| **Reframing** | "I'm nervous" becomes "I'm excited." Same physiological response, different interpretation. | When you notice anxiety symptoms |
| **Preparation confidence** | Track your progress. When you feel "not ready," review your problem log and see how far you have come. | During the final week when imposter syndrome peaks |
| **Power posing** | Stand tall, hands on hips, for 2 minutes before the interview. | 5 minutes before interview starts |
| **Worst-case acceptance** | "If I fail this interview, I will learn from it, rest, and try again. My career is not over." | When catastrophic thinking takes over |

**Imposter syndrome specifically:**

Imposter syndrome tells you "I don't belong here" and "I'll be exposed as a fraud." Counter it with evidence:
- You solved X problems this month. That is real skill.
- You passed the resume screen and phone screen. The company believes you are qualified.
- Every person who works at a FAANG company was once sitting where you are, feeling what you feel.
- Being nervous means you care. That is a feature, not a bug.

### Dealing with Rejection

Rejection is part of the process. Even the strongest candidates get rejected sometimes - interview performance varies, interviewers have bad days, and the bar is genuinely high.

**Rejection statistics to normalize the experience:**
- Average FAANG acceptance rate after onsite: 20-40%
- Many successful FAANG engineers were rejected on their first attempt
- Most companies allow you to re-interview after 6-12 months
- A rejection does not mean you are not skilled - it means your performance on that specific day did not clear the bar

**Post-rejection protocol:**

1. **Day 1-2:** Feel your feelings. Disappointment is normal and valid. Do not pretend it does not hurt.
2. **Day 3:** Write down everything you remember from the interview - problems asked, where you struggled, what went well.
3. **Day 4-5:** Analyze your weaknesses. Was it coding speed? System design depth? Behavioral story quality? Anxiety?
4. **Day 6-7:** Adjust your study plan. Add targeted practice for your weakest areas.
5. **Week 2:** Resume studying. Apply to more companies. The pipeline must stay active.

**Crucial mindset:** Each interview, pass or fail, makes you stronger. The problems you struggled with become problems you know. The system design you fumbled becomes the design you practice until it is perfect. Rejection is feedback, and feedback accelerates learning.

### When to Take Breaks

**Planned breaks (build these into your schedule):**
- 1 full rest day per week (no studying, no LeetCode)
- 15-30 min breaks between study sessions
- 1 rest weekend per month (6-month plan only)

**Unplanned breaks (take these when needed):**
- Feeling unable to concentrate for >30 min: take a 2-hour break, go outside
- Solving problems slower than 2 weeks ago: take 2 full rest days
- Dreading studying every morning: take 3-5 full rest days
- Physical symptoms (headaches, insomnia, stomach issues): take 5-7 rest days and reassess pace

### Signs of Burnout and How to Recover

| Warning Sign | Severity | Action |
|--------------|----------|--------|
| Occasional lack of motivation | Normal | Push through with accountability partner |
| Dreading every study session | Moderate | Take 2-3 rest days, reduce daily hours by 30% |
| Solving problems slower than 3 weeks ago | Moderate | Rest for 3-5 days, then restart at reduced pace |
| Sleep disruption (insomnia, nightmares about coding) | Serious | Full week off. Exercise daily. No screens after 6 PM. |
| Physical symptoms (headaches, stomach issues, tension) | Serious | Full week off. See a doctor if symptoms persist. |
| Loss of interest in programming entirely | Critical | 2+ weeks off. Consider talking to a therapist. Reassess timeline. |

**Recovery protocol for moderate-serious burnout:**

1. **Days 1-3:** Complete rest. No studying. Exercise, cook meals, see friends.
2. **Days 4-5:** Light engagement only - read an engineering blog post, watch a system design video. No problem solving.
3. **Days 6-7:** Solve 2-3 easy problems only. Stop if it feels like a chore.
4. **Week 2:** Return to studying at 60% of your previous volume. Increase by 10% per week.
5. **Adjust timeline:** Push your interview dates back if needed. An extra 2-3 weeks of preparation at sustainable pace beats forcing through burnout.


### Junior → FAANG: Physical and Mental Preparation for Interviews

Cognitive performance on interview day is directly affected by sleep quality, nutrition, and anxiety management. The week before an interview: maintain your normal sleep schedule (do not stay up late studying), eat normally, and reduce alcohol. On interview day: eat a normal meal, avoid caffeine overload, and use a brief warm-up (review one familiar system design pattern or one coding problem you know well) to prime your working memory. High interview anxiety is primarily a signal of insufficient preparation combined with high stakes. The cure is more preparation, not relaxation techniques.

### Senior @ FAANG: Mental Performance Optimization for Senior Interview Loops

Senior candidates running a multi-week interview loop across multiple companies should treat cognitive performance as an athlete treats physical performance. The specific practices: sleep 7-8 hours every night without exception during the active interview period (sleep deprivation reduces working memory and verbal fluency, which are critical for interview performance). Schedule interview days with at least 2 days of recovery between loops at different companies. And develop a personal pre-interview ritual (a specific sequence of review, physical movement, and mental visualization) that primes your best performance. Interview anxiety at the senior level often has a specific trigger: the fear that you will be evaluated as less capable than you believe yourself to be. The most effective antidote is not positive self-talk but genuine competence achieved through deliberate preparation.
