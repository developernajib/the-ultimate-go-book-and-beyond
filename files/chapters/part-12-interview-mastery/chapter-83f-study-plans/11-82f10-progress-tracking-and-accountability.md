## 83F.10 Progress Tracking and Accountability

Without tracking, you cannot know if your preparation is working. Without accountability, you will skip sessions when motivation dips. This section provides concrete tracking tools and accountability structures.

### When to Accelerate or Slow Down

**Accelerate (skip ahead) when:**
- You are solving 80%+ of problems in a topic within time targets
- Your mock interview scores are consistently 4-5
- You have extra time and energy
- Action: Move to the next phase's material or add more mock interviews

**Slow down (extend phase) when:**
- You are solving <50% of problems within time targets for the current topic
- Mock interview scores are below 3
- You are feeling overwhelmed or burned out
- Action: Extend current phase by 3-5 days, reduce daily problem count, focus on understanding over volume

**Seek external help when:**
- You are stuck on the same pattern for >1 week despite review
- Mock interview scores are not improving over 3 weeks
- You are solving problems but cannot explain your approach
- Action: Find a study partner, hire a tutor, or use a structured course

### Finding Accountability Partners

**Where to find study partners:**
- LeetCode discussion forums (look for "study group" posts)
- Blind (anonymous professional network - search "interview prep buddy")
- Discord servers: NeetCode, LeetCode, FAANG Interview Prep
- Reddit: r/cscareerquestions, r/leetcode (weekly partner threads)
- Local meetups or university alumni networks

**Effective accountability structure:**
- Meet 2-3 times per week (30 min each, video call)
- Each meeting: share what you accomplished, what you struggled with, what you will do next
- Weekly mock interview exchange (alternate interviewer/candidate roles)
- Shared problem log (hold each other to daily targets)
- No judgment - accountability is about commitment, not competition

### Study Group Formation

**Ideal study group: 3-4 people**
- Larger groups lose accountability. pairs are fragile (one person drops out and the group dies)
- Similar target level (all targeting L4, or all targeting L5+)
- Compatible schedules (can meet consistently)
- Mix of strengths (one person strong on DP, another on system design)

**Weekly study group schedule:**

| Session | Duration | Activity |
|---------|----------|----------|
| Monday | 1 hour | Problem review: each person presents their hardest problem from last week |
| Wednesday | 1.5 hours | Mock interviews: rotate pairs (2 rounds of 45 min) |
| Saturday | 2 hours | System design: one person presents, others critique and ask follow-ups |


### Junior → FAANG: Tracking Preparation Progress

Progress tracking converts subjective feelings ('I think I am getting better') into objective measurement ('I solved 3 medium DP problems correctly in 35 minutes this week versus taking 55 minutes last week'). Track: problems solved per pattern area, mock interview scores per dimension, behavioral stories completed and practiced, and system design problems studied. Review your tracking log weekly and use it to adjust your study plan allocation.

### Senior @ FAANG: Accountability Systems for Senior Preparation Programs

Senior candidates benefit significantly from external accountability: a preparation partner who checks in weekly, a coach who reviews mock interview performance, or a study group of peers at the same preparation stage. External accountability reduces the risk of the most common senior preparation failure mode: drifting toward comfort (doing more coding problems because you enjoy coding) rather than investing in the hardest and most valuable preparation activities (developing L6-scope behavioral stories, doing deep system design with adversarial follow-up questions). Accountability partners should be briefed on your specific preparation objectives so they can ask targeted check-in questions rather than generic 'how is it going' questions.
