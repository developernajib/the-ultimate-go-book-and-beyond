## Summary

This chapter provided the complete strategic framework for FAANG interview preparation. Here are the key takeaways:

**Understanding the evaluation:** FAANG interviews test four pillars - coding, system design, behavioral, and domain knowledge - plus the hidden fifth pillar of communication. Each company weights these differently, and your preparation must account for these differences. Google emphasizes algorithmic optimality. Meta values speed. Amazon integrates Leadership Principles into every round. Your target company and level determine how you allocate your preparation time.

**Knowing your starting point:** The self-assessment in Section 83F.2 provides an honest diagnostic across coding, system design, behavioral, and Go proficiency. Your composite score determines which plan to follow and where within that plan to start. Skipping this assessment leads to wasted time on areas you already know or dangerous gaps in areas you have not yet identified.

**Following a structured plan:** The 12-week intensive plan (Section 83F.3) provides a day-by-day schedule for full-time preparation, progressing from foundation through core patterns, advanced DSA, system design, mock interviews, and company-specific preparation. The 6-month balanced plan (Section 83F.4) provides the same progression at a sustainable pace for working engineers. The 4-week emergency plan (Section 83F.5) prioritizes the 50 most important problems, 5 essential system designs, and 5 critical behavioral stories.

**Company-specific calibration:** Section 83F.6 and Chapter 83B provide targeted preparation tracks for Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, Netflix, Stripe, and other Tier-1 companies. Each track identifies unique aspects of the company's interview process, the 30 most-tested problems, system design favorites, and cultural values that matter.

**Systematic skill building:** The problem progression system (Section 83F.7) replaces random practice with pattern-based learning, spaced repetition, and speed tracking. The system design framework (Section 83F.8) provides a phased approach from learning components to under-pressure simulation. The behavioral preparation system (Section 83F.9) provides a story mining process, STAR format refinement, LP mapping, and the pivot technique.

**Tracking and accountability:** Section 83F.10 provides weekly scorecards, problem logs, mock interview tracking, and confidence indicators. Find an accountability partner. Join a study group. Track your progress weekly. Adjust your plan based on data, not feelings.

**Full preparation:** Section 83F.11 addresses the physical and mental dimensions that most preparation guides ignore. Sleep, exercise, nutrition, anxiety management, and burnout prevention are not optional - they directly affect your cognitive performance on interview day.

**The numbers that matter:**
- **150-300 hours** of structured preparation is the typical investment for a successful outcome
- **200-300 problems** solved (pattern-based, not random) provides sufficient coverage
- **10-15 system designs** practiced under timer covers the most common interview topics
- **12-15 behavioral stories** in STAR format handles virtually any behavioral question
- **10+ mock interviews** before the real thing builds performance confidence

**The final truth:** There is no shortcut. There is no trick. There is no secret question set that guarantees a pass. What guarantees the highest probability of success is structured preparation, consistent execution, honest self-assessment, and the resilience to keep going after setbacks. The plan is in place. Execute it.

### Junior → FAANG: Study Plans Chapter Summary

The key takeaways from the study plans chapter: choose a plan that fits your timeline and baseline, self-assess honestly using mock interviews rather than self-evaluation, track progress with concrete metrics, and allocate preparation time proportionally to your gaps rather than your preferences. The most common failure modes are insufficient self-assessment (not knowing your actual gaps), over-investment in comfortable dimensions (coding when system design is the gap), and treating the study plan as fixed rather than as a living document updated by weekly progress review.

### Senior @ FAANG: Study Plans Summary as a Transition to Execution

Senior candidates completing this chapter should have a concrete study plan ready to execute, not just read about. The transition from planning to execution is the highest-friction step in preparation: most candidates spend too long planning and not long enough doing. The plan does not need to be perfect. It needs to be good enough to start with and adaptive enough to improve as you learn more. Start executing tomorrow, not next Monday. The preparation value compounds with time, and every week of delay reduces the available preparation window.
