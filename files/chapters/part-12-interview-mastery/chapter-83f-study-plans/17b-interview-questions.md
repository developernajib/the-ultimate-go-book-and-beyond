## 83F.17 Interview Questions

Five FAANG-style questions about structuring preparation and study time.

### Q1 (Google): How do you allocate study time across DSA, system design, and behavioral for a 12-week plan?

**Answer:** For a senior IC role: 50% DSA (LeetCode patterns, mock coding), 30% system design (case studies, mock designs), 20% behavioral (story bank, mock STAR drills). Front-load DSA in weeks 1-6 because it has the steepest learning curve. Layer in system design starting week 3 once core patterns are familiar. Behavioral preparation runs throughout but intensifies in the final 3 weeks. Adjust ratios by level: L6+ shifts to 30/50/20.

### Q2 (Amazon): What is the right LeetCode volume and how do you avoid grinding without retention?

**Answer:** 200-300 problems is the working range, not 800. Quality beats quantity: solve by pattern (sliding window, two pointers, monotonic stack, BFS/DFS, DP, graph, heap, trie). For each pattern, do 10-15 problems and write a one-page summary of the template. Re-solve problems from memory after 3 days, 1 week, and 1 month using spaced repetition. Track problems where you needed a hint. These go on a daily review list until you can solve them cold.

### Q3 (Meta): How do you build a system design study plan when you have never designed a large system?

**Answer:** Start with the fundamentals layer: load balancers, caching, sharding, replication, consistency models, message queues, CDNs. Spend 2 weeks reading "Designing Data-Intensive Applications" chapters 1-9. Then study 15-20 canonical designs (URL shortener, Twitter feed, chat, ride-sharing, search, recommendation) and implement back-of-envelope math for each. Finally, do mock designs on whiteboard or virtual whiteboard, recording yourself and reviewing for clarity gaps.

### Q4 (Netflix): For a working engineer with limited evening hours, what is a sustainable 6-month plan?

**Answer:** 10-12 hours per week is sustainable: weekday evenings 1 hour each (5 hours of focused DSA or system design) and weekend 5-7 hours of mocks and deep work. Block the same hours daily to build habit. Take one full rest day per week to prevent burnout. Track weekly outcomes (problems solved, mocks done, gaps closed) rather than hours. At month 4, shift weight to mocks and weak-area drills.

### Q5 (Apple): How do you know when you are interview-ready and not just feeling productive?

**Answer:** Measurable signals: solving 80% of LeetCode mediums in 25 minutes without hints, completing a system design in 45 minutes with capacity math and three deep dives, delivering STAR stories under 4 minutes with concrete metrics. Run a graded mock with a senior engineer and require a "would hire" verdict on three consecutive mocks. If any signal is below threshold, extend prep by 2-4 weeks. Productivity feel is unreliable. Pass-rate on rubric is the truth.


### Junior → FAANG: Study Plan Practice Questions

The study plan practice questions in this section test your ability to apply the preparation strategies to your own situation. Practice questions include: 'design a 12-week preparation plan given a specific background and target level', 'identify the top three preparation gaps for a candidate who is strong in coding and weak in system design', and 'adapt the 6-month plan for a candidate who can only commit 5 hours per week'. Answering these questions concretely reveals whether you understand the preparation principles or are just reading them.

### Senior @ FAANG: Study Plan Questions as a Preparation Meta-Skill

Senior candidates who can fluently answer study plan questions are demonstrating a preparation meta-skill: the ability to design efficient learning programs for complex, multi-dimensional skill development. This meta-skill transfers directly to engineering leadership: designing onboarding plans for new team members, identifying skill gaps and growth paths for junior engineers, and designing ramp-up programs for engineers moving into new technical domains. The habit of thinking systematically about learning and preparation is a staff-level engineering capability that shows up in behavioral interviews as a signal of coaching and mentoring ability.
