## Exercises

### Exercise 1: Complete Self-Assessment

Complete the full self-assessment from Section 83F.2. This includes the 10-problem coding diagnostic (timed), the 2-problem system design assessment, the behavioral readiness check, and the Go proficiency self-rating. Record your scores in the composite readiness matrix. Based on your results, identify which study plan you will follow and which section of that plan you will start in.

**Deliverable:** Completed scoring matrix with plan selection and start date.

### Exercise 2: Create Your Personal Study Calendar

Using your selected plan from Exercise 1, create a detailed calendar for the first 4 weeks. For each day, specify:
- Exact problems you will solve (by name)
- System design topics you will study
- Behavioral stories you will write or practice
- Mock interviews you will schedule

Block the time in your actual calendar application. Set reminders. If you are doing the 6-month plan while working, identify which evening and weekend hours are dedicated to preparation.

**Deliverable:** 4-week calendar with daily activities blocked.

### Exercise 3: Build Your Go Snippet Library

Write the following data structures and algorithms from memory in Go, without any documentation or reference:

1. Min-heap implementing `heap.Interface`
2. Union-Find with path compression and union by rank
3. Trie with insert, search, and startsWith methods
4. BFS template for graph traversal with visited tracking
5. Binary search template (finding leftmost position where condition is true)

Time yourself. If any snippet takes more than 5 minutes, it needs more practice. Add these to your daily morning warm-up routine.

**Deliverable:** All 5 snippets written from memory, with timing recorded.

### Exercise 4: System Design Practice Session

Choose one of the 15 system designs from Section 83F.8. Set a 45-minute timer. Using only a whiteboard, paper, or Excalidraw (no references), work through the full ACED framework:
- Requirements gathering (5 min)
- Capacity estimation (5 min)
- High-level design (5 min)
- Detailed component design (15 min)
- Scalability and reliability (10 min)
- Wrap-up and trade-offs (5 min)

Record yourself (audio or video). Review the recording and score yourself using the system design scoring rubric from Section 83F.2.

**Deliverable:** Completed system design with self-assessment score and identified improvement areas.

### Exercise 5: Behavioral Story Portfolio

Complete the story mining exercise from Section 83F.9. Write 8 stories in full STAR(L) format (Situation, Task, Action, Result, Learning). For each story:
- Time yourself reading it aloud (target: 90-120 seconds)
- Map it to at least 2 Amazon Leadership Principles
- Ensure it contains at least 1 quantified result
- Identify 2 other question types this story could answer (the pivot technique)

Practice telling each story aloud at least twice. Record yourself once and review for filler words, vague language, and missing quantified results.

**Deliverable:** 8 written STAR(L) stories with LP mapping, pivot options, and at least 1 audio recording reviewed.


### Junior → FAANG: Study Plan Exercises

The exercises in this section require you to produce actual preparation artifacts: write your 12-week study plan given your specific timeline and baseline, complete a full self-assessment using the mock interview protocol, and write three behavioral stories at L6 scope. Completing these exercises is the difference between having read about interview preparation and actually being prepared.

### Senior @ FAANG: Exercises as Senior Preparation Infrastructure

Senior candidates completing these exercises should treat them as the foundation of their preparation infrastructure, not as homework to check off. The study plan exercise produces a living document that you update weekly as you learn more about your actual gaps. The self-assessment exercise produces a baseline that you remeasure after each major preparation milestone. The behavioral story exercises produce a story bank that you refine continuously as you practice and receive feedback. The value compounds over the preparation period rather than being a one-time activity.
