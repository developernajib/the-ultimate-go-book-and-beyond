### Junior → FAANG: Using Self-Assessment Scorecards

A self-assessment scorecard gives you a structured way to evaluate your mock interview performance against the actual criteria interviewers use. For coding interviews, the dimensions are: problem understanding (did you clarify requirements), approach (did you identify the right algorithm), implementation (clean, correct code), testing (did you trace through edge cases), and communication (did you narrate your thinking clearly). Rate yourself 1-5 on each dimension after each mock and track the trend over multiple sessions.

### Senior @ FAANG: Scorecard-Driven Preparation at the Senior Level

Senior candidates should use scorecards that include L6-specific dimensions not covered in standard coding scorecards: scope of impact (was the behavioral story at the right organizational level), trade-off articulation (did you explicitly discuss alternatives and rejection criteria), bar-raiser signal (did your answer demonstrate anything that would make a peer of yours likely to advocate strongly for a hire decision), and depth-breadth balance (did you cover all major system design areas while also going deep on the most important components). The scorecard is most valuable when reviewed with the mock interviewer, not just self-assessed: your perception of which dimensions you covered well often differs from the interviewer's perception.
