## Summary

Mock interviews are the bridge between knowledge and performance. This chapter provided complete practice sessions across all four interview types you'll face in a FAANG Go engineering interview loop:

**Coding interviews** test your ability to solve algorithmic problems in Go under time pressure. The four mock interviews covered arrays and strings (Google-style), trees and graphs (Meta-style), dynamic programming (Amazon-style), and concurrency (Go-specific). Each included realistic timing, complete solutions, follow-up questions, and scoring rubrics. The key insight is that coding interviews are as much about communication and process as they are about the algorithm - a perfect solution delivered in silence scores lower than a good solution delivered with clear narration.

**System design interviews** test your ability to architect distributed systems with principled trade-offs. The three mock interviews spanned L4 (URL shortener), L5/L6 (chat system), and L6/Staff (distributed task queue), with increasing emphasis on driving ambiguity, consistency trade-offs, and production concerns. The key insight is that interviewers evaluate your process more than your solution - requirements gathering, estimation, and trade-off discussion matter more than the final architecture diagram.

**Behavioral interviews** test your self-awareness, leadership, and ability to learn from experience. The 20 model answers demonstrated the difference between "good" and "great" responses: specificity, quantified impact, ownership of failures, and growth from negative experiences. The key insight is that behavioral interviews are not storytelling contests - they are evaluations of your judgment, values, and potential for growth at the next level.

**Go-specific deep dives** test whether your Go expertise is production-depth or textbook-depth. The mock covered GC internals, goroutine leak debugging, hot path optimization, and interface-based design. The key insight is that interviewers care about your debugging process and design reasoning, not just your knowledge of facts.

Use the self-assessment scorecards after every practice session. Track your scores over time. Focus your practice on your weakest dimension - improving from 2/5 to 4/5 in one area is more valuable than improving from 4/5 to 5/5 in another. Set a target interview date and work backward to create your practice schedule.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is consistent, reliable performance under pressure. You achieve that through repetition, honest self-evaluation, and deliberate focus on your weaknesses. Start with Section 83C.2 tomorrow. Set a timer. Do the work.

### Junior → FAANG: Mock Interview Chapter Summary

The key takeaways from mock interview practice: high-fidelity simulations produce significantly better results than low-fidelity study. Specific feedback on specific dimensions is required to improve. And the improvement model is iterative (practice, receive feedback, improve one dimension, repeat). The most actionable next step is to schedule a mock interview with a real person within the next week, not to do more solo practice.

### Senior @ FAANG: Mock Interview Chapter Summary at the Senior Level

Senior candidates completing this chapter should have a clear picture of their readiness: which dimensions are at L6 level, which are at L5 level, and which need more work.
