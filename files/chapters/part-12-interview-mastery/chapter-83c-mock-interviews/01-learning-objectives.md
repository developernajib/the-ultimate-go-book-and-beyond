## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Conduct realistic self-administered mock interviews with proper timing and evaluation
- Solve FAANG-caliber coding problems in Go within 45-minute time constraints
- Navigate system design interviews at L4 through L6/Staff levels with structured approaches
- Deliver compelling behavioral interview answers using the STAR format
- Evaluate your own performance using the same rubrics hiring committees use
- Identify specific weaknesses and build targeted practice plans
- Handle common follow-up questions and interviewer pressure with composure
- Demonstrate Go-specific expertise in concurrency, interfaces, and runtime internals


### Junior → FAANG: Mock Interview Learning Goals

Set specific learning goals for each mock interview rather than generic goals like 'practice coding'. Before each mock, identify the specific area you want to improve: for example, 'practice the first 10 minutes of a system design: framing requirements and making estimation decisions without being prompted'. Specific goals make the mock more deliberate and make the feedback more actionable.

### Senior @ FAANG: Objective Setting for Senior Mock Interview Programs

Senior candidates should set objectives at the competency level rather than the topic level. Instead of 'practice system design', set 'demonstrate that my system design answers include explicit trade-off analysis and conditions for revisiting decisions'. This level of specificity makes feedback from the mock interviewer much more useful: they are looking for a specific signal and can tell you whether you produced it. At the L6 level, the competency objectives are: scope appropriate to staff/senior (organizational impact, not individual), fluency in trade-off articulation, and the ability to drive deep on the highest-risk component without being prompted.
