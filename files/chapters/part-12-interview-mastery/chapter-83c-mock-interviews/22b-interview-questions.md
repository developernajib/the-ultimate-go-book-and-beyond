## 83C.21 Interview Questions

Five FAANG-style questions that test how to extract maximum value from mock interviews.

### Q1 (Google): How do you structure a 45-minute mock coding interview to maximize realism?

**Answer:** Mirror the real loop: 3 minutes for problem statement and clarifying questions, 5 minutes for examples and approach discussion, 25 minutes for coding, 7 minutes for testing and complexity analysis, 5 minutes for follow-ups. Use a shared doc or CoderPad without an IDE. Have the mock partner refuse to confirm correctness until you walk through test cases yourself. Record the session and review the transcript for filler words, silent stretches over 30 seconds, and missed clarifications.

### Q2 (Meta): What feedback loop should a mock interviewer give to actually improve the candidate?

**Answer:** Score on Meta's four signals: problem solving, coding, verification, communication. For each, give one concrete strength and one concrete gap with a timestamp. Avoid vague praise like "good job." Instead say "at minute 12 you jumped to coding before stating the brute force, which cost you a chance to discuss trade-offs." Suggest a specific drill (e.g., "always state brute force in one sentence before optimizing") rather than abstract advice.

### Q3 (Amazon): How do mock behavioral interviews prepare you for the bar raiser round?

**Answer:** The bar raiser drills into one story for 15-20 minutes with follow-ups like "what would you do differently," "what was the alternative," "who disagreed and why." Mock partners must commit to deep follow-ups instead of moving on after the initial STAR. Practice answering "tell me more about the conflict" three times in a row without repeating yourself. This builds the muscle to surface specifics under pressure.

### Q4 (Netflix): When mock interviewing for senior roles, how do you simulate the architecture deep-dive format?

**Answer:** Pick a system you built and have the mock partner act as a skeptical staff engineer. They should challenge every choice (why Postgres not DynamoDB, why this consistency model, why this team boundary). Set a 60-minute timer and forbid hand-waving. If you cannot defend a decision with data or trade-off reasoning, flag it as a study item. Senior loops fail more often on shallow ownership of past work than on missing knowledge.

### Q5 (Apple): How many mock interviews are enough before the real loop, and how do you avoid burnout?

**Answer:** For a typical FAANG loop, 8-12 coding mocks, 4-6 system design mocks, and 3-4 behavioral mocks is the working range. Beyond that, returns diminish and fatigue degrades performance. Stop mocks 48 hours before the real loop and switch to light review and rest. Track a rubric across mocks (time to first viable solution, communication clarity, bug rate). When the trend plateaus for three sessions, move to the real loop.


### Junior → FAANG: Interview Questions on Mock Interview Methodology

Questions about mock interview methodology test your understanding of deliberate practice principles and your self-awareness as a candidate. Know the difference between low-fidelity practice (reading solutions) and high-fidelity practice (timed mocks with real feedback), and be able to describe your own preparation system. Interviewers sometimes ask 'how did you prepare for this interview?' as a behavioral screen for growth mindset and systematic thinking.

### Senior @ FAANG: Interview Questions on Preparation Quality at the Senior Level

Senior candidates should be prepared to discuss their preparation approach at a meta level: what they identified as their specific gaps, how they designed a preparation program to close those gaps, and how they measured improvement. This kind of systematic self-improvement narrative is a positive signal in senior interviews because it demonstrates the same analytical rigor applied to problem-solving. It also demonstrates intellectual honesty: a candidate who knows their weaknesses and has a plan for them is more credible than a candidate who claims uniform strength across all dimensions.
