## 83G.11 Virtual Interview Mastery

Virtual interviews are now the norm for initial rounds and are common even for final rounds. They have unique challenges and opportunities.

### Screen Sharing: What to Show, What to Hide

**Show:**
- A clean editor window with readable font size (14pt+)
- The specific file or tab you are working on
- A drawing tool or whiteboard app for system design

**Hide:**
- Other browser tabs (especially anything embarrassing or distracting)
- Desktop notifications (Slack, email, social media)
- Your file system (close the sidebar in VS Code if not needed)
- Terminal history (clear it before the interview: `clear`)

**Tip:** Share a specific window, not your entire screen. This prevents accidentally showing something you did not intend.

### Using an IDE vs. Online Editor

Some companies give you a choice. Here is the trade-off:

| IDE (VS Code, GoLand) | Online Editor (CoderPad, CodeSignal) |
|---|---|
| Familiar environment | What the interviewer expects |
| Autocomplete and linting | Proves you don't rely on autocomplete |
| Can run code locally | Shared environment (interviewer sees your work) |
| More professional-looking | Less setup required |

**Recommendation:** Use whatever the company provides by default. If they offer a choice, use CoderPad or similar --- it is designed for interviews and the interviewer can see your code in real-time. If they specifically say "use your own IDE," use VS Code with a clean profile.

### Handling Technical Difficulties

Internet drops, audio fails, screen share crashes. Technical problems happen. Have a plan.

```
TECHNICAL DIFFICULTY RECOVERY PLAN

Problem: Internet drops during the interview
Solution:
  1. Reconnect immediately
  2. Text the interviewer or recruiter from your phone:
     "My internet dropped. Reconnecting now."
  3. Have a phone hotspot ready as backup
  4. If reconnection fails, call the recruiter to reschedule

Problem: Audio stops working
Solution:
  1. Try switching to phone audio (most platforms support this)
  2. Type in the chat: "I'm having audio issues. Switching to phone."
  3. Have your phone number shared with the interviewer in advance

Problem: Screen share doesn't work
Solution:
  1. Try sharing a different window
  2. Use the platform's built-in editor instead of screen share
  3. In the worst case, describe your code verbally while debugging

Problem: Computer crashes
Solution:
  1. Restart and rejoin (aim for under 2 minutes)
  2. The recruiter's phone number should be saved in your phone
  3. Most companies will give you extra time or reschedule gracefully

CRITICAL: Test everything the day before. This is preventable pain.
```

### Maintaining Eye Contact Through a Camera

The natural instinct is to look at the interviewer's face on your screen. But to the interviewer, this looks like you are looking down or away. True "eye contact" in a virtual setting means looking at the camera lens.

**Practical tips:**
- Position the video call window as close to the camera as possible
- Look at the camera when speaking, at the screen when listening
- If screen sharing, you have a natural excuse to look at the screen
- Practice this before the interview --- it feels unnatural at first

### Body Language in a Virtual Setting

Your body language is visible from the chest up. Make it count.

**Do:**
- Sit up straight (conveys confidence)
- Nod when the interviewer speaks (shows engagement)
- Use hand gestures when explaining (visible if camera is positioned correctly)
- Smile when appropriate (builds rapport)
- Lean slightly forward when speaking (conveys enthusiasm)

**Do not:**
- Slouch (conveys boredom or fatigue)
- Touch your face repeatedly (distracting)
- Look at your phone (obvious on camera)
- Rock in your chair (distracting)
- Stare at something off-camera (looks disengaged)

### Background and Lighting Setup

Your visual presentation in a virtual interview creates a subconscious impression that affects how the interviewer perceives your answers. This is not about vanity --- it is about removing distractions so the interviewer focuses on your words, not your environment.

**The three-point lighting setup (simplified for interviews):**

1. **Key light (most important):** A bright, diffused light source in front of you, slightly above eye level. A window works perfectly during the day. At night, place a desk lamp behind your monitor pointed at your face. Avoid harsh direct lighting --- bounce it off a wall or use a diffuser.

2. **Avoid backlighting:** Never sit with a window behind you. The camera will expose for the bright window, turning you into a silhouette. If you must sit in front of a window, close the blinds.

3. **Even illumination:** Both sides of your face should be roughly equally lit. Strong light from one side creates dramatic shadows that look fine in a movie but distracting in an interview.

**Background options (ranked):**

| Background | Impression | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Clean bookshelf | Professional, intellectual | Best option if available |
| Blank wall | Neutral, clean | Perfectly fine, slightly bland |
| Virtual blur | Professional if done well | Can glitch with fast movements |
| Virtual background (image) | Risky | Avoid unless your hardware handles it well |
| Messy room visible | Unprofessional | Always use blur if your space is messy |

**Quick pre-interview camera check:**
1. Open your camera app or the video call preview
2. Check: Is your face well-lit and centered?
3. Check: Is the background clean or blurred?
4. Check: Can you see your shoulders and full head with some headroom?
5. Check: Are there any reflections or glare on your glasses (if applicable)?

### Common Virtual Interview Pitfalls to Avoid

**1. The "looking down" problem.** If your laptop is on your desk, the camera looks up at you, creating an unflattering angle and making it look like you are looking down at the interviewer. Stack books under your laptop until the camera is at eye level.

**2. The typing volume problem.** If you are coding in a shared editor while not muted, your typing can be distractingly loud. Use a quiet keyboard or type more gently during the interview. If your keyboard is mechanical and loud, warn the interviewer: "My keyboard is a bit loud --- please let me know if it's distracting."

**3. The notification popup problem.** A Slack message pops up on your shared screen: "Hey, how's the interview going?" Embarrassing. Close all non-essential apps. Turn on Do Not Disturb mode at the OS level, not just in individual apps.

**4. The "you're on mute" problem.** Start every round by confirming audio: "Can you hear me OK?" It takes 3 seconds and prevents 3 minutes of frustrated troubleshooting.

**5. The dead-air-on-disconnect problem.** If you lose connection, the interviewer does not know if you disconnected or if your audio broke or if you are thinking silently. Always have a backup communication channel ready (phone number, chat in the platform).


### Junior → FAANG: Virtual Interview Technical Mastery

Virtual interview mastery begins with setup: test your audio, video, and shared coding environment the day before. Use a wired ethernet connection if possible for stability. Know the specific tools the interview will use (CoderPad, CodeSignal, Google Docs) and practice using them before the interview. Have a backup plan for technical failures: a phone number to call if the video fails, a secondary device if your primary fails, and the interviewer's email address to communicate if you get disconnected.

### Senior @ FAANG: Virtual Interview as a Signal of Distributed Collaboration Skill

At the senior level, virtual interview performance is a proxy for distributed collaboration effectiveness. Most FAANG teams operate with engineers across multiple time zones, and communication quality over video is a genuine work skill, not just an interview skill. Strong virtual interview performers maintain eye contact (look at the camera), speak clearly and at appropriate pace, manage shared digital workspaces efficiently, and check for alignment actively ('does this diagram make sense on your end?'). The meta-signal to senior bar-raisers is: would this person be an effective collaborator in our distributed team? Demonstrating virtual collaboration excellence during the interview is itself a senior-level signal.
