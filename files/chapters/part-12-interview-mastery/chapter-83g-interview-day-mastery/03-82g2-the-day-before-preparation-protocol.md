## 83G.2 The Day Before: Preparation Protocol

The day before your interview is not the time to learn new things. If you do not know it by now, one more day of frantic studying will not help --- and it may actively hurt by increasing your anxiety and depleting your mental energy. The day before is for consolidation, logistics, and rest.

### Physical Preparation Checklist

Your body is the hardware your brain runs on. Treat it accordingly.

```
THE DAY BEFORE: PHYSICAL CHECKLIST

[ ] Exercise in the morning (30-60 minutes, moderate intensity)
    - Reduces cortisol, improves sleep quality
    - DO: walk, jog, swim, yoga
    - DON'T: intense training that leaves you sore or exhausted

[ ] Hydrate consistently throughout the day
    - At least 8 glasses of water
    - Reduce caffeine after 2 PM
    - Avoid alcohol entirely

[ ] Eat clean, familiar foods
    - No new restaurants, no heavy meals
    - Complex carbs + protein for dinner
    - Avoid excess sugar (crash the next morning)

[ ] Plan tomorrow's meals NOW
    - Breakfast ready (or ingredients purchased)
    - Lunch planned if it's a full-day onsite
    - Snacks packed (nuts, fruit, energy bars)

[ ] Prepare your clothes
    - Lay out what you'll wear (see clothing section)
    - Iron if needed. No morning decisions.

[ ] Set TWO alarms
    - Primary alarm + backup alarm on a different device
    - Allow at least 2 hours before the interview
```

### Mental Preparation Exercises

**1. The Confidence List.** Write down 10 things you are genuinely good at. Not things you hope you are good at --- things you know. These can be technical ("I can implement BFS/DFS in my sleep"), professional ("I designed a system that handles 50K RPS"), or personal ("I explain complex ideas clearly"). Put this list on your nightstand. Read it before bed and again in the morning.

**2. Light Problem Review (not solving).** Spend 30--45 minutes reviewing problems you have already solved. Do not attempt new problems. Leaf through your notes. Glance at patterns you have identified. Remind yourself that you know this material. This primes your brain without exhausting it.

**3. The "Worst Case" Exercise.** Imagine the absolute worst interview scenario: you blank on every problem, you cannot write a single line of code, the interviewer is hostile. Now ask yourself: "What happens then?" You do not get the job. You try again in 6--12 months. Your life continues. Your career continues. Nothing catastrophic happens. Defining the worst case and accepting it removes the paralyzing fear of the unknown.

### Technical Warm-Up Routine (Light Problems Only)

```
DAY-BEFORE WARM-UP (30-45 minutes max)

DO:
  ✓ Review your "pattern cheat sheet" (the one you built in Chapter 78B)
  ✓ Re-read 2-3 problems you solved well (remind yourself you can do this)
  ✓ Skim your Go syntax reference (error handling, goroutines, slices)
  ✓ Review your STAR stories (one read-through)
  ✓ Glance at your system design notes (high-level only)

DON'T:
  ✗ Attempt new problems
  ✗ Grind LeetCode
  ✗ Read about unfamiliar topics
  ✗ Practice for more than 1 hour
  ✗ Stay up late studying
  ✗ Watch YouTube tutorials on topics you've never covered
```

### Equipment and Logistics Check

**For Virtual Interviews:**

```
VIRTUAL INTERVIEW TECH CHECK (do this the night before)

[ ] Test video call software (Zoom, Google Meet, CoderPad, etc.)
    - Install updates NOW, not 5 minutes before the interview
    - Test with a friend if possible
[ ] Test microphone and camera
    - Check audio quality in the app's settings
    - Verify camera angle and lighting
[ ] Test internet connection
    - Run a speed test (aim for 10+ Mbps upload)
    - Identify backup: phone hotspot, nearby cafe, library
[ ] Test screen sharing
    - Know how to share specific window vs full screen
    - Close all non-interview tabs and applications
[ ] Prepare your coding environment
    - Open the editor you'll use (VS Code, plain text, CoderPad)
    - Set font size to be readable when screen-shared
    - Dark mode or light mode (whichever you'll use)
[ ] Charge laptop and have charger accessible
[ ] Silence phone, disable notifications on computer
[ ] Tell housemates/family about the interview time (no interruptions)
[ ] Put a "Do Not Disturb" sign on your door
[ ] Have a glass of water at your desk
[ ] Have a pen and paper for notes/diagrams
```

**For In-Person Interviews:**

```
IN-PERSON INTERVIEW LOGISTICS CHECK

[ ] Confirm the address and floor/room number
[ ] Plan your route (drive it if possible, or check transit)
[ ] Add 30 minutes of buffer for unexpected delays
[ ] Know where to park (or which transit stop)
[ ] Have the recruiter's phone number saved (in case you're late)
[ ] Print your resume (3 copies --- you may have multiple interviewers)
[ ] Pack a bag:
    - Water bottle
    - Snacks (energy bars, nuts)
    - Phone charger
    - Pen and notepad
    - Deodorant (for long onsites)
    - Breath mints
    - Any medication you take
[ ] Know the company's visitor check-in process
    - Government ID required?
    - Sign an NDA at the desk?
    - Will someone meet you in the lobby?
```

### Sleep Optimization

Sleep is the single most impactful thing you can do the night before. Research from the University of Pennsylvania shows that getting 6 hours of sleep instead of 8 reduces cognitive performance by approximately 25% --- equivalent to being legally impaired. One night of poor sleep affects working memory, reaction time, and creative problem-solving, which are exactly the skills you need in a technical interview.

```
SLEEP PROTOCOL

- Set a bedtime that gives you 7-8 hours before your wake-up alarm
- Stop screen time 30 minutes before bed (or use blue light filters)
- No caffeine after 2 PM
- No alcohol (it disrupts sleep quality even if you fall asleep faster)
- Room temperature: 65-68°F (18-20°C) is optimal
- If your mind races, write down your worries on paper ("worry dump")
- If you can't sleep: don't panic. One night of 6 hours won't ruin you.
  Research shows worrying about not sleeping is worse than the sleep loss itself.
- DO NOT take sleeping pills you haven't used before.
  An unfamiliar medication could leave you groggy.
```

### What to Eat and Avoid

| Category | Good Choices | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Dinner (night before) | Grilled chicken + rice + vegetables, pasta with protein, salmon + quinoa | Spicy food, heavy cream sauces, large portions, foods you haven't tried before |
| Breakfast (day of) | Oatmeal with berries, eggs + whole wheat toast, Greek yogurt + granola | Sugary cereal, pastries (sugar crash), heavy breakfast (food coma), nothing (low blood sugar) |
| Snacks | Nuts, fruit, dark chocolate, cheese | Candy, chips, energy drinks |
| Beverages | Water, moderate coffee/tea, green juice | Alcohol, excessive caffeine, sugary drinks |

### Setting Up Your Environment (Virtual Interviews)

A virtual interview is a production deployment of your home setup. Test everything the day before, not the morning of:

```
VIRTUAL INTERVIEW ENVIRONMENT SETUP

Background:
  - Clean, uncluttered wall or bookshelf
  - Blur background if your room is messy
  - No distracting posters or personal items visible
  - Professional but not sterile

Lighting:
  - Face a window (natural light on your face)
  - If no window, place a lamp behind your monitor
  - Avoid overhead-only lighting (creates shadows under eyes)
  - Test your lighting on camera the night before

Camera:
  - Eye level (stack books under laptop if needed)
  - Arms-length distance from your face
  - Centered in frame with head room above

Audio:
  - Use headphones with a microphone (reduces echo)
  - Wired headphones are more reliable than Bluetooth
  - Test that your mic picks up your voice clearly
  - Ensure no background noise (close windows, silence pets)

Desk:
  - Water within reach
  - Pen and paper for notes/diagrams
  - Phone silenced and face-down (unless it's your backup)
  - No second monitor showing distracting content
```

### What to Wear

**In-Person:** Business casual unless told otherwise. Clean shirt/blouse, decent pants/skirt, clean shoes. Overdressing is better than underdressing. When in doubt, one level above what you'd wear daily at that company. Do not wear a suit to a startup that posts photos of hoodie-wearing engineers.

**Virtual:** Professional from the waist up (you will stand at some point --- make sure the bottom half is presentable too). Solid colors work best on camera. Avoid busy patterns, bright white (causes glare), or colors that match your background.

**Both:** Wear something you feel confident in. This is not vanity --- clothing affects your psychological state. Studies show that wearing clothes you associate with competence ("enclothed cognition") measurably improves cognitive performance.


### Junior → FAANG: Day-Before Interview Preparation

The day before an interview should be a consolidation day, not a study day. Light review only: scan your key behavioral stories, review 2-3 system design patterns you feel strongest in, and solve one easy coding problem to maintain fluency. The goal is to prime your memory without exhausting your cognitive resources. Get 8 hours of sleep. It has a larger impact on interview performance than any last-minute study.

### Senior @ FAANG: Day-Before Protocol for Senior Multi-Round Loops

Senior candidates in a multi-round interview loop (where the same company's loop spans two consecutive days) should treat day one as both an interview day and a preparation update day. After completing day one, take 20 minutes to write down topics that came up, questions that surprised you, and adjustments you want to make for day two. This rapid iteration approach lets you improve your performance mid-loop in a way that single-day loops do not allow. The adjustment should be targeted: do not try to deeply learn a new topic overnight. Instead, clarify and rehearse your answer to a question you answered weakly in day one.
