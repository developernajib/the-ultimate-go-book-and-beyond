## 83G.13 Post-Interview Protocol

What you do after the interview matters more than most candidates realize. It affects your negotiating position, your ability to learn from the experience, and your long-term professional network.

### Immediately After: Write Down Everything

Within 30 minutes of the interview ending, document the following while it is fresh:

```
POST-INTERVIEW DEBRIEF TEMPLATE

Date: _______________
Company: _______________
Position: _______________
Round: _____ of _____

For each interviewer:
─────────────────────
Name: _______________
Round type: [Coding / System Design / Behavioral / Other]

Problem/Question:
(Write the exact problem statement or question as close to verbatim as you can)

My approach:
(How did you tackle it?)

Where I struggled:
(What parts were difficult? Where did you get stuck?)

Where I did well:
(What parts went smoothly?)

Things I said that I'm proud of:
(Specific moments of good communication)

Things I wish I'd said differently:
(Missed opportunities)

Interviewer's demeanor:
(Encouraging? Neutral? Challenging?)

My confidence level: [1-5]
(1 = bombed it, 5 = nailed it)
```

This debrief serves three purposes:
1. **Negotiation preparation:** If you receive an offer, knowing which rounds went well helps you negotiate confidently.
2. **Learning:** If you don't get the offer, this is your study guide for next time.
3. **Pattern recognition:** After several interviews, you will see patterns in where you struggle.

### Thank-You Emails

**When to send:** Within 24 hours of the interview.

**Who to send it to:** Your recruiter. You typically do not have the interviewers' email addresses, and the recruiter will pass along your thanks if appropriate.

**What to write:**

```
Subject: Thank you - [Position] Interview

Hi [Recruiter Name],

Thank you for coordinating today's interviews. I enjoyed the
conversations and came away even more excited about the
opportunity.

I particularly appreciated [specific thing --- e.g., "the
system design discussion with Sarah about scaling the
notification system" or "learning about the team's
approach to reliability engineering"].

Please let me know if there's anything else you need from me
for the next steps.

Best regards,
[Your name]
```

**What NOT to write:**
- A lengthy recap of your performance
- Excuses for anything that went wrong ("I know I struggled with the coding problem because...")
- Requests for hints about the outcome
- Anything that sounds desperate

### Following Up with the Recruiter

If the recruiter said they would get back to you in X days and X days have passed:

**Day X+1:** Send a brief follow-up. "Hi [Name], I wanted to check in on next steps. I'm still very interested in the position."

**Day X+5:** Follow up again, slightly more directly. "Hi [Name], I'm following up on my interview from [date]. Could you share an update on the timeline?"

**Day X+10:** If still no response, send a final follow-up. If they do not respond, they have likely moved forward with other candidates but have not gotten around to telling you.

Do not follow up more than three times. It does not help and may come across as pushy.

### Evaluating Your Own Performance Honestly

After the emotional dust settles (give it 24--48 hours), rate yourself honestly:

```
SELF-EVALUATION RUBRIC

For each round, rate yourself 1-5 on:

Communication:
  1: Long silences, mumbled explanations
  2: Spoke but was disorganized
  3: Clear enough, some rambling
  4: Clear and structured throughout
  5: Excellent --- articulated thoughts like a presentation

Problem-Solving:
  1: Could not make progress
  2: Needed significant hints, got partially there
  3: Solved with some hints or solved brute force
  4: Solved optimally with minor hints
  5: Solved optimally independently, handled follow-ups

Code Quality:
  1: Couldn't write working code
  2: Code worked but was messy and buggy
  3: Code mostly worked, found/fixed bugs
  4: Clean code, minor issues
  5: Clean, correct, well-structured code

Composure:
  1: Visible panic, gave up
  2: Stressed but continued
  3: Managed stress, occasional wobbles
  4: Calm and professional throughout
  5: Confident, composed, even under pressure

Overall impression I likely left:
  1: No Hire
  2: Lean No Hire
  3: Lean Hire
  4: Hire
  5: Strong Hire
```

### Learning from the Experience Regardless of Outcome

Every interview is a data point. Win or lose, extract the lessons.

**If you got the offer:** What worked? What techniques from your preparation were most valuable? What can you share with others?

**If you did not get the offer:** Where exactly did you struggle? Was it technical knowledge, communication, problem-solving approach, or composure? Your debrief notes will tell you. Build a study plan that targets those specific weaknesses.

### The Debrief Call: What to Say and Ask

If the recruiter schedules a debrief call, this typically means either an offer or a rejection with feedback.

**If it is an offer:**
- Express genuine enthusiasm: "I'm really excited about this."
- Ask about the team and role: "Can you tell me more about which team I'd be joining?"
- Ask about the offer details: "What does the compensation package look like?"
- Do NOT accept immediately: "I'd like to take a few days to review the details. When do you need my answer by?"

**If it is a rejection:**
- Stay gracious: "I appreciate the opportunity and the feedback."
- Ask for specific feedback: "Are there specific areas where I could improve?"
- Ask about the future: "Would it be appropriate to apply again in the future? After how long?"
- Maintain the relationship: "I enjoyed meeting the team. I'd love to stay in touch."


### Junior → FAANG: Post-Interview Protocol

After each interview round, write down your immediate impressions within 30 minutes while the memory is fresh: which questions were asked, how you answered, what went well, and what you would answer differently. This post-interview log has two uses: it helps you adjust for subsequent rounds in the same loop, and it builds a long-term database of interview questions and your performance on them for future preparation cycles.

### Senior @ FAANG: Post-Interview Protocol as a Learning System

Senior candidates should run a structured post-interview debrief that includes both performance evaluation and cultural observation. Performance questions: which dimensions felt strongest, which felt weakest, were there topics that surprised you (indicating a gap in your preparation), and was your time management within each round effective? Cultural questions: did the interviewers seem engaged and intellectually curious, was the problem framing creative or formulaic, did the interviewers ask about your questions thoughtfully? These cultural observations inform your offer decision as much as your performance assessment. The post-interview debrief should be written down, not just reflected on internally, because written reflection produces more systematic and accurate analysis than internal reflection.
