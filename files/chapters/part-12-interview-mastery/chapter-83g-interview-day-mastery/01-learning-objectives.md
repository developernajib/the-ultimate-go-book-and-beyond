## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. **Manage interview anxiety** using evidence-based techniques from performance psychology, transforming nervous energy into focused performance
2. **Execute a complete interview day protocol** from the night before through the post-interview debrief, with nothing left to chance
3. **Follow minute-by-minute playbooks** for coding, system design, and behavioral interviews that keep you on track even when your brain wants to panic
4. **Recover from every common failure mode** --- blanking on a problem, writing buggy code, running out of time, facing a hostile interviewer --- using specific, practiced strategies
5. **Communicate effectively under pressure** by using rehearsed phrases and scripts that demonstrate competence even when you are struggling
6. **Optimize your virtual and in-person interview environment** to eliminate technical distractions and present your best self
7. **Write clean Go code under time pressure** by avoiding the specific mistakes that Go candidates make when stressed
8. **Manage multi-company interview processes** strategically, including competing offers, deadline extensions, and graceful rejections


### Junior → FAANG: Interview Day Mastery Learning Objectives

The learning objectives for this chapter are practical: by the end, you should have a specific pre-interview routine, know how to handle getting stuck in each interview type, and have a plan for the between-round reset protocol. These are not abstract concepts. They require specific decisions about what you will do in each scenario.

### Senior @ FAANG: Objectives as Performance Protocols

Senior candidates should translate these learning objectives into written protocols before the interview loop. Write down: your specific pre-interview morning routine (times, activities, nutrition), your getting-stuck protocol for coding (what you say out loud, how you step through the problem), your getting-stuck protocol for system design (how you use clarifying questions to buy thinking time), and your between-round reset routine (duration, activities, mental state goals). Having these protocols written down and practiced in mocks means you can execute them under pressure without deliberating.
