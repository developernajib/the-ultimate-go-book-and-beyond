## 83B.9 The 12-Week FAANG Preparation Plan

### Overview

The plan is structured in four overlapping phases rather than sequential blocks because skills compound: early DSA practice builds the pattern recognition needed for system design, and system design study reinforces the data structure choices that appear in hard coding problems. Behavioral preparation is intentionally placed in weeks 7-9 after your technical foundation is solid, trying to craft compelling impact stories before you can articulate technical decisions leads to vague, unquantified answers. The final two weeks shift from learning to performance: mock interviews under real time pressure expose gaps that self-study cannot reveal.

### Junior → FAANG: Following a Structured 12-Week Plan

A 12-week preparation plan is the right timeframe for a comprehensive FAANG preparation from a strong baseline. Weeks 1-4 focus on algorithmic fundamentals: arrays, strings, trees, graphs, dynamic programming. Weeks 5-8 add system design and behavioral preparation in parallel with continued coding practice. Weeks 9-12 shift to mock interviews, company-specific preparation, and refinement of weak areas identified in mocks. The most common failure mode is spending all 12 weeks on coding and neglecting system design and behavioral preparation until the last two weeks.

### Senior @ FAANG: 12-Week Plan as a Senior Engineer Calibration Protocol

Senior candidates using a 12-week plan should weight the dimensions differently from junior candidates. Weeks 1-2 should include a rigorous self-assessment: mock interviews for coding, system design, and behavioral with experienced interviewers, not self-assessment. The self-assessment creates a baseline that the remaining 10 weeks are explicitly designed to improve. Senior candidates often find their behavioral preparation is the weakest because they have been writing code rather than practicing STAR stories. The L6 system design bar also requires depth in distributed systems topics (consensus protocols, distributed transactions, vector clocks) that junior candidates are not tested on. Allocate preparation time proportionally to the gap between your baseline and the target bar, not proportionally to how much you enjoy each topic.
