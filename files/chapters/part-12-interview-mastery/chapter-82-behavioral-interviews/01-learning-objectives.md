## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Master the STAR method for structured responses
- Prepare compelling stories from your experience
- Answer leadership principle questions effectively
- Handle difficult behavioral questions with confidence
- Adapt your stories for different company cultures
- Demonstrate senior-level impact and ownership


### Junior → FAANG: Setting Clear Learning Goals for Behavioral Prep

Learning objectives for behavioral interview preparation are often vague, which leads to superficial preparation. Make your objectives specific and verifiable. Instead of 'understand the STAR method', write 'write three STAR stories about technical failures with measurable results'. Instead of 'know Amazon leadership principles', write 'for each of the 16 LPs, identify one specific story from my experience that demonstrates it'. Verifiable objectives force you to actually produce the stories rather than just reading about the method.

### Senior @ FAANG: Learning Objectives as a Preparation Audit for Senior Candidates

Senior candidates approaching behavioral preparation should audit their story bank against a structured rubric: scope of impact (individual, team, org, company), level of ambiguity handled, type of influence exercised (technical, relational, authority-based), and outcome quality (measurable business metric, not just 'the team was happy'). The objective is not to have stories for every category but to identify which categories are thin and deliberately seek stories from your experience that fill those gaps. If your story bank is heavily weighted toward individual technical achievements and light on organizational influence and cross-team leadership, that is a risk for L6 bar-raiser rounds at companies like Google and Amazon, where staff-level scope is a prerequisite for the offer.
