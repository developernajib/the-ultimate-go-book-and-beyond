### Key Takeaways

1. **STAR is your foundation** - Situation, Task, Action, Result
2. **Build a versatile story bank** - Stories can be adapted
3. **Know the company values** - Amazon, Google, Meta differ
4. **Own your failures** - Shows growth and self-awareness
5. **Quantify results** - Numbers make stories memorable
6. **Practice out loud** - Reading isn't the same as speaking
7. **Pivot gracefully** - Adjust stories mid-answer if needed
8. **Scale for seniority** - Junior focuses on learning, Staff on strategy

### Junior → FAANG: Final Behavioral Interview Preparation Checklist

Before your behavioral interviews, confirm: you have 8-10 specific STAR stories covering all major behavioral themes. You have mapped each story to the target company's leadership principles. You have practiced each story out loud at least three times. You have a strong failure story with genuine self-reflection. And you have at least one story that demonstrates influence beyond your immediate team. This is the minimum preparation bar for a FAANG behavioral interview at any level.

### Senior @ FAANG: Behavioral Readiness at the L6/L7 Level

Senior readiness for behavioral interviews requires an additional layer: your stories must consistently demonstrate org-level impact, leadership through ambiguity, and multi-team coordination. The checklist for L6: at least two stories where you made a decision with incomplete information and a high cost of being wrong. At least one story where you changed the direction of a significant initiative through technical influence rather than authority. At least one story demonstrating how you developed another engineer's judgment (coaching, code review quality, mentoring on design). And at least one story about handling a difficult colleague or stakeholder relationship with a positive long-term outcome. If any of these are missing from your story bank, target an experience from the last 3 years that comes closest and invest time in developing that story before the interview loop.
