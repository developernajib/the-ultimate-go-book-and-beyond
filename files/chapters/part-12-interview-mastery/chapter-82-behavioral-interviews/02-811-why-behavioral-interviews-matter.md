## 82.1 Why Behavioral Interviews Matter

### The Purpose Behind Behavioral Questions

Technical skills establish a baseline, but behavioral interviews determine whether you can operate effectively inside a specific organization. Interviewers use structured behavioral questions because past behavior in concrete situations is a far stronger predictor of future performance than hypothetical answers. The level-specific expectations are particularly important: a senior engineer who answers like a junior, focusing on personal tasks rather than cross-team impact, signals a ceiling that disqualifies them regardless of coding ability.

### Junior → FAANG: Understanding the Purpose of Behavioral Interviews

Behavioral interviews exist because technical skill is necessary but not sufficient for success in a high-performing engineering organization. The research basis for structured behavioral interviews is the principle that past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior. When an interviewer asks 'tell me about a time you disagreed with a technical decision', they are probing for judgment, communication style, and whether you can influence without authority. As a junior candidate, the framing mistake is to give hypothetical answers ('I would do X') instead of specific past examples. If you lack a direct example, find the closest analog from a project, academic work, or open source contribution.

### Senior @ FAANG: Why Behavioral Signals Weight More at Senior Levels

At the senior level, behavioral interview results carry more weight relative to technical results than they do for junior roles. The reason is that at L6 and above, the team has established that you can write correct code. The remaining question is whether you can operate as a force multiplier for those around you. Bar-raisers at this level are specifically calibrated to reject candidates who are technically excellent but behaviorally narrow: candidates who avoid conflict, defer all ambiguous decisions, or have impact only on their own work. The behavioral interview at the senior level is a leadership screen. The organizational cost of a false positive (hiring an L6 who cannot lead) is significantly higher than a false positive at L4, which raises the bar accordingly. Understanding this dynamic helps you calibrate the scope and ambiguity of your stories to the level you are targeting.
