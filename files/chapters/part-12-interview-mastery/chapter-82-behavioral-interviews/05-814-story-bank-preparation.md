### Junior → FAANG: Building a Story Bank Before Interviews

A story bank is a personal inventory of work experiences organized by behavioral theme. Build it as a table: each row is a story, each column is a behavioral theme (leadership, conflict, failure, influence, technical decision, prioritization, ambiguity). The goal is to have 8-10 stories that collectively cover all major themes. Each story should be tailored for a 2-minute delivery: enough detail to be concrete, not so much that you lose the interviewer's attention. Practice delivering each story out loud at least three times. Silent review is not sufficient preparation.

### Senior @ FAANG: Story Bank at the Senior Level: Depth, Range, and Adaptability

Senior candidates should approach their story bank with three additional requirements. First, depth: each major story should have a 2-minute version, a 4-minute version (for when the interviewer follows up), and answers ready for common probes ('what would you do differently?', 'how did you handle the team member who disagreed?', 'what was the risk you were most concerned about?'). Second, range: the story bank should include failures and recoveries, not just successes. Bar-raisers specifically probe for intellectual honesty about failure, and a story bank without genuine failure stories reads as defensive. Third, adaptability: each story should be adaptable to multiple behavioral dimensions. A strong story about a technical decision under ambiguity can also address 'a time you handled uncertainty', 'a time you influenced stakeholders', and 'a time you took calculated risk'. Knowing which pivots each story supports makes you more flexible in the actual interview.
