## 82.8 Virtual Interview Tips

### Camera and Setup

Virtual interviews introduce distractions and technical friction that in-person formats do not, a poorly positioned camera reads as disengagement, and audio drops break the narrative flow of your answers at the worst moments. Treating the environment as a controllable variable (lighting, background, network stability) removes the cognitive load of managing technical issues mid-interview so you can focus entirely on the conversation. Work through this checklist 30 minutes before your scheduled time, not in the final seconds, so you have room to recover from any problems.

```
BEFORE THE INTERVIEW:

□ Test video/audio 30 minutes before
□ Good lighting (face the window, not away from it)
□ Camera at eye level
□ Neutral, clean background
□ Stable internet (use ethernet if possible)
□ Close all other applications
□ Silence phone
□ Have water nearby
□ Have notes/stories printed (not visible on screen)

DURING THE INTERVIEW:

□ Look at camera when speaking (not at the screen)
□ Nod to show you're listening
□ Don't interrupt - video lag makes this worse
□ Speak slightly slower than normal
□ Check if they can hear/see you if there's silence
```

### Handling Technical Difficulties

How you react to an unexpected technical failure during a virtual interview is itself a signal of composure under pressure, exactly what behavioral interviews are designed to assess. The scripts below give you ready-made, professional phrases that acknowledge the disruption without dwelling on it, allowing the conversation to resume quickly. Use them verbatim if needed. The goal is to re-establish shared context with the interviewer rather than letting the disruption derail your answer.

```
"I'm sorry, you froze for a moment. Could you repeat
the question?"

"My audio seems to have dropped. Let me try
reconnecting quickly."

"I want to make sure I'm addressing your question -
could you confirm what aspect you'd like me to focus on?"
```


### Junior → FAANG: Virtual Interview Setup and Performance

Virtual interviews have logistical failure modes that in-person interviews do not. Test your setup the day before: audio quality (use a headset, not laptop microphone), lighting (face a light source, do not sit in front of a window), background (neutral or professional), and internet stability (wired connection preferred). During the interview, maintain eye contact by looking at the camera rather than at the interviewer's face on screen. Speak slightly slower than normal because internet audio sometimes introduces small delays. Have water nearby and notes on paper rather than on screen to avoid the appearance of reading answers.

### Senior @ FAANG: Virtual Interview as a Signal of Remote Work Readiness

At the senior level, a virtual interview is also a subtle evaluation of how you will perform in a distributed team environment, which is the default operating model at most FAANG companies post-2020. The quality of your virtual communication, your ability to be concise and engaging over video, and your comfort with asynchronous communication cues (reading the interviewer's interest level through a compressed video stream) all signal your effectiveness as a remote collaborator. Staff-level candidates should also be prepared for virtual whiteboarding tools (Excalidraw, Miro, Google Jamboard) and be fluent in sketching system designs in real time without relying on physical props. Practice designing systems in a shared digital canvas before the interview, because the cognitive overhead of the tool should not subtract from the cognitive budget available for the design itself.
