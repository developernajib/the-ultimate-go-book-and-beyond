## 83.2 Researching Your Market Value

Before you negotiate, you need to know what the market actually pays for your role, level, and location. Without this data, you are guessing, and guessing almost always means leaving money on the table. The good news is that the tech industry has unusually transparent compensation data compared to most fields, so building a solid picture of your market value takes hours, not weeks.

### Data Sources

Several public and semi-public resources let you research compensation ranges before you ever speak to a recruiter. The most reliable source is levels.fyi, which collects verified compensation data broken down by company, level, and location. Cross-referencing multiple sources gives you confidence that your target range is realistic and defensible.

```
PUBLIC DATA:
├── levels.fyi - Verified compensation data by level
├── Glassdoor - Self-reported salaries
├── Blind - Anonymous tech worker discussions
├── LinkedIn Salary - Based on member data
└── Payscale/Salary.com - General market data

CALCULATE YOUR RANGE:
1. Find your target level at target companies
2. Look at 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles
3. Your range: 50th to 75th if competitive candidate
4. Adjust for location (COL) if remote

EXAMPLE RESEARCH:

levels.fyi shows for Senior SWE at Company X:
- Base: $185K - $210K - $235K (25th/50th/75th)
- Stock: $250K - $350K - $450K total
- Bonus: 15% target

Your target: $200K+ base, $350K+ stock
```

### Location Adjustments

Where you live has a significant impact on what companies will offer you. Most large tech companies apply a geographic multiplier to their compensation bands, meaning the same role at the same level can pay 20-40% less if you work from a lower cost-of-living area. Understanding these adjustments is critical when comparing offers across locations, because a lower number in Austin may actually give you more purchasing power than a higher number in San Francisco.

### Junior → FAANG: Researching Your Market Value

Researching market value before negotiating gives you an anchor number based on data rather than hope. Primary sources: Levels.fyi (crowdsourced FAANG TC data by level and location), Glassdoor (less accurate but useful for directional sense), LinkedIn Salary (requires contributing your own data), and conversations with peers at target companies who are willing to share their compensation. The goal is to identify the 50th and 75th percentile TC for your target role, level, and location, so you know what a fair offer looks like and what an excellent offer looks like.

### Senior @ FAANG: Market Value Research as a Negotiation Foundation

Senior candidates should invest 3-4 hours in structured market value research before entering any negotiation. The research should produce: (1) a table of TC by company and level for your target role (using Levels.fyi with level filters for L5/L6/Staff), (2) a specific target TC number based on the 75th percentile for your target level (not the median, because you are negotiating for the best outcome), and (3) a BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement), which is the specific offer you would accept from your second-priority company if your first-priority company's final offer is below your threshold. The BATNA prevents the common negotiation failure of overplaying your hand with your top-priority company out of overconfidence in alternatives that are not yet concrete.
