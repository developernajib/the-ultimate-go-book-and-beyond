## 83.4 Negotiation Scripts

This section provides word-for-word scripts for the five most critical moments in a compensation negotiation. These scripts are not meant to be memorized verbatim. They are templates that you should adapt to your voice and situation. Practice saying them out loud until the structure feels natural, because confident delivery matters as much as the words themselves.

### Receiving the Offer

When you first receive an offer, your immediate goal is to express genuine enthusiasm without committing to anything. Accepting on the spot is one of the most common and costly mistakes candidates make. Instead, thank the recruiter, ask for the offer in writing, and buy yourself time to research, prepare a counter, and evaluate any competing offers you may have.

```
SCRIPT 1: INITIAL RESPONSE

Recruiter: "We'd like to offer you the Senior Engineer position
at $195K base, $300K RSUs over 4 years, 15% bonus target, and
a $30K signing bonus."

You: "Thank you so much - I'm really excited about this
opportunity and the team. I appreciate you putting this
together. I'd like to take some time to review the full
details. Can you send me the written offer? I'll review
it and get back to you by [specific date]."

WHAT YOU'RE DOING:
✓ Showing enthusiasm (they need to feel wanted)
✓ Not accepting immediately
✓ Buying time to evaluate and prepare counter
✓ Getting it in writing
```

### Making Your Counter

The counter-offer is the most important single communication in the entire negotiation. A well-structured counter opens with enthusiasm, cites specific market data, connects your unique value to the team's needs, and makes clear, specific asks. Vague requests like "I was hoping for more" give the recruiter nothing to work with, while overly aggressive demands can damage the relationship. The script below strikes the right balance.

```
SCRIPT 2: THE COUNTER

"Thank you again for the offer. I'm genuinely excited about
joining [Company]. I've reviewed the package and would like
to discuss a few adjustments.

Based on my research on levels.fyi and conversations with
peers in similar roles, the typical range for a Senior
Engineer with my experience is $210-230K base and $350-400K
in equity.

Given my specific experience with [relevant skill], which
directly addresses the [specific challenge] you mentioned
the team is facing, I believe I can make an immediate impact.

Would it be possible to increase the base to $215K and
the equity to $400K? I'm also wondering if there's
flexibility on the signing bonus to help offset the
unvested equity I'm leaving."

STRUCTURE:
1. Express enthusiasm
2. Cite specific data sources
3. Connect your value to their needs
4. Make specific asks
5. Keep the door open for discussion
```

### Handling Pushback

Pushback is normal and expected. When a recruiter says "that is the top of our band," they are not ending the negotiation. They are telling you that one particular lever is maxed out. Your job at this point is to pivot gracefully to other components of the package. Companies often have more flexibility on equity, signing bonuses, and review timelines than they do on base salary, so treat pushback as a redirect rather than a rejection.

```
SCRIPT 3: WHEN THEY PUSH BACK

Recruiter: "Unfortunately, $195K is at the top of our band
for this level. We can't go higher on base."

You: "I understand there are constraints on base salary.
I appreciate you explaining that. Given that, could we look
at other components of the package? Specifically:

1. Could we increase the equity grant? Even an additional
   $50K over 4 years would help close the gap.

2. Is there flexibility on the signing bonus to help
   with the transition?

3. What about a one-time performance review in 6 months
   with potential for adjustment if I exceed expectations?"

TECHNIQUES:
✓ Accept the constraint gracefully
✓ Pivot to other levers
✓ Offer multiple options (they can say yes to something)
✓ Show you're reasonable while still advocating
```

### Handling Multiple Offers

Having multiple offers is the single strongest position you can be in during a negotiation. A competing offer provides objective, external proof of your market value and creates urgency for the company to act. The key is to use competing offers honestly and transparently. Lying about offers you do not have, or inflating the numbers, is a career-ending risk in a small industry where recruiters talk to each other.

```
SCRIPT 4: USING COMPETING OFFERS

"Thank you for the updated offer. I really appreciate you
working with me on this. I want to be transparent with you -
I'm also in the final stages with [Company B], and their
offer is currently higher in [specific component].

That said, [Company A] remains my first choice because of
[specific reason - team, product, growth opportunity]. If
we can get closer on [specific ask], I'm ready to sign today.

Is there anything else you can do on the equity grant?"

WHAT YOU'RE DOING:
✓ Being transparent (builds trust)
✓ Showing preference (they want to win you)
✓ Being specific about the gap
✓ Indicating willingness to close quickly
```

### Accepting the Offer

Once you have reached an agreement, the acceptance itself should be warm, definitive, and confirm every negotiated detail. Restate the exact numbers in your acceptance message so both sides have a clear written record of what was agreed upon. This protects you if the offer letter arrives with different numbers, and it gives the recruiter an opportunity to correct any misunderstandings before you sign.

```
SCRIPT 5: FINAL ACCEPTANCE

"Thank you for working with me on this. I'm thrilled to
accept the offer of $210K base, $380K equity, $45K signing
bonus, and 15% target bonus.

Can you send the updated offer letter for my signature?
I'm ready to proceed and give notice to my current employer.

I really appreciate how professional this process has been.
I'm excited to join the team and start contributing."

AFTER ACCEPTING:
□ Get final offer in writing
□ Confirm start date
□ Understand pre-boarding requirements
□ Plan your resignation timeline
□ Send thank-you to recruiter and hiring manager
```


### Junior → FAANG: Negotiation Scripts and Phrases

Negotiation scripts give you exact phrases for the most common negotiation conversations. Key phrases: 'I'm very excited about this opportunity. Based on my research and the scope of the role, I was expecting a TC in the range of -. Is there flexibility?' and 'I have another offer from [Company] at  TC. I'd prefer to work at [Target Company] if we can get to that range.' These scripts are direct, specific, and non-confrontational. Avoid vague language ('I was hoping for more') and avoid apologizing for negotiating (negotiating is expected and not offensive).

### Senior @ FAANG: Senior Negotiation Scripts: Specificity, Competing Offers, and Package Components

Senior candidates should have scripts prepared for the specific negotiation scenarios that arise at their level: negotiating equity (not just total TC), handling a base salary band that cannot be exceeded (pivot to equity or signing bonus), declining an extended deadline when you are ready to decide, and gracefully declining a lower-priority offer without burning a relationship (you may interview with them again in 2-3 years). The equity negotiation script is specific: 'I understand the base is at the top of the band. Can we look at the initial RSU grant? Based on my research, the 75th percentile for this level and location is X RSUs. The current offer is at Y. Is there flexibility there?' This specificity signals that you have done your homework and are negotiating based on data, not entitlement.
