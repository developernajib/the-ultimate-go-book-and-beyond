### Key Negotiation Principles

The ten principles below represent the non-negotiable rules of compensation negotiation. Every one of them is grounded in the detailed scenarios and scripts covered earlier in this chapter. Print this list, save it on your phone, or tape it to your wall, because in the heat of a live negotiation you will be tempted to break at least one of them.

```
THE 10 COMMANDMENTS OF NEGOTIATION:

1. NEVER name a number first (let them anchor)
2. ALWAYS negotiate total comp, not just base
3. NEVER accept on the spot (take 24-72 hours)
4. ALWAYS have your research ready (data wins)
5. NEVER lie about offers (it's a small industry)
6. ALWAYS get everything in writing
7. NEVER burn bridges (you may see these people again)
8. ALWAYS express enthusiasm (they want to feel wanted)
9. NEVER negotiate against yourself
10. ALWAYS know your walk-away point (and don't share it)
```

### Core Negotiation Takeaways

- **Never give a number first**: Volunteering a salary expectation anchors the negotiation in the company's favour - redirect with "What is the budgeted range for this role?" to let them move first.
- **Total compensation vs base salary**: Base salary is one component. Evaluate equity (type, vesting, cliff), annual bonus, sign-on bonus, benefits, and 401k match together as a single package.
- **Competing offers as leverage**: A genuine competing offer is the strongest negotiating tool available - it establishes your market value with concrete, external evidence rather than personal opinion.
- **Equity vesting and cliff**: Understand the vesting schedule (typically 4 years with a 1-year cliff), the equity type (ISO vs RSU vs NSO), and the current 409A valuation before placing value on the grant.
- **Negotiating non-salary items**: When base is fixed, negotiate signing bonus, additional PTO, remote work flexibility, professional development budget, or an earlier performance review cycle.
- **Timing of negotiation**: Begin negotiating only after you have a written offer in hand - raising compensation before an offer signals misplaced priorities and weakens your position.
- **Written offers vs verbal**: Verbal agreements are unenforceable. Always request that any agreed change be reflected in an updated written offer letter before declining other opportunities.
- **BATNA (best alternative)**: Know your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement before every negotiation - if you have no walkaway option, you have no leverage, and preparation is the only remedy.
- **When to ask for more time**: It is professionally acceptable to request 24-72 hours to review an offer. frame it as diligence, not hesitation, and always give a specific date by which you will respond.
- **Professional tone throughout**: Negotiation is the start of your working relationship with this company - remain enthusiastic, collaborative, and gracious regardless of the outcome.

### Junior → FAANG: Consolidating This Chapter into a Repeatable System

The ten bullet points in this summary define the minimum viable negotiation system for a junior or mid-level candidate. If you internalize only these ten principles and nothing else from the chapter, you will outperform the majority of candidates who negotiate without preparation.

The highest-leverage habit to build immediately: research your market value on levels.fyi for every role you apply to, before the phone screen, not after the offer. This converts the compensation conversation from a reactive scramble to a proactive, data-grounded discussion. It also changes how you present yourself in the screen because you know what a reasonable number looks like and can communicate that confidence without stating a specific expectation.

BATNA development for first-time negotiators: if you have no competing offers and no current job to fall back on, your BATNA is weak, and the negotiation will reflect that. The most effective way to strengthen your position is to run multiple processes in parallel. Three to five active interview processes create a real BATNA. A single active process does not. Plan your job search accordingly.

Interview entry-point framing: the negotiation chapter is the last chapter you study but the first process you begin, because the signals you send during the interview loop already shape the recruiter's view of your compensation expectations. Communicate your level of seniority, your scope of impact, and your market awareness during the technical and behavioral rounds, not just during the offer call.

### Senior @ FAANG: The Chapter Summary as a Governance Playbook for Staff-Level Decisions

For L6 and above candidates, this chapter summary functions as a governance playbook. Each principle maps to a specific risk category that experienced engineers face.

"Never give a number first" prevents anchoring risk, which is highest at the staff level because the variance in total comp across the L6 band at FAANG companies is $200,000 to $400,000. The wrong anchor in either direction is a large and compounding financial error.

"Total compensation vs base salary" is the principle that most distinguishes senior from junior candidates in practice. Staff engineers who negotiate only base salary leave equity on the table at exactly the level where equity is the dominant component of compensation. At L6, equity often represents 60 to 70 percent of total comp. A 10 percent improvement in the RSU grant is worth 4 to 6 times a 10 percent improvement in base.

"BATNA" at the staff level is not just competing offers. It is your assessment of the full set of alternatives including your current role, its trajectory, and its compensation growth. A staff engineer at a strong company with a clear promotion path to Principal and a 15 percent annual comp increase has a strong BATNA even without external offers. Documenting this clearly before you enter a negotiation prevents you from undervaluing your alternatives and making concessions that are not necessary.

Maturity model for negotiation sophistication at staff level: Level 1 is knowing the ten commandments. Level 2 is executing them fluently under pressure. Level 3 is using the negotiation conversation itself as a signal to the company about how you operate: structured, data-driven, collaborative, and clear about your reasoning. The best staff-level negotiations close with both parties feeling that they made a good decision together. That outcome is achievable and it is the standard to aim for.

Post-acceptance governance: after you sign, file the complete negotiation record (all emails, offer letters, verbal agreement summaries, and your personal decision matrix) in a permanent, accessible location. This record is the starting point for your next negotiation, either at your annual review or at your next company. Senior engineers who maintain this record across multiple negotiations build a calibrated, empirical understanding of what works, what the real constraints are at each company, and how to improve their approach each cycle.
