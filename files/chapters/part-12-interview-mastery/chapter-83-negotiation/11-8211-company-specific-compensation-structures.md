## 83.10 Company-Specific Compensation Structures

Every major tech company has a distinct compensation philosophy, and understanding these differences is essential for effective negotiation. Some companies cap base salary and rely heavily on stock (Amazon), while others offer higher base with moderate equity (Stripe). This section provides estimated compensation ranges and negotiation tips for the companies candidates ask about most frequently. Use these as benchmarks, but always verify current data on levels.fyi since ranges shift year to year.

### Google Compensation Structure

Google is known for competitive compensation across all levels, with a relatively balanced split between base salary, RSUs, and bonuses. Their RSU refresher grants are front-loaded (33-33-22-12 over four years), which means your total compensation actually increases in years two and three if you perform well. The signing bonus is often used as a flexible lever when RSU grants are at the top of the band.

### Amazon Compensation Structure

Amazon has the most unusual compensation structure among FAANG companies. Base salary is capped (Amazon raised the cap from $160K to $350K in 2022, though most offers still land far below it), and RSUs follow a heavily back-loaded vesting schedule of 5-15-40-40 over four years. This means your Year 1 and Year 2 compensation will be significantly lower than Years 3 and 4, which is why Amazon offers large signing bonuses split over the first two years to bridge the gap. Understanding this structure is critical for comparing an Amazon offer to offers from other companies.

Since going public in 2025, their equity grants have transitioned to RSUs, which makes them easier to value compared to the pre-IPO options that earlier employees received. ### Startup Negotiation Strategies

Negotiating with a startup requires a fundamentally different mindset than negotiating with a public company. Your equity is not liquid, its value depends entirely on future events you cannot control, and the company may not survive long enough for your shares to vest. The upside, however, can be enormous if the company succeeds. Before accepting a startup equity grant, you need to understand the math behind dilution, liquidation preferences, and exercise windows so you can make an informed bet.

```
STARTUP EQUITY EVALUATION:

Questions to Ask:
1. What's the current valuation?
2. Total shares outstanding?
3. What's my grant as % of company?
4. What's the liquidation preference on preferred shares?
5. When was last funding round?
6. What's the runway?

EQUITY CALCULATION EXAMPLE:

Offer: 50,000 shares
Outstanding: 10,000,000 shares
Your %: 0.5%

Valuation: $100M → Your equity = $500K
Valuation: $1B → Your equity = $5M (at exit)

BUT consider:
- Dilution (future rounds)
- Liquidation preference
- Vesting schedule
- Exercise price vs fair market value
STARTUP VS BIG TECH COMPARISON:

┌─────────────────┬────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│ Factor          │ Startup        │ Big Tech        │
├─────────────────┼────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Base Salary     │ 70-90% market  │ 100%+ market    │
│ Equity          │ High risk/upside│ Liquid, stable │
│ Bonus           │ Rare           │ Standard        │
│ Benefits        │ Basic          │ Premium         │
│ Stability       │ Variable       │ High            │
│ Growth          │ Fast-track     │ Structured      │
│ Exit Potential  │ IPO/Acquisition│ Refreshers      │
└─────────────────┴────────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

### Counter-Offer Scripts by Company

Each company responds differently to counter-offers because their compensation structures and internal approval processes vary. A counter that works well at Google, where RSU is the most flexible lever, will not work at Amazon, where base is capped and signing bonuses are the primary tool for bridging gaps. The scripts below are tailored to the specific structure and negotiation culture of each company.

```
GOOGLE COUNTER-OFFER SCRIPT:

"Thank you for the offer. I'm very excited about joining Google.
I've done research on levels.fyi and Glassdoor for L5 engineers
with my experience profile. Based on this data, I was expecting
RSU closer to $400K over 4 years rather than $300K. Given my
competing offer from [Company] and my background in [specific
relevant tech], I believe $400K RSU would be appropriate.
Is there flexibility on the equity component?"
AMAZON COUNTER-OFFER SCRIPT:

"I appreciate the offer and am excited about the role. I understand
Amazon has a base cap, but I was hoping for a stronger total
compensation package. Given the back-loaded RSU structure, could
we increase the year 1-2 sign-on bonus to $200K split evenly?
This would help bridge the compensation gap until RSU vests.
Alternatively, increasing the RSU grant to $500K would also work."
META COUNTER-OFFER SCRIPT:

"Thank you for the E5 offer. I'm very interested in joining Meta.
Based on my research and a competing offer I have, I was targeting
a total compensation package around $600K in year one. The current
offer is at $500K. Would it be possible to increase the RSU grant
from $500K to $650K over 4 years? I have [X competing offer] but
Meta is my top choice if we can close this gap."
```

### Junior → FAANG: Reading Company Structures to Know Which Lever to Pull

The most common junior mistake when comparing compensation packages across companies is comparing Year 1 total comp as if it were the same number at every company. Amazon's back-loaded vesting schedule means Year 1 at Amazon looks worse than Year 1 at Meta for identical four-year totals. Google's front-loaded refresher structure means your Year 2 and Year 3 comp can be significantly higher than the initial grant implies. You need to model at least four years, not one.

Prerequisite: build a simple spreadsheet before any negotiation call. Columns are Year 1 through Year 4. Rows are base, equity vest per year (using the actual schedule, not a flat annual average), bonus at target, and sign-on. Sum each column. Then compare. This takes 20 minutes and completely changes the picture for Amazon offers in particular.

Startup equity reality check: the worked example in this section (0.5% at $100M valuation) uses a pre-dilution percentage. By the time of acquisition or IPO, dilution from future rounds commonly reduces that percentage by 30 to 60 percent. Model the exit scenario at current valuation, and add a 40% dilution assumption to get a realistic expectation.

Entry-point interview framing: when a recruiter tells you the equity grant is "valued at $X," ask what stock price they used and whether that is the 409A or the most recent preferred round price. These can differ by a factor of two at late-stage startups.

### Senior @ FAANG: Compensation Structure as a Platform-Level Decision

At the staff and principal level, the choice of company is not just a compensation decision. It is a platform decision that determines the scope of problems you can work on, the resources you can deploy, and the network you build.

Google compensation governance: the L6 band is wide, and the internal process for exceeding the band requires a specific approval chain. Candidates coming in at the top of the L6 band with exceptional competing offers have successfully used the "market adjustment" process to receive grants above the standard ceiling. The argument is not "I deserve more." The argument is "the market has priced this skill set above your standard band, and here is the evidence." The evidence must include a specific competing offer at a comparable or higher level.

Amazon structural analysis at senior levels: the RSU-heavy mix and the 5-15-40-40 vesting schedule create a unique cash flow pattern that most staff-level candidates underweight. Year 1 and Year 2 cash compensation at Amazon is often 20-30% lower than at comparable companies even with the sign-on bridge. For candidates with significant financial obligations (mortgage, family, etc.), this temporal mismatch matters and should be addressed directly: "The back-loaded schedule creates cash flow pressure in years one and two. Can we increase the sign-on to $X to bridge that gap?" This is a legitimate ask and Amazon recruiters understand the structural reason behind it.

Meta's refresher governance: Meta's semi-annual performance reviews determine both your rating and your refresher grant amount. An E5 engineer who achieves "Greatly Exceeds Expectations" in their first two performance cycles can receive a refresher that effectively doubles their original four-year grant, while a "Meets Expectations" rating yields a modest refresher. Asking about the distribution of ratings on the specific team you are joining is not rude. It is how you make an informed financial decision.

Stripe post-IPO analysis: for staff-level candidates considering Stripe, the transition from options to RSUs changes the risk profile fundamentally. Pre-IPO option holders benefited from strike prices set at the 409A valuation, which was a fraction of the preferred share price. Post-IPO RSU holders receive shares priced at the market, which means the upside is correlated with general stock performance rather than company-specific liquidity events. Negotiate the RSU grant size based on the current price with a conservative expected return model, not the pre-IPO option-equivalent math.

Startup negotiation at staff level requires a liquidation preference analysis that most candidates skip. If the startup has raised multiple rounds of preferred shares with a 1x or 2x liquidation preference, common stock holders (which is you, after option exercise) are paid only after preferred holders recover their investment. In a scenario where the company exits at 1.5x the last-round valuation, common shareholders may receive nothing. Ask the CFO or a trusted contact to walk you through the cap table structure and the waterfall before accepting any startup equity.
