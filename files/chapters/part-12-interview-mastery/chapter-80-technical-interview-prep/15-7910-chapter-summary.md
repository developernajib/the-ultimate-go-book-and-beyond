## 80.14 Core Concepts Recap

### Key Takeaways

1. **Understand Go's philosophy**: Simplicity, composition, and explicit error handling
2. **Master concurrency**: G-M-P model, channels, synchronization primitives
3. **Know the memory model**: Happens-before relationships, escape analysis
4. **Practice live coding**: Clear structure, edge cases, test thinking
5. **Prepare for all levels**: Language basics to system design

### Interview Preparation Checklist

- [ ] Can explain Go's type system and interfaces
- [ ] Understand slice and map internals
- [ ] Can write concurrent code without race conditions
- [ ] Know context package usage patterns
- [ ] Understand GC and memory optimization
- [ ] Can debug production issues
- [ ] Familiar with testing and benchmarking
- [ ] Know common algorithms in Go

### Practice Resources

1. **LeetCode** - Practice DSA with Go
2. **Go Playground** - Quick experiments
3. **Exercism** - Go track for idiomatic code

### Junior → FAANG: Using the Summary as a Spaced Repetition Anchor

Chapter summaries are most useful as spaced repetition anchors. After your first read-through of the chapter, use this summary as a daily review prompt during the two weeks before your first screen. Read each key takeaway and try to recall the specific technique or example from the chapter without looking back. If you cannot recall it, that is the gap to address that day.

The summary references the same five preparation domains this chapter covers: Go-specific idioms, live coding mechanics, mock interview practice, pitfall avoidance, and company-specific research. Treat each as a separate preparation track, not a linear progression. You can work on live coding mechanics every day without finishing the Go idioms review.

Entry-point interview signal: candidates who reference specific techniques by name during interviews ("I am applying the four-step framework: clarify, pattern, code, test") demonstrate that their preparation was structured rather than ad hoc. Interviewers interpret structured preparation as a signal of professional discipline.

### Senior @ FAANG: Chapter Summary as a Team-Level Resource

For senior and staff engineers, a well-organized chapter summary is not just personal study material. It is the kind of resource that can be adapted into an internal interview preparation guide for your team or organization. If you are joining FAANG and will be asked to interview candidates within your first six months (which is standard), being able to quickly orient new interviewers to the evaluation criteria is a direct contribution.

The five resources listed at the end of this section (LeetCode, Go Playground, Exercism, Go by Example, Effective Go) represent a calibrated toolset. At the senior level, you might add to this list based on your specialization: for distributed systems Go, the Go blog posts on the scheduler and GC are essential reading. For high-performance Go, the `perf` tool, `pprof`, and the `benchstat` comparison tool are the standard toolkit.

Staff-level governance: great preparation resources, when shared internally, become a competitive advantage for a team. If your organization does not have a curated Go interview preparation guide, creating one (drawing from resources like this chapter) is a lightweight staff-level contribution that improves the quality and consistency of your team's hiring process over time.
