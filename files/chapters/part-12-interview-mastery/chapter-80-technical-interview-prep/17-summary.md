## Summary

- **FAANG interview structure**: Expect multiple rounds covering coding, system design, behavioral, and sometimes a domain-specific or bar-raiser round - each targeting a distinct dimension of engineering ability.
- **Coding round expectations**: You will be assessed on correctness, efficiency, code quality, and communication - not just whether the solution compiles.
- **Talk through your approach**: Narrating your thought process before writing code signals seniority and lets interviewers guide you, turning the session into a collaborative dialogue.
- **Time complexity as a requirement**: Always state and justify the Big-O complexity of your solution. interviewers expect it and will probe further if you skip it.
- **Handling edge cases**: Proactively identify and test boundary conditions (empty input, overflow, duplicates) - it demonstrates thoroughness that separates good candidates from great ones.
- **Behavioral stories using STAR**: Prepare structured Situation-Task-Action-Result narratives for common themes (conflict, failure, leadership) before every interview loop.
- **System design interview framework**: Clarify scope, estimate capacity, sketch high-level architecture, select databases and services, address bottlenecks - follow a repeatable structure to avoid running out of time.
- **The value of mock interviews**: Simulated interviews under time pressure expose weaknesses that solo practice never reveals. Use peers, Pramp, or paid services regularly.
- **Company-specific culture fit**: Research the company's engineering blog, leadership principles, and recent product decisions - interviewers notice when candidates have done their homework.
- **Negotiating after an offer**: The offer is not the finish line. compensation is negotiable and companies expect it - always evaluate total comp, not just base salary, before responding.

### Junior → FAANG: Treating the Summary as a Readiness Checklist

The six summary bullets in this file are a readiness checklist for your first FAANG loop. If you can expand on each bullet with a specific technique and a concrete example of that technique in practice, you are ready for a phone screen. If any bullet still feels abstract, that is the gap to close before scheduling.

The mock interview bullet deserves special emphasis for junior candidates. Most junior candidates underestimate how different performing under real observation feels compared to solo practice. Even one recorded mock interview, reviewed afterward, reveals verbal habits (filler words, long silences, unclear narration) that are invisible in solo practice.

Company culture research is not optional. Interviewers at FAANG companies notice when a candidate references the company's published engineering decisions. Reading the last 6 months of an engineering blog takes 3 hours and materially affects your perceived interest and preparation level.

### Senior @ FAANG: Using This Summary as a Gap Analysis for Senior-Specific Preparation

For senior candidates, this summary points to six preparation domains, but the senior-specific depth in each is not fully captured in the summary bullets. Use the summary as a starting point and add senior-specific criteria to each:

FAANG interview structure at senior level: you will likely face a bar-raiser round and a leadership round in addition to the standard loop. Prepare behavioral stories that demonstrate organizational impact, not just individual execution.

Go-specific performance and runtime knowledge: the senior bar requires production experience with pprof, escape analysis, the race detector, and scheduler internals. Candidates who can cite a specific incident and the Go tooling they used to diagnose it consistently outperform candidates who describe these tools abstractly.

System design at senior level: the framework is necessary but not sufficient. Senior candidates are evaluated on whether they proactively address failure modes, data consistency models, and operational concerns without prompting.

Negotiation at senior level: total compensation structures at FAANG are significantly more complex than base salary alone. Equity refreshers, performance bonuses, sign-on bonuses, and level calibration all compound over a four-year tenure. The difference between a well-negotiated and a poorly-negotiated L5 offer at a major FAANG company can exceed $200,000 over four years. This makes compensation research and negotiation preparation among the highest-ROI activities in your job search.
