# System Design: Zoom - Video Conferencing at Scale

> Zoom's architecture challenge isn't delivering video. It's orchestrating hundreds of media servers globally to minimize latency while handling network variability for every participant.

Video conferencing is one of the most demanding distributed systems problems because it combines hard real-time constraints with extreme heterogeneity. Every participant has a different network (fiber, LTE, spotty Wi-Fi), a different device (browser, desktop, mobile), and a different display layout (speaker view, gallery view, screen share). The system must deliver audio within 150ms end-to-end and video within 300ms, or the call feels broken. At the scale we target here, 30 million daily meeting participants concentrating into 3 million concurrent participants at peak across 100,000 simultaneous meetings, and 3.3 Tbps of ingress bandwidth, even small inefficiencies cascade into massive infrastructure costs.

The three hardest engineering problems in this design are: (1) adaptive bitrate selection, where each subscriber must receive the right quality layer for their current bandwidth without the SFU re-encoding any video, (2) global latency minimization through Edge PoPs that keep the first network hop under 10ms, and (3) seamless failover so that a media server crash migrates all its meetings to healthy servers without participants noticing more than a brief reconnection.

Section 3 presents the three-layer architecture (Edge PoPs, SFU media servers, signaling). Sections 4-5 detail the core service implementations and database schema. Section 6 explains the key design decisions (SFU vs MCU, simulcast, host migration). Section 7 covers interview follow-ups. Section 9 defines the API surface. The deep dives in Sections 10-12 go deep on adaptive bitrate control, the recording pipeline, and end-to-end encryption. Sections 13-14 cover scaling strategy and failure handling. Sections 15-17 provide the interview scoring rubric, common mistakes, and follow-up questions. The remaining sections cover quality monitoring, analytics, the TURN relay server, monitoring and alerting, and cost analysis.
