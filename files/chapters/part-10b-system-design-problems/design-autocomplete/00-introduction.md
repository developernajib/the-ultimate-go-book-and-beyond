# System Design: Autocomplete / Typeahead Search

> Autocomplete answers on every keystroke, billions of times a day. The entire round trip must finish in under 100ms including network time, leaving perhaps 20ms for actual computation.

Autocomplete is one of the most latency-sensitive systems in all of software engineering. Every keystroke triggers a prefix lookup that must return ranked suggestions in under 20 milliseconds of server-side processing, fast enough that results appear before the user types the next character. The design point here is 100K suggestion queries per second against a dataset of 50 million unique search terms (a large search engine, though still an order of magnitude below Google), all while keeping the suggestion set fresh enough to surface trending topics within minutes of a breaking news event.

Three engineering challenges define this problem. First, the data structure choice: a trie with precomputed top-K results at each node turns prefix lookup into an O(prefix_length) operation instead of the O(N) full traversal that a naive approach would require. Second, the freshness pipeline: raw search events flow through Kafka and Flink at millions per minute, and the system must aggregate them into frequency counts, detect trending spikes, and rebuild the trie, all without interrupting live traffic. Third, multi-layer caching: single-character prefixes like "g" absorb an outsized share of the 100K QPS and belong in memory or on the CDN edge, while deep prefixes like "golang tutorial" are rare enough to serve on-demand from Redis.

The design targets 100K QPS with a P99 latency under 50ms end-to-end. The in-memory trie holds approximately 15GB of precomputed suggestions, and a 20-server fleet serves all traffic statelessly. Monthly infrastructure cost is approximately $7,000 on AWS.
