# System Design: Airbnb - Vacation Rental Marketplace

> Search-and-book for millions of listings requires keeping inventory consistent across search, reservations, and payments. The hardest part is saying 'no' reliably.

Airbnb is a two-sided marketplace that must serve two radically different workloads within a single platform. Search is read-heavy and latency-sensitive: 750 million daily searches across 7 million listings demand sub-200ms response times, which rules out querying PostgreSQL directly and requires an Elasticsearch-backed search layer with Redis caching. Booking is write-heavy and consistency-critical: even at a modest 11.5 bookings per second, a double-booking (two guests confirmed for the same dates) is a catastrophic failure that erodes trust in the entire platform.

The platform handles 150 million monthly active users, 7 million active listings, and 1 million bookings per day. The read-to-write ratio for search exceeds 750:1, which makes caching and read replicas highly effective for the search path. Booking generates approximately $140 million in daily gross booking value, with the platform taking a 14% guest service fee and a 3% host service fee.

The three hardest engineering problems are double-booking prevention (requiring a Redis-based availability hold with TTL during checkout, backed by a PostgreSQL transaction with row-level locking as the second line of defense), search ranking (a multi-signal scoring function combining relevance, listing quality via Bayesian-averaged ratings, conversion rate, and guest-specific personalization), and the financial coordination loop (authorization at checkout, capture at confirmation, delayed payout to hosts 24 hours after check-in with a dispute window, and reconciliation for stuck payments).
