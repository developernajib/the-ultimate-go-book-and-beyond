# System Design: Food Delivery - DoorDash/Uber Eats

> Food delivery coordinates three parties, customer, restaurant, and driver, each with their own state machine. Getting the timing right between order prep and driver arrival is the core engineering challenge.

Food delivery is a three-sided marketplace problem where the system must coordinate customers, restaurants, and drivers in real time, with each party operating on a different timescale and with different failure modes. The customer expects an accurate ETA and live tracking. The restaurant needs to know when to start cooking so food is ready when the driver arrives. The driver needs optimal routing and fair order distribution. Getting any of these coordination points wrong, a driver who arrives 10 minutes before food is ready, an ETA that is off by 15 minutes, directly degrades the user experience and increases cancellation rates.

The system serves 25 million daily active users, processes 1 million orders per day (peaking at 50 orders per second during dinner rush), and tracks 200,000 concurrent driver locations with GPS updates every 4 seconds, producing 50,000 writes per second to the location service. Active orders generate roughly 250,000 concurrent WebSocket connections for real-time tracking at the dinner peak (about 120,000 in-flight orders, each with a customer and a driver connected). Daily gross merchandise value is approximately $35 million.

The three hardest engineering problems are driver dispatch (matching the right driver to each order using a weighted scoring algorithm that considers proximity, rating, and acceptance rate), ETA prediction (combining ML-predicted restaurant prep time with real-time traffic routing, where the key insight is that driver travel and food prep happen in parallel so total ETA equals the maximum of the two, not their sum), and real-time state coordination (managing a 10-state order state machine across three parties with Kafka events broadcasting each transition to downstream services like tracking, notifications, and analytics).
