# System Design: Spotify - Music Streaming Platform

> Serving a catalog of 80 million tracks to over 600 million users is a CDN problem. Making the right song appear next is a machine learning problem. Making you discover music you did not know you would love is the product.

A music streaming platform operates at the intersection of content delivery, recommendation systems, and financial accounting. With 600 million users, 80 million songs, and 50 million concurrent streams at peak, the system must deliver audio segments with sub-second start times while continuously learning user preferences to power features like Discover Weekly. The CDN tier alone handles petabytes of monthly egress, but only 1% of tracks are "hot" enough to justify edge caching. The remaining 99% follow a long-tail distribution that requires a different delivery strategy. Meanwhile, every 30-second qualifying play generates a royalty event that must be tracked accurately across billions of events per day for financial reconciliation with labels and artists.

The three hardest engineering challenges are: (1) adaptive audio delivery using 10-second segments at multiple bitrates (96, 160, 320 kbps Ogg Vorbis, plus a lossless FLAC tier for Premium) so the client can switch quality mid-stream without audible artifacts, (2) cross-device playback state synchronization (Spotify Connect) where pausing on your phone instantly reflects on your desktop and smart speaker, and (3) recommendation at scale using collaborative filtering over 600 million user taste vectors and 80 million track embeddings, requiring approximate nearest neighbor search (FAISS/Annoy) rather than brute-force similarity.
