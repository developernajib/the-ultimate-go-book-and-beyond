## Follow-up Questions

These questions explore advanced topics that distinguish a strong candidate from an exceptional one. Each answer provides enough detail to demonstrate expertise without consuming excessive interview time.

**Q: How would you implement header bidding?** Traditional waterfall bidding calls demand sources sequentially (first DSP gets priority, second DSP only sees remnant inventory), which leaves money on the table because the first DSP may bid lower than the third. Header bidding runs all demand sources simultaneously: client-side JavaScript (Prebid.js) fires parallel requests to all configured SSPs and DSPs, collects bids, and passes the highest bid to the ad server. The server-side variant (Prebid Server) moves this logic to the backend, reducing client-side latency and page load impact. Server-side is faster (no browser overhead) but less transparent to publishers because they cannot see bid responses in the browser. Most major publishers use a hybrid approach.

**Q: Identity strategy in a post-cookie-uncertainty world?** The landscape is fragmented rather than uniformly cookieless: Safari and Firefox block third-party cookies by default, while Chrome reversed its long-announced deprecation plan in 2024 and kept them. A serious ad system therefore cannot bet on a single identity signal. Three strategies must be supported simultaneously: (1) contextual targeting, which targets based on the content of the page (keywords, categories) rather than user identity and is privacy-safe by design, (2) first-party identity solutions like UID 2.0, which use hashed email addresses to create cross-site identifiers with user consent, and (3) browser-provided privacy-preserving APIs such as Google's Privacy Sandbox, whose adoption remains uncertain enough that it should be an adapter in your targeting service, not a foundation. The interview signal here is recognizing that identity availability now varies per browser, per region (GDPR consent state), and per publisher, so the targeting pipeline needs graceful degradation from user-based to cohort-based to contextual.

**Q: Floor price optimization?** The publisher's floor price (minimum bid) directly affects revenue. Set it too high and no DSP bids, leaving the impression unfilled. Set it too low and you leave money on the table. An ML model trained on historical bid data optimizes the floor per combination of publisher, ad slot, time of day, and day of week. The model predicts the distribution of bids and sets the floor at the point that maximizes expected revenue (bid_price * fill_rate). Changes are A/B tested on 10% of traffic with a 7-day evaluation window to account for weekly cycles.

**Q: Brand safety?** Advertisers require guarantees that their ads will not appear next to harmful content. The system provides this through multiple layers: pre-scanning all ad creatives for policy violations before they enter the system, publisher category blocking (advertisers can exclude specific IAB content categories), and supply chain transparency via ads.txt (publishers declare authorized sellers) and sellers.json (sellers declare their identity and relationship to each publisher). Real-time page classification using NLP categorizes page content and matches it against advertiser exclusion lists before the auction runs.

**Q: Multi-touch attribution?** A user may see an ad, click it, see a retargeting ad a week later, and then convert. Which ad gets credit for the conversion? Last-click attribution (the default) gives all credit to the final touchpoint, which unfairly penalizes awareness campaigns. Multi-touch models distribute credit across all touchpoints: linear (equal credit), time-decay (more credit to recent touchpoints), or Shapley value (game-theoretic fair allocation based on marginal contribution). Conversion paths are stored as ordered sequences of touchpoints, and attribution runs as a nightly batch job that recalculates per-campaign conversion metrics. The choice of model significantly affects how advertisers evaluate campaign performance.
