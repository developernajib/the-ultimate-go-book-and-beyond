## Key Design Decisions

Each design decision below has a direct impact on system correctness, revenue, or advertiser trust. In an interview, you should be prepared to explain not just what was chosen, but why the alternatives were rejected and what failure modes the chosen approach prevents.

**Second-Price Auction:** Vickrey auction (pay second-highest bid + $0.01) incentivizes true-value bidding from DSPs. In a first-price auction, bidders are incentivized to shade their bids below their true valuation to avoid overpaying, which leads to a complex game-theoretic arms race. In a second-price auction, bidding your true value is the dominant strategy because you will never pay more than the second-highest bid regardless. This simplifies optimization for advertisers and produces more predictable revenue. The trade-off is that revenue per impression is slightly lower than a first-price auction (you always charge the second-highest price), but the transparency and simplicity increase long-term DSP participation. Note that Google Ad Exchange switched to first-price auctions in 2019, so be ready to discuss both models: both run in production across the industry.

**Budget Pacing:** Without pacing, campaigns with large budgets would win all morning impressions and exhaust budget by noon, leaving the afternoon with no ads to show. Pacing uses time-proportional spend targets: at 25% of the day elapsed, the target is to have spent 25% of the daily budget. A bid modifier slows spending when the campaign is ahead of pace (modifier < 1.0 reduces bid prices) and speeds up when behind pace (modifier > 1.0). The implementation uses three states: on-pace (modifier = 1.0), over-spending (modifier = 0.5 to slow down), and under-spending (modifier = 1.5 to catch up). This approach is more sophisticated than a simple rate limiter because it adapts to changing traffic patterns throughout the day.

**Impression Validation:** Impression ID stored in Redis with TTL. Click handler validates that the impression exists and that the ad ID matches before counting the click. After the first valid click, the impression key is deleted from Redis to prevent replay-based click fraud, where a malicious actor captures a valid click URL and replays it thousands of times. The TTL is set to 2 hours, which is generous enough to handle users who see an ad and click much later (for example, returning to a tab after a break), while not keeping stale data in Redis indefinitely.

**Fraud Detection:** Three-level detection provides defense in depth. Layer 1 (real-time, under 1ms): deterministic rule checks including velocity limits (more than N clicks from the same IP in one hour), known bot user-agent signatures, and datacenter IP blocklists. Layer 2 (near-real-time, under 5ms): velocity-based anomaly detection using Redis sorted sets to track click patterns per IP, user, and ad. Layer 3 (offline): an ML model trained on click stream data processes events from Kafka via Flink, identifying sophisticated fraud patterns like coordinated click farms. Retroactive fraud detection uses partner feeds (like DoubleVerify and IAS) to flag additional fraud, triggering automated refunds to affected advertisers.
