# System Design: Instagram / TikTok - Photo & Video Sharing Platform

> Scale is not a feature. It's a constraint. Design for the constraint first.

Instagram and TikTok represent the extreme read-heavy end of social media design. With 500 million daily active users generating 100 million uploads per day but consuming 10 billion feed views, the system operates at a 100:1 read-to-write ratio. This means that CDN placement, feed caching, and read-path optimization dominate every architectural decision. The numbers are concrete: 115,740 feed reads per second at average load (300,000 at peak), 100 terabytes of new photo data per day, and a petabyte of new video data per day requiring multi-quality transcoding.

The hardest engineering problems are: (1) feed generation, where the hybrid push-pull fan-out model must balance write amplification (1.16 million Redis writes per second for push) against read latency (pulling celebrity posts adds ~50ms per celebrity at read time), (2) media processing at scale, where FFmpeg transcoding of 50 million videos per day into 4 quality levels must be fault-tolerant with per-stage checkpointing, and (3) the algorithmic feed ranking that TikTok pioneered, which scores posts by combining recency decay with engagement signals to surface the most relevant content.

This chapter implements each layer in Go: presigned S3 uploads, an SQS-driven media processing pipeline, Cassandra-backed post storage with query-first table design, a hybrid feed generation service with Redis sorted sets, and a multi-layer caching strategy spanning in-memory application caches, Redis clusters, and CDN edge nodes. You will learn why Cassandra's partition-per-user model enables single-digit-millisecond timeline reads, how stories leverage TTL-based automatic expiration, and how to design notification batching that prevents overwhelming users during viral events.

The chapter begins with the problem statement and capacity estimation to establish the read-to-write ratios and storage volumes that shape every decision. The high-level architecture section lays out the major services, then core services provides Go implementations of the upload, feed, and media processing components. Database design and caching strategy follow as separate sections, reflecting their outsized importance in a read-heavy system. Key design decisions explains the major trade-offs, and the API design section defines the public interface. Three deep dives explore content moderation, notification batching, and view counting at scale. The chapter continues with scaling strategy, failure handling, an interview scoring rubric, and common mistakes, then closes with advanced follow-up questions, Go implementations of the follow service with social graph, explore/discovery feed, story service, comment service, hashtag service, like service with idempotency, and media CDN URL signing, along with monitoring and observability patterns, a Redis data structure summary, and extended capacity planning.
